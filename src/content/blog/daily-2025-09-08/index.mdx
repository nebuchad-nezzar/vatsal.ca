September 8, 2025

Japan’s leader stepped down, U.S.-China trade talks reportedly aren’t going all that well, France is ready to kick their PM to the curb later this morning, Russia is ramping up attacks on Ukraine and U.S. employment growth is sputtering (more large downward revisions expected tomorrow).

All that and stocks are doing pretty well today. Imagine how good things would be if there was more stability in the world…

The Asian session saw equity indices ex-Australia all in the green. The Nikkei led gains rallying 1.5% after Japanese PM Ishiba handed in his resignation, how’s that for a vote of confidence in your leadership.

A weaker Yen helped stocks (the currency has since recouped a good part of its losses) as did better than expected GDP data out of Japan. The Hang Seng and stocks in mainland China finished higher despite the sour trade talk reports and China’s trade data showing a month over month decline in exports, particularly shipments to the U.S. which were down -33% YoY.

This week China is holding its National People’s Congress (NPC) Standing Committee meeting. Stimulus announcements usually follow so keep an eye out later this week for new measures to boost growth as trade with the U.S. continues to come under pressure.

France’s PM Bayrou was shown the door. At one point the market was really concerned about this but now, not so much. Yields are a bit lower today and equity markets in Europe are all up across the board today.

Of note, France’s rating is up for review by Fitch on September 12th so maybe things won’t be as calm later this week/early next. North American stock markets moved moderately higher during trading on Monday, regaining ground after ending last Friday’s session well off their worst levels but still mostly lower. With the upward move, the tech-heavy Nasdaq reached a new record closing high.

The strength partly reflected optimism about the outlook for interest rates following last Friday’s weaker-than-expected U.S. employment data. Following the release of a closely watched report showing employment increased by much less than expected in the month of August, the Federal Reserve is widely expected to lower rates later this month.

CME Group’s FedWatch Tool is currently indicating an 88.2 percent chance the Fed will lower rates by a quarter point and a slim 11.8 percent chance of a half-point rate cut. Trading activity was somewhat subdued, however, as traders looked ahead to the release of inflation data that could impact the outlook for rates. The Labor Department is scheduled to release reports on producer price inflation and consumer price inflation on Wednesday and Thursday, respectively.

Economists currently expect the annual rate of producer price growth in August to come in unchanged from July at 3.3 percent. The annual rate of growth by consumer prices is expected to accelerate to 2. 9 percent in August from 2.7 percent in July, while the annual rate of growth by core consumer prices, which exclude food and energy prices, is expected to hold at 3.1 percent. Software stocks turned in some of the market’s best performances on the day, driving the Dow Jones U.S. Software Index up by 1.2 percent. An increase in the price of gold also contributed to strength among gold stocks, as reflected by the 1.2 percent gain posted by the NYSE Arca Gold Bugs Index.

In the bond market, treasuries extended the strong upward move seen over the past few sessions. As a result, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, fell 4.0 basis points to a five-month closing low of 4.046 percent. A lack of major U.S. economic data may keep some traders on the sidelines on Tuesday ahead of the release of the inflation data later in the week.
