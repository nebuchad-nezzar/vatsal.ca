U.S. equities finished mixed on Monday, with technology stocks acting as the primary drag on the broader market. The S&P 500 declined 0.3% to 7,652.86, the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.8% to 25,980.19, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average managed a modest gain of 0.3% to 53,417.16. The session left the S&P 500 further below its recent record high as investors positioned ahead of Nvidia’s earnings on Wednesday and Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh’s remarks at the Jackson Hole Symposium on Friday.

Within the technology sector, performance was distinctly weak. The Information Technology sector led the downside, and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index declined more than 2%. Nvidia, Micron Technology, and Broadcom were among the notable decliners as caution built ahead of key AI-related earnings reports later in the week. High-beta semiconductor and AI infrastructure names faced particular pressure, reflecting ongoing rotation and profit-taking after the strong multi-month advance in the group. Select software and platform names showed more resilience, but the overall tone in tech remained defensive.

Bond yields eased modestly on the day as oil prices pulled back from recent highs, offering limited relief after last week’s backup. Longer-term yields, however, remain elevated by recent standards. Oil prices retreated, with Brent easing toward the low $93 area, though the move appeared driven more by profit-taking than any meaningful improvement in the geopolitical backdrop. The unresolved standoff over the Strait of Hormuz continues to keep a risk premium embedded in energy markets. Shipping volumes through the strait remain far below pre-conflict levels, and both the United States and Iran remain entrenched. Markets are closely watching for details of potential new U.S. economic measures against Iran, which could further influence energy prices and inflation expectations.

Major institutional developments also shaped the day’s narrative. U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent is advancing plans for intensified economic pressure on Iran, describing the approach as an “economic D-Day” and signaling potential sanctions targeting Tehran’s trading partners. Separately, reports indicated that the Treasury may draw on its General Account to help fund expanded bond buybacks, following last week’s surprise increase in the size of those operations. Attention is also building toward Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh’s keynote address at Jackson Hole on Friday, which will be scrutinized for any signals on the policy path amid elevated yields and persistent inflation concerns.

Attention within the technology complex remains firmly fixed on Nvidia’s upcoming results, which will provide an important update on the durability of AI infrastructure demand. The semiconductor sector has entered a more selective and rotational phase after a powerful run driven by hyperscaler spending and custom silicon demand. While the structural AI cycle remains intact, elevated valuations and heavy positioning have made the group more sensitive to any signs of slowing momentum or shifts in risk appetite.

The longer-term outlook for technology and AI-related equities as constructive, supported by resilient demand for advanced semiconductors, custom silicon, and data-center infrastructure. At the same time, concentration risk within mega-cap technology and high-valuation semiconductor names remains elevated. Our preference remains for selective exposure to quality companies with genuine pricing power, strong free-cash-flow generation, and clear visibility into AI-related revenue growth, while maintaining discipline around valuation and avoiding overly aggressive directional positioning.

Nvidia’s earnings on Wednesday will be a litmus test for the semiconductor sector. Next week we will monitor residual technology results, developments around the Strait of Hormuz, the path of bond yields and energy prices, and incoming inflation data, and will keep you updated as conditions evolve.
