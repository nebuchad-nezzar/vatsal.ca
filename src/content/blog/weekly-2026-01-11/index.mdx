January 11, 2026

Global stock markets continued climbing this week amid the ongoing broadening seen thus far in 2026. Banks remained particularly strong ahead of earnings season as analysts predict several more Fed rate cuts later this year. Out of the gate, oil stocks popped as investors scrambled to assess what the ousting of Venezuelan President Maduro could mean for production from that region going forward.

The gains for both crude and oil stocks was mostly short lived. By midweek the Trump Administration had provided details on how they intend to extract sales of sanctioned Venezuelan oil. Lots of questions lingered ahead of a key meeting with industry executives on Friday, but the plans could offer Trump additional leverage when dealing with China and Russia, as well as Canada.

President Trump continued to insist the US has control of Venezuela, while his Administration negotiated with defacto leadership. Crude prices moved up late in the week with some pointing to the deteriorating political situation in Iran and the ongoing quagmire in Ukraine. Stocks were higher on the week, with the S&P gaining 1.6%, the DJIA up 2.3%, and the Nasdaq adding 1.9%.

European bourses also participated in the stock market with gains between 2% and 3%. Asia Pacific stock markets were once again bifurgated with mainland China and Japan registering +3% gains, while Hong Kong was modestly lower but India slumped 2.6%. Global economic readings were viewed as mostly solid and generally constructive for growth.

December U.S. ISM data fell to the lowest reading since October 2024, while prices remained elevated, but that was overshadowed by surging productivity and strong ISM services.

Preliminary Q3 productivity jumped at the fastest rate in 2-years aided by declining labor costs. Weekly jobless claims rose but remained well behaved. The October trade balance narrowed more than expected, allowing economists to take up Q4 growth expectations as exemplified by the Atlanta Fed Q4 GDP forecast, which climbed above 5%.

December U.S. payrolls missed expectations while previous jobs growth was revised lower. That was mostly offset by a larger than expected decline in the unemployment rate.

Futures still priced in a Fed that is on hold through at least the next two FOMC meetings following the Labor Department release. Outside the U.S., German CPI data was notably cooler than expected, supporting EU fixed income prices.

On the other hand, demand was tepid for two JGB auctions sending Japanese yields higher and fanning concerns surrounding Japan’s fiscal trajectory. Corporate news this week was highlighted by President Trump’s new housing affordability initiatives, which sent names in the sector for a loop. Stocks of large investors in residential real estate took it on the chin as Trump announced a ban on large institutional investors from buying more single-family homes.

The housing market also had to digest Trump’s next idea the following day, as the President ordered GSEs Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac to make efforts to purchase $200B of mortgage bonds. News was mixed for the AI storyline this week: Reports that the China government has approved some Nvidia H200 chip purchases was not enough to overcome the creeping doubts about the AI industry. A report circulated that OpenAI’s ChatGPT experienced its second month in a row of declining traffic amid cutthroat competition.

Meta also seeded its future energy needs with a series of power supply contracts, including with small nuclear reactor start-up Oklo, but much of that new energy is not slated to go online until next decade. Elsewhere, General Motors and, to a lesser degree Stellantis, announced cutbacks in their struggling EV businesses, with GM posting a $6B charge (following an even larger EV restructuring by Ford last month). Costco demonstrated the big box stores are still drawing consumer dollars, reporting December same-store sales up 6.2%, led by their U.S. stores. Global investors have a busy week ahead that will see the start of the earnings season and a host of economic data. Major U.S. banks take the spotlight as they gear up for their quarterly results. Investors will hear from the biggest lender, JPMorgan, on Tuesday, followed by number-two lender Bank of America on Wednesday, along with Wells Fargo and Citi, and Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley on Thursday. Looking at the economic calendar, traders will receive the U.S. December consumer price index report on Tuesday, followed by the delayed November producer price index report on Wednesday. The U.S. retail sales report for November is also set for Wednesday. Finally, market participants will hear from some Federal Reserve speakers this week, including Governor Stephen Miran, Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle Bowman, and Vice Chair Philip Jefferson. The Fed’s Beige Book will be published as well.

Elsewhere, markets expect a slew of Chinese data. This includes trade reports on Tuesday (which will put hard numbers on the state of a fragmented global trade regime) followed by retail sales on Wednesday and GDP data on Thursday. Strong Chinese data tends to have a boosting effect on Antipodean currencies (AUD and NZD).

Following last month’s improved inflation data, traders will look to confirm a smoother rate cut cycle for the Bank of England in 2026, provided UK employment figures don’t show unexpected strength. The release is scheduled for the Monday-Tuesday overnight session at 2:00 A. M. On the data front, attention will turn to inflation, with the French and German CPI releases scheduled for Thursday and Friday, respectively.
