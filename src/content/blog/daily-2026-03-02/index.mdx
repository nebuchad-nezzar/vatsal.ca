March 2, 2026

Overnight, markets in Asia were mixed. The Hang Seng led the way lower declining by more than 2%, the Nikkei was down 1.3% but somehow stocks managed to finish the session flat in Australia while mainland China actually had a positive close (South Korea was out for a holiday). Outside of Iran headlines, news flow was light in Asia.

BoJ member Himino sounded a tiny bit more dovish noting that the central bank should gradually shift to a more neutral stance through moderate policy rate hikes (no rush to hike it seems). We should see a few more headlines out of China later this week as this years’ National People’s Congress session kicks off on March 5. China’s 15th Five Year Plan (2026–2030) is being formulated with a central focus on developing “New Quality Productive Forces” and if you guessed that means in large part AI, you guessed right.

Look for the plan to prioritize advanced manufacturing, artificial intelligence, quantum technology, biotechnology, and commercial aerospace. AI will be highly integrated with various sectors to boost efficiency and create new production models. As always, watch for a 5% GDP target.

Risk sentiment dropped further as Europe opened. Iran is grabbing today’s headlines but we still have an ongoing war in Ukraine with reports that Russia is considering a halt to peace talks unless Ukraine agrees to cede territory. As if Russia-Ukraine concerns weren’t bad enough, the action in Iran this weekend has driven energy prices higher and we know Europe remains particularly vulnerable to rising energy prices.

On a more positive note, PMI data out of the Eurozone this morning was encouraging with manufacturing returning to expansionary territory among the big three EU economies. Italy saw its first expansion in three months. France experienced its third consecutive month of expansion, while Germany’s manufacturing PMI entered expansionary territory for the first time in 44 months.

North American stock markets moved sharply lower at the start of trading on Monday in reaction to the Middle East conflict but showed a substantial recovery over the course of the session. The major averages climbed well off their session lows before eventually ending the day narrowly mixed. The day’s turnaround came as traders used the initial sell-off as an opportunity to buy stocks at reduced levels; the Dow rebounded after hitting its lowest intraday level in two months.

Earlier, selling pressure was generated in reaction to the news that the U. S. and Israel launched joint strikes against Iran over the weekend, killing Iranian Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei. Iran retaliated by launching waves of drones and missiles at countries across the Middle East, including Kuwait, the United Arab Emirates, Bahrain, Saudi Arabia, Oman, and Qatar.

The conflict in the region escalated further today after Israel launched airstrikes on Hezbollah targets in Beirut and other parts of Lebanon following projectile fire from Lebanese territory into northern Israel. In remarks at the White House, President Donald Trump said the conflict with Iran is expected to last four to five weeks but said the U. S. has the “capability to go far longer than that.” The attacks led to a spike in the price of crude oil, adding to recent concerns about the inflation outlook.

In U. S. economic news, the Institute for Supply Management released a report showing a slight slowdown in the pace of growth in U. S. manufacturing activity in the month of February.

The ISM said its manufacturing PMI edged down to 52.4 in February after surging to 52.6 in January, but a reading above 50 still indicates growth. Economists had expected the index to dip to 51.8. Networking stocks moved sharply higher during the session, driving the NYSE Arca Networking Index up by 3.7 percent to a record closing high.

The sharp increase in crude oil prices also substantially strengthened oil producer stocks, with the NYSE Arca Oil Index surging by 3.4 percent. In the bond market, treasuries pulled back sharply after moving notably higher over the two previous sessions. Subsequently, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, surged 8.6 basis points to 4.048 percent.

Amid a quiet day on the U. S. economic front, trading on Tuesday may be impacted by reaction to the latest developments in the Middle East.
