Markets returned from the long weekend in a cautious mood. Selling concentrated in traditional blue-chip names while technology once again showed relative resilience. The S&P 500 finished down 0.6% at 7,673.52, the Dow took the sharper hit with a 1.2% decline to 52,786.07, and the Nasdaq Composite eased a more modest 0.3% to 26,421.41.

The split was familiar: capital continues to favor growth and AI-linked exposure even when broader risk appetite softens. Oil set the tone for the day. Brent crude briefly touched near $99.50 a barrel—its highest level in weeks—after Iran-backed Houthi forces struck Saudi energy facilities and Tehran issued fresh threats.

Shipping through the Strait of Hormuz remains far below pre-conflict levels, and these latest incidents kept the geopolitical risk premium firmly in place. Higher energy costs feed straight into inflation worries at a delicate moment. Last week’s strong jobs report had already lifted the odds of a rate hike; this week’s CPI and PPI numbers will be the last major data points the Federal Reserve sees before its mid-September meeting.

Within technology, the picture was more constructive and selective. Semiconductors and memory names stood out. Intel rose several percentage points on reports it could raise CPU prices and progress on High-NA EUV technology.

Qualcomm advanced after announcing a partnership with Amazon on next-generation AI data-center infrastructure. Micron, AMD, and Nvidia also traded higher, supported by ongoing optimism around AI demand. Memory-related names such as Sandisk, Seagate, and Western Digital extended recent strength.

The PHLX Semiconductor Index finished higher even as the broader market declined. In contrast, software names including Salesforce, ServiceNow, and Intuit faced pressure as renewed concerns about AI disruption—sparked by OpenAI’s latest model—weighed on the group. Tesla gained roughly 4%, while Apple was softer ahead of its product event under new CEO John Ternus.

Sector performance overall reflected these cross-currents. Energy and utilities finished higher on the oil move. Health care lagged, weighed down by Amgen’s steep drop of roughly 9–10% after a key Phase 3 cardiovascular trial failed to meet its primary endpoints.

Financials also underperformed. Beyond equities, a few other developments stood out. The Japanese yen continued to firm, approaching multi-month highs against the dollar as markets price a higher chance of a Bank of Japan rate increase this month.

Canada’s retaliatory tariffs on certain U. S. goods formally took effect, adding another layer of trade friction. Copper prices reached a fresh record amid speculation about potential U.

S. import measures. Taken together, the day captured the market’s current tension well: durable demand for advanced semiconductors, memory, and data-center infrastructure on one side, and a macro backdrop complicated by elevated energy costs, sticky inflation risks, and an approaching policy decision on the other. Valuation sensitivity remains high, and the coming inflation prints will carry real weight in shaping near-term positioning.

The longer-term case for selective technology and AI-related exposure continues to rest on resilient fundamental demand. At the same time, concentration risk, elevated multiples, geopolitical uncertainty around energy supply, and the possibility of further monetary tightening still call for discipline and a focus on companies with clear pricing power and strong free-cash-flow generation. Attention now turns to this week’s inflation data, residual earnings, and any further developments in the Middle East and the path of yields.
