January 13, 2025

Overnight, Asian equity markets traded mostly higher led by a more than 3% jump in the Nikkei. Japanese equities went on a tear after reports that Japan’s ruling Liberal Democratic Party plans to dissolve the country’s Lower House later this month and opt for a snap election likely in early February. Japanese PM Takaichi is looking to capitalize on her personal popularity (riding high in the polls with a 75% approval rating) before any honeymoon period ends and shore up support in the lower house.

That leads the market to anticipate more stimulus and less action from the BoJ (Japan definitely has less central bank independence). This morning that means higher yields, higher stocks and a weaker currency for Japan. Elsewhere, the Kospi enjoyed healthy gains helped by a weaker Korean Won while reportedly the U.

S. and Taiwan are near a trade deal which would include a TSMC pledge to build more manufacturing in the U. S. in return for lowering tariffs on Taiwanese imports to 15%. I am sure the U.

S. is looking to get more and more semi-conductor manufacturing out of Taiwan given reports that China’s goal is to prepare for military unification of Taiwan by 2027. European bourses were lower today although losses have been fairly limited. Geopolitics remains front and center as the war still rages on in Ukraine, Greenland remains in focus as of course does Iran.

Reports note that President Trump is being steered by senior administration officials towards a diplomatic solution for Iran rather than strikes. Let’s hope those same senior officials can talk some sense into POTUS about Greenland and maybe the threats against using military force on a NATO ally will stop. Last note out of Europe: it is hard to believe but the French budget debate is still dragging on.

For now, there seems to be little prospect of a solution and apparently French PM Lecornu is thinking about pushing the budget through using Article 49.3 of the Constitution. Under the procedure, the bill is deemed adopted unless a vote of no confidence is tabled within 24 hours and wins an absolute majority, in which case the government falls. After recovering from initial weakness to end Monday’s session modestly higher, North American stock markets showed a lack of direction over the course of the trading day on Tuesday.

The major averages spent the day bouncing back and forth across the unchanged line before eventually ending the day in negative territory. The Dow pulled back off the record closing high set on Monday amid a slump by shares of JPMorgan Chase, with the financial giant tumbling by 4.2 percent. JPMorgan came under pressure after reporting fourth quarter profits that decreased year-over-year, although its adjusted earnings exceeded analyst estimates.

The choppy trading by the broader markets may reflect uncertainty about the near-term outlook amid rising geopolitical tensions around the world and a flurry of proposals by President Donald Trump. Trump has recently called for a one-year cap on credit card rates at 10 percent and has also said defense companies should not be permitted to issue dividends or stock buybacks and that large institutional investors should be banned from buying single-family homes. In U.

S. economic news, the Labor Department released a report showing consumer prices in the U. S. increased in line with economist estimates in the month of December. The Labor Department said its consumer price index climbed by 0.3 percent in December, matching economist expectations.

Excluding food and energy prices, core consumer prices rose by 0.2 percent in December. Economists had expected core prices to rise by 0.3 percent. The report also said the annual rate of growth by consumer prices came in at 2.7 percent in December, unchanged from November and in line with estimates.

The annual rate of growth by core consumer prices was also unchanged from the previous month at 2.6 percent, while economists had expected an uptick to 2.7 percent. In the bond market, treasuries moved back to the upside following the consumer price inflation data. Subsequently, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, dipped 1.6 basis points to 4.171 percent.
