Weekly Market CommentaryJuly 27th, 2025The S&P and NASDAQ continued probing fresh all-time highs this week amid growing optimism around trade and generally reassuring earnings commentary. Japan’s election outcome was definitely not great for the ruling LDP party, but perhaps not as dire as some had forecast. In the wake of the vote, the U.

S. and Japan appeared to reach a long awaited trade agreement. The White House touted the hard fought deal that included a reduction in reciprocal and automotive tariffs from President Trump’s threatened 25% to 15% in response to Japan’s willingness to open up its markets to U. S. products and, perhaps more importantly, invest $550B into the U.

S. in a new kind of partnership that will be at Trump’s discretion. Details about the investment plans remained mostly sparse and reports emanating out of Japan surfaced contradicting what was said by some White House officials. Nevertheless, the 15% tariff rate was quickly viewed as what could be a baseline for deals with the EU, South Korea, and others as the August trade deadlines approached.

July Richmond Fed manufacturing data showed significant deceleration including the volume of new orders. Separately, S&P PMI data was once again led by services, while manufacturing disappointed. That data series also saw pricing pressures intensify in July, suggesting those effects could feed through to households later this year.

Existing and new homes sales illustrated the ongoing headwinds facing the U. S. housing market. Initial jobless claims fell for the 6th straight week heading into next week’s employment report and FOMC meeting.

The President continued to call on the Fed to cut rates, concluding with an awkward visit to see the highly scrutinized Fed DC building renovations. The ECB met and held rates steady as expected, and Lagarde intimated they could be on hold for some time. The U.

S. 10-year yield was little changed this week while the U. S. dollar gave back some ground. For the week, the S&P gained 1.5%, the DJIA rose 1.3%, and the NASDAQ added 1.0%.

European bourses were flat on the week. Asia Pacific stock markets were generally positive to varying degrees; Hong Kong was up 2.3% and Japan surged 4.1%. Your BSD Global Tech Hedge Fund was flat on the week.

Big tech names posted some rocky earnings reports, while firms in other sectors reported ongoing difficulties with the macro trade situation. Intel’s new CEO has yet to right the ship, reporting another EPS miss while announcing he will scrap foundry projects in Germany and Poland. Alphabet beat expectations, and meaningfully raised its capex outlook, but some analysts worried over weak free cash flow, a theme that is developing across a number of companies reporting.

Another firm reporting weak FCF was Tesla, which withdrew its production guidance due to tariffs and the imminent end of U. S. tax credits, as Elon Musk predicted the EV maker could have a ‘few rough quarters’ ahead. General Motors beat analyst estimates, but forecast tariffs would weigh heavily on Q3 results.

Chemical giant Dow Inc. saw shares slump to a 52-week low as it missed the mark on Q2 and was compelled to halve its dividend, citing a challenging global macroeconomic backdrop and anti-competitive behavior from newer market entrants. Puma cut its FY25 guidance sharply citing U. S. tariffs and a major driving factor.

M&A chatter continued to swirl in the rail sector, with reports emerging that Berkshire’s BNSF is considering a bid for a rival railway, even as Union Pacific and Norfolk Southern confirmed discussions about a possible merger. Elsewhere, Paramount finally got FCC approval for its deal to be acquired by Ellison’s Skydance, moving another legacy media firm into the sphere of influence of a tech billionaire. There were plenty of discussions over the weekend between President Trump and EU Head von der Leyen and they resulted in yet another trade deal.

The U. S. and the EU have come to an agreement less than a week before the August 1st deadline despite both sides saying the odds were 50-50 at best just late last week. I have to admit, the deal seems a bit one sided with a 15% tariff on most EU exports (with aluminum and steel staying at 50% for now) while the U.

S. faces no tariffs from Europe. The EU has also agreed to buy $750bio worth of U. S. energy products and will increase its U.

S. investments by $600B. I am sure there are plenty of details to be ironed out, especially with respect to the “investment” component. It seems quite similar to the deal struck by the U.

S. and Japan last week and Japanese officials definitely have a different interpretation of that investment component than their U. S. counterparts. Trump and Lutnick have claimed the U.

S. will get 90% of the profits from Japan’s investment, while Japanese officials have said profit share from any deals funded by Japan would be based on the degree of contribution and risk taken by each party. Japan’s top negotiator Akazawa also said to only expect 1-2% of the $550bio to be investment, with the remainder being loans which Japan will actually earn interest on. Why let details get in the way of a good social media post though!

The August 1 tariff deadline is no longer an overhang for stocks, as investors have seemingly moved on from bracing for tariff headlines to embracing positive economic data, including a continuing drop in jobless claims. That jubilant mood could be put to the test this week with a host of important macroeconomic events and data releases. The Federal Open Market Committee meets on Tuesday and Wednesday, culminating in Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell’s press conference.

Of particular interest will be how the divisions within the FOMC play out at the meeting, with Fed governors Christopher Waller and Michelle Bowman both advocating for an interest-rate cut despite traders having priced in a negligible chance of that happening at this meeting. Powell’s remarks at the post-decision press conference will be heavily scrutinized at a time when President Trump has piled on enormous criticism and pressure to cut interest rates. An inflation reading from the Bureau of Economic Analysis on Thursday and the jobs report from the Bureau of Labor Statistics on Friday will be the two biggest market movers of the week, giving more insight into the Fed’s dual mandate to facilitate stable prices and maximum employment.

This will also be the busiest week of the second-quarter earnings season, with roughly 150 S&P 500 companies reporting, including four of the Magnificent Seven. One-thirs of the S&P 500 companies have reported so far, and things have gotten off to a strong start: more than 80% of companies beat earnings-per-share estimates, and about 80% beat revenue projections. Procter & Gamble and Visa will announce earnings on Tuesday, followed by Meta Platforms and Microsoft on Wednesday. Apple, Amazon and MasterCard will release their results on Thursday, while two oil giants, Chevron and Exxon Mobil will round out the week on Friday.
