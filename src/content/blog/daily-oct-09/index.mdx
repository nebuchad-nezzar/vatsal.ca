U.S. equities advanced on Friday, recovering most of Thursday's technology-led decline. The Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 0.51% to 51,655.01, the S&P 500 gained 0.59% to 7,811.51, and the Nasdaq added 0.64% to 30,088.86. The Russell 2000 increased 0.52% to 2,808.57. The S&P 500 closed approximately 0.1% below its October 6 record of 7,818.93. All three major indices finished the week higher, led by the Dow, with the Nasdaq recording the smallest gain. The VIX eased to roughly below 15, consistent with an orderly pullback rather than a change in market regime.

**AI Valuations: Timing, Not Demand, Drove the Swing**

Sentiment improved following reports that OpenAI expects to reach $70 billion in annualized revenue by the end of 2026, driven primarily by enterprise demand. This followed Thursday's Financial Times report that its current annualized revenue was approximately $50 billion, below the roughly $70 billion figure that had circulated among investors, which had pressured semiconductor shares by about 3.4%. The two reports are not contradictory: reaching $70 billion from $50 billion within roughly one quarter would require about 40% sequential growth, which is ambitious but within range of the 77% third-quarter run-rate growth the company has cited. The episode illustrates that valuations across the AI complex are sensitive to the timing and definition of reported revenue, not only to its direction. Neither session's move altered the underlying demand picture, and investors should interpret both with that in mind.

**Energy and Geopolitics: The Russia Diesel Agreement**

The most consequential headline of the day arrived late in the session. President Trump announced that he had reached an agreement with Russian President Vladimir Putin, following a call of roughly an hour and a half, under which Russia would supply more than 300,000 tons of diesel fuel immediately, a further 500,000 tons in November, and 1,000,000 tons thereafter, with the stated aim of lowering fuel prices for American and global consumers. The Kremlin confirmed Russia's readiness to supply oil and petroleum products to U.S. and world markets, and reports indicated the arrangement may involve easing sanctions on Russian diesel sales, though both governments provided limited detail.

The context is important. U.S. retail diesel reached a record of about $6.52 per gallon on September 22 and remained near $6.37 this week, Russia had banned diesel exports after Ukrainian strikes disabled a significant share of its refining capacity, and the Group of Seven agreed last week to release 100 million barrels of oil and fuel products. The announcement drew immediate criticism. Ukrainian President Zelenskyy characterized it as support for Russia's war effort, and critics noted that it sits uneasily with the sanctions law the president signed last month, which directs tariffs of up to 100% on the largest importers of Russian oil and gas. Because the announcement came near the close, we have not confirmed a market reaction and would expect energy and refined-product markets to respond on Monday.

We read this as part of a pattern. With midterm elections on November 3 and fuel costs weighing on consumer sentiment, the administration is treating diesel prices as a priority, as evidenced by last week's G7 release, this week's statement on Iran, and now the Russia arrangement. That raises the probability of further supply-side measures through early November, which is supportive for near-term energy-inflation expectations. It also introduces policy risk: the durability of any sanctions relief, the response in Congress, and the effect on Ukraine negotiations are all open questions.

Earlier in the session, oil prices had eased modestly following the president's statement that the United States will not attack Iran before the election and that discussions with Tehran are productive. The Strait of Hormuz blockade remains in effect, and additional sanctions on Iranian shadow-fleet vessels were announced Thursday. Energy costs continue to drive consumer inflation expectations, as this morning's survey shows.

**Consumer: A Widening Divide Beneath Record Equity Prices**

The University of Michigan's preliminary October sentiment index declined to 46.3 from 48.1, below the 47.6 consensus, and its measure of current conditions reached a record low of 44.7. One-year inflation expectations rose to 4.7% and the five-to-ten-year measure to 3.5%, both the highest since May. The survey indicates that weakness is concentrated among lower-income households and those with limited equity holdings, while households with larger stock portfolios show little change. This divergence suggests that consumption is increasingly supported by asset prices at the upper end of the income distribution, a dependency worth monitoring. Rising long-term inflation expectations also strengthen the case for the Federal Reserve to remain restrictive.

**Rates and the Fed: Front End Easing, Long End Elevated**

In fixed income, the front end of the Treasury curve has eased while the long end remains elevated. The two-year yield has declined from approximately 4.84% on Monday to about 4.75%, while the 10-year yield remains near 5.25% to 5.27%, after reaching roughly 5.36% intraday on Wednesday, its highest level since 2002. Demand at Wednesday's $39 billion 10-year auction was strong, with the highest bid-to-cover ratio since 2016, which helped stabilize the market. Fed Governor Waller indicated on Thursday that additional rate increases are likely warranted but need not occur at consecutive meetings. September CPI data and the Beige Book are due Wednesday and represent the next significant test for rate expectations.

Separately, the administration announced a committee to examine mortgage fraud allegations against Fed Governor Lisa Cook. The allegations are unproven, but developments bearing on the perceived independence of the Federal Reserve Board warrant monitoring given their potential effect on long-term yields.

**Earnings and Corporate Developments**

Earnings season began with several notable developments. Delta Air Lines reported third-quarter adjusted earnings of $1.72 per share against consensus of $1.89, with revenue of $17.59 billion, and lowered its outlook; shares declined about 2% and airline stocks broadly lagged, underscoring fuel-cost sensitivity, which makes the diesel developments directly relevant to the sector. In telecommunications, SpaceX's announced purchase of approximately $8 billion of low-band spectrum from Grain Management weighed on AT&T, T-Mobile and Verizon, while tower operators American Tower, Crown Castle and SBA Communications advanced, reflecting expectations of growing satellite-to-device competition. In managed care, the 2027 Medicare Advantage Star Ratings drove significant dispersion: Humana and Clover Health rose, while Alignment Healthcare declined after its flagship California plan was rated 3.5 stars. Star ratings influence bonus payments and therefore margins.

The equity markets showed notable resilience this week, but that resilience rested on specific supports: strong demand for Treasuries at higher yields, a favorable interpretation of AI revenue targets, and a willingness to look past soft consumer data. None of these is guaranteed to persist. We continue to view the earnings outlook for AI beneficiaries as constructive and favor companies with self-funded capital expenditure, diversified revenue and clear visibility. We are more cautious on businesses with significant fuel or financing exposure, although any sustained decline in diesel prices would ease that pressure, and we recommend maintaining disciplined portfolio duration while long-term yields remain elevated. Investors should not interpret this week's recovery as evidence that the underlying risks have been resolved.

**What We Are Watching Next Week**

The market reaction to the Russia diesel announcement on Monday, and any clarification of its terms and its relationship to the sanctions law, will be an early focus. Major banks begin reporting Tuesday, providing an early read on credit quality and consumer health at current interest rates. September CPI and the Beige Book follow on Wednesday, with an upside inflation surprise likely to raise expectations of a further rate increase this year. Further disclosure from OpenAI, and the anticipated public filing from Anthropic, will inform valuations across private AI developers and their infrastructure partners. Developments in the Strait of Hormuz and the sanctions regime remain the principal drivers of oil prices. A sustained move in the 10-year yield above 5.36% would present a meaningful challenge to equity valuations regardless of earnings results.
