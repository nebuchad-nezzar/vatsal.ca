The AI trade got the evidence it was waiting for, and the market rewarded it. The Nasdaq Composite rose 1.05% to 26,702.86, the S&P 500 gained 0.58% to 7,757.22 for its third straight advance, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average added 360.21 points, or 0.69%, to 52,856.09. The VIX eased 3.64% to 16.68. The S&P 500 is now about 0.8% below its October 6 record and the Nasdaq about 3.3% below its own (my arithmetic). Semiconductors rebounded from two-month lows after eight straight losing sessions, the longest streak in 18 years. A rebound this sharp from depressed levels is partly mechanical. Nomura’s Charlie McElligott noted that the 25 best-performing Russell 1000 stocks of the first half fell an average of 19% last week, a momentum unwind that makes today’s recovery in beaten-down names unsurprising.

### What Led: Evidence of Demand

SpaceX rose about 7.8% to roughly $159 after its first full public quarter showed AI revenue of $7.6 billion, up 830%, and Musk said the company is rapidly bringing Nvidia GPUs online. That lifts its value to roughly $2 trillion (my arithmetic). Nvidia gained 2.5% to $221.86, and Super Micro closed up about 9% at roughly $35.50 after raising its fiscal first-quarter revenue outlook to $18.5 billion to $19.5 billion from $14.5 billion to $15.5 billion. The context tempers the enthusiasm: Super Micro is still down about 22% over the past month, and Intel, up 1% to $92.61, is down about 26%. This rally rewards real demand data, but it is rebuilding from a deep hole, and Super Micro’s after-hours gain of 12% to 13% had already faded by the close.

### Guidance Rules: Netflix and AT&T

The reaction function I have described all week held again. Netflix reported a better-than-expected profit and fell roughly 4% to 6% to a 52-week low, because investors worried that its momentum is slowing. AT&T also beat on profit and dropped about 6% because its growth forecast for an underlying earnings measure fell short of some estimates. Alphabet slipped about 1.5%. In a market that expected nearly 30% third-quarter profit growth, trailing beats earn nothing, and soft guidance is punished immediately. If you are judging an earnings report, read the outlook first.

### After the Close: Meta and Tesla Show Two Ways to Fund AI

Meta reported GAAP earnings of $3.83 per share ($7.40 non-GAAP excluding a $9.89 billion legal charge) against about $3.57 GAAP / $5.74 non-GAAP expected, and revenue of $64.60 billion, up 26%, against $63.37 billion. GAAP operating income fell 37% to $12.99 billion, a 20% margin, after the legal settlement charge and heavier spending, and the shares rose about 3.8% after hours from a $697 close. Tesla reported adjusted earnings of $0.48 against $0.44 to $0.45 expected, adjusted EBITDA of $3.90 billion against $3.66 billion, and revenue of $28.05 billion, in line with consensus. Automotive revenue fell 3% to $20.56 billion and energy revenue fell 16%, while operating margin compressed to 2.6% from 5.8% as AI and Optimus spending rose. Free cash flow was positive at $1.53 billion after $4.73 billion of capital spending, and cash stood at about $45.4 billion.

The point I take from the pair is about funding. Both companies are spending heavily on AI, but both are paying for it from operating cash flow and balance sheets, unlike SpaceX, whose capital spending of about $19.6 billion exceeds its $8.8 billion of adjusted EBITDA. The market is gradually learning to price those three funding profiles differently. SpaceX, which shares a possible merger with Tesla under special board committees, also faces a supply test Thursday when a large lockup release begins, with up to 1.3 billion shares reported to become tradable.

### Oil: Prices Disagree With the Flows

Brent for December settled up 0.98% at $109.86 after trading between roughly $108 and $111, while WTI traded between about $97 and $100. Prices rose early on Iran saying it saw no basis for talks until its demands are met, on disputed claims about a mined tanker and a downed U.S. drone, and on Kpler’s count of at least 18 vessel attacks in two weeks, before paring gains. The EIA reported crude inventories down 3.06 million barrels to 412.5 million, a larger draw than the 0.82 million expected, with Gulf Coast crude stocks at their lowest since January 2025. The number that matters most for the diesel debate is that distillate stocks rose 1.68 million barrels to 104.3 million, against an expected decline of 1.16 million.

The supply side is also less bleak than the headlines. The IEA says UAE output hit a record in September and Saudi output rose by a third to above 8 million barrels a day, Iraq has nearly restored exports through steep discounts, and Secretary of State Rubio said Hormuz flows are close to pre-war levels, though that claim is unverified because ships are running dark. I read this as a market pricing security risk and political timing more than current physical shortage, which is consistent with the post-midterm escalation premium analysts flagged yesterday.

### Rates and Cross-Asset

The 10-year Treasury yield eased to about 5.09% to 5.10% during the session from 5.11% on Tuesday and finished near 5.13%, still about 115 basis points above its 3.97% level before the war with Iran began. The Associated Press captured the link to technology well: AI stocks have become shaky on worries that the data center buildout may slow because borrowing has become so much more expensive. Bitcoin rose about 2% to roughly $84,000 to $84,500, and gold futures edged up 0.26% to $4,153.40 (with spot near $4,135), still down on the week as energy-driven inflation keeps rate-hike bets alive.

Today supports a distinction I have drawn all week. AI demand evidence is strong and arriving on schedule, from TSMC, Super Micro and SpaceX. What remains unresolved is who funds the buildout at a 5.1% risk-free rate. Companies that pay for AI from operating cash flow, like Meta and Tesla, can absorb margin compression, while those financing growth with debt or future revenue cannot. I continue to favor the first group, to be selective in AI hardware where guidance is rising, and to be cautious on leveraged infrastructure and on any stock where a beat is the only good news. Energy remains a reasonable diversifier. I would turn more constructive if guidance across technology keeps rising and credit spreads on AI borrowers tighten, and more defensive if the 10-year moves back above 5.3% or Hormuz attacks escalate.
