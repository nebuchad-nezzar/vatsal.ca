February 11, 2026

Japan was out for a holiday so volumes were lighter than normal in Asia, but equity markets all enjoyed a positive session. Sentiment was given a lift after reports that South Korea chip exports for the first ten days of February jumped 137% YoY. The tech story lives on with demand reportedly fueled by AI and data centers.

Elsewhere, we saw both PPI and CPI data out of China last night and if you guessed weak, you guessed right. PPI came in at -1.4% YoY (negative for the 40th month in a row) while CPI was softer than the already low expectations at .2% YoY. Definitely room for lower rates in China.

On another note, the PBOC continues to fix the yuan stronger. European bourses were mixed today with politics and geopolitics remaining front and center. UK PM Starmer appears to have steadied the ship for now, Iran and the U.S. will continue nuclear talks but President Trump is moving a second carrier group to the Middle East “just in case” while reports say that Ukrainian President Zelensky is set to announce spring presidential elections and a national referendum on a potential peace deal with Russia later this month. Clearly the pressure is on Zelensky with the U.S. looking for a peace deal by June well in advance of the U.S. midterms. After failing to sustain an initial move to the upside, North American stock markets quickly gave back ground in early trading on Wednesday and showed a lack of direction over the remainder of the session. The major averages spent the day bouncing back and forth across the unchanged line before eventually closing modestly lower.

The initial strength came following the release of a closely watched Labor Department report showing employment in the U.S. increased by much more than expected in the month of January. The Labor Department said non-farm payroll employment jumped by 130,000 jobs in January after rising by a downwardly revised 48,000 jobs in December.

Economists had expected employment to climb by 70,000 jobs compared to the addition of 50,000 jobs originally reported for the previous month. The report also said the unemployment rate edged down to 4.3 percent in January from 4.4 percent in December, while economists had expected the unemployment rate to remain unchanged. However, the report also showed a significant downward revision to 2025 job growth, revising the employment increase to 181,000 jobs from 584,000 jobs.

The stronger-than-expected job growth in January may also have reduced the Federal Reserve’s likelihood of near-term interest rate cuts, offsetting the initial positive reaction. On Friday, the Labor Department is scheduled to release a separate report on consumer price inflation that may shed additional light on the outlook for rates. Despite the lackluster performance by the broader markets, energy stocks moved sharply higher along with the price of crude oil, with the Philadelphia Oil Service Index and the NYSE Arca Oil Index surging by 3.1 percent and 2.8 percent, respectively.

A notable increase by the price of gold also contributed to substantial strength among gold stocks, as reflected by the 2.6 percent jump by the NYSE Arca Gold Bugs Index. In the bond market, treasuries moved to the downside in reaction to the monthly jobs data. As a result, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, climbed 2.5 basis points to 4.172 percent.

Trading on Thursday may be impacted by reactions to the latest U.S. economic data, including reports on weekly jobless claims and existing home sales.
