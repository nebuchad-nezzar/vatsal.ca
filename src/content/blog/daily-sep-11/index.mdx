U. S. stocks rebounded on Friday, snapping a four-day losing streak as a pullback in oil prices and strong technology results outweighed sticky inflation data. The S&P 500 rose approximately 0.9% to around 7,657, the Dow Jones Industrial Average advanced nearly 1.0% to about 52,566, and the Nasdaq Composite gained roughly 0.9–1.0% to approximately 26,328.

Despite the firm finish, the major averages remained on track for weekly declines after the sharp sell-off earlier in the week. The August consumer price index provided the main data focus. Headline CPI rose 0.4% month-over-month and 3.4% year-over-year, while core CPI increased 0.3% and 2.4%, respectively.

The figures were broadly in line with expectations but did little to ease concerns about persistent inflation, particularly with energy prices still elevated. Markets continued to price a high probability—around 86% in some estimates—of a Federal Reserve rate increase at next week’s meeting. The 10-year Treasury yield eased slightly from recent multi-year highs but remained near 4.94%.

Oil offered some relief after its sharp weekly advance. Brent and WTI pulled back several percent from multi-month highs, helping support risk assets. The decline came even as geopolitical risks remained elevated.

Iran-aligned Houthi forces advanced further, seizing the Yemeni port of Mocha (Mokha) and reaching the strategic island of Perim (Mayun) near the Bab al-Mandeb Strait—adding pressure on a second critical shipping chokepoint alongside the still heavily restricted Strait of Hormuz. Transit volumes through Hormuz stayed well below normal, and the broader U. S.-Iran maritime confrontation continued.

Some reports of exploratory talks on temporary management of Hormuz shipping provided modest relief to energy prices, but the underlying risk premium remains firmly in place. Technology delivered some of the day’s most eye-catching moves. Oracle’s strong first-quarter results—total revenue up 30% to $19.3 billion, cloud infrastructure revenue surging 121% to $7.4 billion, and remaining performance obligations rising $209 billion year-over-year to $664 billion—continued to resonate across the AI infrastructure complex.

Dell surged more than 11%, reaching record territory, while Hewlett Packard Enterprise jumped roughly 9–11% and HP advanced around 8%. Arista Networks also gained more than 5% as management highlighted solid demand. The broader semiconductor and AI-linked group participated in the rebound, providing a clear positive read-through from Oracle’s capacity and spending commentary.

Sector performance was mostly positive, with technology and communication services among the leaders. The session reflected a classic risk-on reaction to lower oil and constructive corporate news, even as the inflation data kept the higher-for-longer rates narrative firmly in place. The week as a whole highlighted the market’s ongoing tension: durable demand for advanced semiconductors, cloud infrastructure, and AI capacity on one side, and a macro environment still complicated by elevated energy prices, sticky inflation, rising yields, and unresolved geopolitical risk on the other.

Valuation sensitivity remains elevated after the recent pullback. The longer-term case for selective technology and AI-related exposure continues to rest on resilient fundamental demand for chips, data-center capacity, and related infrastructure. At the same time, concentration risk, elevated multiples, geopolitical uncertainty around energy supply, and the possibility of further monetary tightening still call for discipline and a focus on companies with clear pricing power and strong free-cash-flow generation.
