November 2, 2025

Trading opened this past week with an influx of risk-on sentiment as investors braced for one of the busiest agendas of the year. U.S. stock markets once again hit new all-time highs while gold prices continued to roll over.

De-escalation on the U.S.-China trade front was one of the main drivers after negotiators reached another framework agreement. Coming out of his meeting with Xi in Malaysia, President Trump touted another landmark deal which did include the lowering of the overall China tariff rate below 50%, in return for a delay in China rare earth curbs alongside a revival of U.S. soybean purchases. In reality though, the news appeared to be more of a reset to where the U.S.-China relationship was coming out the Geneva detente this summer.

Separately, capital markets activity remained very robust, fueling bubbling animal spirits. Another string of M&A announcements early in the week gave way to a deluge of significant corporate bond and equity offering announcements largely tied to the financing of M&A and the build out of AI infrastructure. Wednesday’s FOMC announcement offered something for both the hawks and the doves, but the main takeaway could not be understated.

It revealed just how divided the committee currently is. There were two dissenters: Miran maintained a 50bps cut bias, while Schmid gave a hawkish dissent, calling on the committee to hold rates. At his presser Powell deliberately pushed back on market expectations, noting a December rate cut was far from a “done deal” amid a “growing chorus” of policy makers who favor a pause.

Several members of that chorus came out on Friday expressing their preference that rates stay on hold given we have now fallen to a range they view as near neutral. U.S.

Treasury yields finished the week above where they were heading into the Fed rate cut. For the week, the S&P gained 0.7%, and the Nasdaq added 2.2%. European bourses finished the week down around 1%.

Asia Pacific stock markets were quite bifurcated; Japan surging 6% while the Hong Kong Hang Seng lost 1%. Your BSD Global Tech Hedge Fund advanced 1.3% this past week. Spending on the AI buildout was a prominent theme again this week, amid a raft of corporate earnings reports.

Nvidia dominated the news in the early part of the week with product updates and dozens of partnership announcements out of its GTC conference in Washington DC. CEO Jensen Huang further juiced markets when he touted visibility into a $500B backlog through 2026, even with zero orders coming from China (which he said could amount to $50B if trade talks go well). Qualcomm managed to steal a little of Nvidia’s thunder as it unveiled two new competing AI chips for data centers to launch next year.

Microsoft shares got a boost after confirming a long expected recapitalization and resetting of its relationship with OpenAI, intended to allow both parties to develop AI technology less encumbered. One by one Microsoft, Google, Apple, Meta, and Amazon also beat expectations for earnings and/or guidance, but investors showed at least some hesitation to continue buying up the Mag-7 names as their spending projections reached ever more dizzying heights. While AI tech continues to dazzle markets, a lot of traditional business and industry are struggling.

International Paper reported a huge miss, with negative earnings, and cut revenue guidance substantially for the year, blaming soft consumer sentiment, housing weakness, and trade uncertainty. At Starbucks, more than a year into the tenure of CEO Brian Niccol, results are still not turning around and shareholders appear to be losing patience. On the M&A front, Olympic Steel agreed to be acquired by industrial metals processor Ryerson Holding in an all stock deal.

Meanwhile, Cadence Bancorporation was snapped up by Huntington Bancshares in a $7.4B deal pairing a southern regional bank with a Midwest powerhouse. The week ahead will not be as busy as the one just concluded, but there are still some high-impact data releases and geopolitical risk. This week will also mark a somewhat ignominious milestone as, absent any action, the ongoing U.S. government shutdown will officially become the longest on record on Thursday. Beyond the data, markets will keep a close eye on Russia-Ukraine as well as a potential move by the U.S. in Venezuela.

Traders focus this week will be on the earnings season, which will ramp up further with hundreds of companies on the docket; including Palantir, AMD, Shopify, Uber, Amgen, Pfizer, McDonald’s, Qualcomm, and Airbnb. Market participants will also be keeping an eye on trade developments and Federal Reserve speakers.
