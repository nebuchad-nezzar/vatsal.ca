February 13, 2026

Asian equity markets finished lower across the board last night led by a 1.7% drop in the Hang Seng. All major indices were down at least 1% with the exception of the Kospi where losses were a bit more limited. Not even reports that President Trump is increasingly likely to extend the trade truce with China could boost sentiment.

Apparently, the Trump administration has shelved a number of key security and technology issues ahead of Trump’s April meeting with President Xi, including use of Chinese equipment in US data centers. I suppose a bit of an appeasement given China’s dismay at the recent US-Taiwan trade deal and ongoing US arms sales to Taiwan. Maybe risk is getting pared back a bit ahead of China’s Lunar New Year holiday which kicks off on February 17th.

Asian trade will definitely be quieter next week. European bourses were mixed in Friday trade with the main headlines coming from the Munich Security Conference. After showing a lack of direction early in the session, North American stock markets saw some strength in afternoon trading on Friday before once again giving back ground again toward the end of the day.

The major averages eventually ended the day narrowly mixed. For the week, the Nasdaq tumbled by 2.1 percent, while the S&P 500 and the Dow slumped by 1.4 percent and 1.2 percent, respectively. The choppy trading came even after the Labor Department released its highly anticipated report on consumer price inflation in the month of January.

The report showed consumer prices rose by slightly less than expected on a monthly basis, while the annual growth rate slowed more than anticipated. The Labor Department said its consumer price index rose by 0.2 percent in January after climbing by 0.3 percent in December. Economists had expected prices to rise by another 0.3 percent.

The annual rate of growth by consumer prices slowed to 2.4 percent in January from 2.7 percent in December, coming in below estimates of 2.5 percent. Meanwhile, the Labor Department said core consumer prices, which exclude food and energy prices, increased by 0.3 percent in January after rising by 0.2 percent in December, matching expectations. The annual growth rate for core consumer prices dipped to 2.5 percent in January from 2.6 percent in December, which was also in line with estimates.

The tamer-than-expected headline inflation data led to some renewed optimism about the interest rate outlook and a continued slump by treasury yields. However, traders continued to express concerns about potential disruptions from the recent artificial intelligence buildout, keeping buying interest subdued. Despite lackluster performance in the broader markets, gold stocks moved sharply higher along with the price of the previous metal, resulting in a 5.6 percent spike by the NYSE Arca Gold Bugs Index.

Considerable strength was also visible among computer hardware stocks, as reflected by the 2.7 percent surge by the NYSE Arca Computer Hardware Index. Networking, utilities, natural gas and transportation stocks also turned in strong performances on the day, while steel stocks moved to the downside amid a report President Donald Trump plans to roll back tariffs on steel and aluminum. In the bond market, treasuries extended the strong upward move seen in the previous session.

Subsequently, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, slid 4.8 basis points to a two-month closing low of 4.056 percent. Following the Presidents’ Day holiday on Monday, the Commerce Department’s report on personal income and spending will likely be the focus next week, as it includes the Fed’s preferred inflation readings.
