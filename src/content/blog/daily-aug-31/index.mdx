August 31, 2026

U.S. equities declined on Monday as renewed military exchanges between the United States and Iran over the weekend pushed oil prices higher and lifted market expectations for further Federal Reserve rate hikes. The S&P 500 fell approximately 0.4–0.5%, the Dow Jones Industrial Average declined around 0.5–0.7%, and the Nasdaq Composite slipped roughly 0.3–0.4%. Despite the softer session, the major averages remained on track to finish August higher, with the S&P 500 and Nasdaq posting their first positive months since May.

Sector performance reflected the geopolitical and rate-sensitive backdrop. Energy shares provided relative support as crude prices rose, while interest-rate sensitive groups such as Utilities and Communication Services lagged. Technology was mixed after last week’s strong earnings-driven rally. Residual strength persisted in select semiconductor and software names following Nvidia’s blockbuster results and the solid prints from Salesforce, CrowdStrike, Okta, Marvell, and Workday, but broader risk appetite was tempered by the escalation in the Middle East and the higher-for-longer rates narrative.

The primary driver of the session was the weekend’s military activity. U.S. forces struck Iranian rocket launchers on Larak Island that were reportedly preparing to deploy sea mines in the Strait of Hormuz—the first known American strikes on Iranian targets in several weeks. Iran responded by firing missiles at U.S. positions in Jordan, which were intercepted. The exchange ended a period of relative calm and pushed Brent crude back toward and above the $90 level at points during the day, with WTI also advancing. Shipping volumes through the strait remain well below pre-conflict norms, and the renewed hostilities have once again elevated the geopolitical risk premium in energy markets.

Treasury yields firm ed as the combination of higher oil prices and the weekend escalation reinforced concerns about sticky inflation. This followed Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh’s Jackson Hole remarks on Friday, in which he emphasized that inflation remains above target and that the Fed “has work to do” if underlying trends do not improve at a sufficient pace. Market pricing for a rate increase later this year moved higher in response.

Stepping back, August proved resilient for equities despite elevated geopolitical risk, firm energy prices, and a more hawkish policy tone. Strong corporate earnings—particularly from the technology and AI complex—provided an important fundamental offset. Nvidia’s guidance for roughly 70% revenue growth into fiscal 2028, alongside robust results from other high-profile technology names, reinforced the durability of AI infrastructure demand even as valuation sensitivity and macro uncertainty remain elevated.

We continue to view the longer-term outlook for technology and AI-related equities as constructive, supported by resilient demand for advanced semiconductors, data-center infrastructure, and enterprise software platforms. At the same time, concentration risk, elevated valuations, residual geopolitical uncertainty around energy supply, and the possibility of further monetary tightening argue for discipline. Our preference remains selective exposure to quality companies with clear visibility into AI-driven revenue growth, genuine pricing power, and strong free-cash-flow generation.

Looking ahead, the coming week brings important labor market data, ISM surveys, and residual technology earnings, including reports from Broadcom and others. Developments around the Strait of Hormuz, the path of bond yields and energy prices, and the evolving policy outlook will remain central. We will continue to monitor these factors closely and keep you updated as conditions evolve.
