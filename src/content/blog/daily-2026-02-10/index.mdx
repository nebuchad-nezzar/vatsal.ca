February 10, 2026

We’ll call the overnight session “risk on” with stocks mostly higher. The “Takaichi” trade continues to play out in Japan – for equities at least with the Nikkei up another 2.25% overnight. The long USDJPY and short JGB Takaichi trade components, well that’s a different story.

The ongoing post-election bounce in Japan helped lift stocks across the board in Asia (Australia was flat) with sentiment getting an added boost after chipmaker TSMC reported a 37% jump in revenue for January. TSMC’s shares also hit a record high on the Taiwan stock exchange, leading the index to hit a record high as well. Elsewhere, things may be a bit more precarious regarding U.

S.-China relations. A White House official noted that President Trump’s trip to China to meet with President Xi had not yet been finalized. Meanwhile, a senior policy advisor to President Xi said China will resolutely crack down on Taiwan independence amid continued arms sales by the U.

S. to Taiwan. This is on top of the recent recommendation from China to pare back holdings of U. S.

Treasuries. European bourses equity ex-UK all closed in the green today although gains have been mild. France’s President Macron commented that now was a good time for Europe to launch a means of joint borrowing, for example via Eurobonds while also noting that plans to make Europe more sovereign are not progressing fast enough.

After rising significantly over the previous two sessions, North American stock markets showed no clear direction during Tuesday’s trading. While the Dow reached a new record intraday high in early trading, the Nasdaq and the S&P 500 spent the day bouncing back and forth across the unchanged line. The choppy trading came as traders seemed reluctant to make significant moves ahead of the release of the Labor Department’s closely watched monthly jobs report on Wednesday.

The report, delayed by last week’s brief government shutdown, is expected to show employment climbed by 70,000 jobs in January after rising by 50,000 jobs in December. The unemployment rate is expected to hold at 4.4 percent. Meanwhile, traders largely shrugged off a Commerce Department report showing retail sales in the U.

S. were unexpectedly flat in the month of December. The report said retail sales came in virtually unchanged in December after climbing by 0.6 percent in November. Economists had expected retail sales to rise by 0.4 percent.

Excluding a slight dip in sales by motor vehicle and parts dealers, retail sales were still virtually unchanged in December after increasing by 0.4 percent in November. Ex-auto sales were expected to grow by 0.3 percent. A separate report released by the Labor Department showed import prices in the U.

S. crept up in line with estimates in the month of December. Housing stocks showed a substantial move to the upside amid a notable decrease by treasury yields, driving the Philadelphia Housing Sector Index up by 3.4 percent to a five-month closing high. On the other hand, brokerage stocks moved sharply lower on the day, dragging the NYSE Arca Broker/Dealer Index down by 2.5 percent.

The index ended the previous session at a record closing high. In the bond market, treasuries moved notably higher after closing roughly flat for two straight sessions. Subsequently, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, slid 5.1 basis points to a nearly one-month closing low of 4.147 percent.
