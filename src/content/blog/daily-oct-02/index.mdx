This morning's labor data delivered a clear reminder of how asymmetric this market's reaction function has become: weak economic data, in the current regime, is bullish for risk assets. September payrolls rose just 29,000, well short of the roughly 84,000 consensus, while the unemployment rate climbed to 4.2% from 4.1%. Markets interpreted the print as sufficient justification for the Federal Reserve to remain on hold at its October meeting, and repriced accordingly — the implied probability of an October hike fell from 66% earlier this week to approximately 20% by the close.

Equity markets responded decisively. The Nasdaq Composite advanced over 1%, touching an intraday high near 27,190. The S&P 500 gained roughly 0.8% to close near 7,725, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose about half a percent to 51,176. On a weekly basis, however, the gains were not uniform: the Nasdaq closed the week higher, while the Dow and S&P 500 both registered weekly losses. This distinction matters — today's rally was concentrated in technology and AI-adjacent names rather than broad-based, and that concentration should inform how we size exposure to today's move.

Fixed income markets offered a more measured read than equities did. The 10-year Treasury yield eased to 5.18%, a welcome retracement after a month of sustained upward pressure. The short end was more telling: the 2-year yield fell sharply to 4.69% in the immediate aftermath of the release before reversing to close five basis points higher at 4.84%. We read this as the market distinguishing between near-term policy accommodation and the broader rate trajectory — a single soft payrolls print buys the Fed room for one meeting; it does not, on its own, alter the medium-term path.

Energy markets provided an additional tailwind, with crude prices declining on reports of coordinated efforts to stabilize global supply, extending the relief that began earlier in the week on hopes of de-escalation around the Strait of Hormuz. The combination of softer energy prices and reduced near-term hike probability created favorable conditions for risk assets across the session.

Within technology, Nvidia led index gains and drove the Nasdaq's intraday high, extending momentum from Micron's strong earnings print yesterday and reaffirming investor conviction in the AI memory and compute complex. HPE also posted a notable AI-related gain, underscoring that the infrastructure buildout continues to generate beneficiaries beyond the established mega-cap names — consistent with the broadening theme we flagged around Akamai earlier this week.

On rare earths, there is no new development to report. The U.S.-China trade truce extension continues to hold, and the absence of fresh headlines remains a modest, if underappreciated, source of stability.

We view today's move constructively but are cautious about extrapolating a single data point into a sustained trend. The past two sessions — Micron's earnings beat and today's dovish policy repricing — represent genuine fundamental improvement after a difficult September. That said, a softening labor market is not an unambiguous positive: it provides the Fed near-term flexibility while simultaneously signaling underlying economic deceleration. The reversal in the 2-year yield suggests fixed income markets share this ambivalence even where equities have not yet priced it in. Our positioning bias remains to stay engaged with AI monetization and infrastructure-beneficiary names leading this move, while resisting the temptation to treat one data release as confirmation of a durable shift in the macro backdrop. The more relevant test will be whether labor market softness translates into weaker consumer spending and Q4 earnings revisions.

We will be watching for Federal Reserve commentary pushing back against the market's dovish repricing, given that a rising unemployment rate is not an unambiguous "all clear" signal. We are also monitoring whether today's AI-led advance broadens beyond Nvidia, Micron, and HPE, or remains narrowly concentrated. With an October hike now viewed as unlikely, incoming inflation data becomes the next material swing factor — any upside surprise would likely reverse a meaningful portion of today's move.
