September 2, 2026

U.S. equities rebounded modestly on Wednesday, reclaiming some ground after a three-day losing streak as investors searched for value amid elevated yields and persistent geopolitical risks. The S&P 500 rose approximately 0.46% to around 7,667, the Dow Jones Industrial Average advanced roughly 0.56% to about 53,062, and the Nasdaq Composite gained around 0.45% to approximately 26,218. The session reflected a tentative recovery in risk appetite, though trading remained cautious given the backdrop of high energy prices and firm Treasury yields.

Sector performance was more balanced than in recent sessions. Materials and Energy showed relative strength, supported by elevated commodity prices, while select cyclical areas including regional banks and transportation also participated. Technology was mixed: hardware names such as Dell surged more than 15% after the company raised its full-year profit and revenue guidance, providing a positive counterpoint within the broader AI and data-center theme. Software and services faced more pressure, reflecting ongoing valuation sensitivity in rate-sensitive growth areas. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index remained volatile as investors positioned ahead of and reacted to Broadcom’s results after the close.

Broadcom delivered a solid third-quarter report, with revenue of $29.6 billion (up 86% year-over-year) and adjusted EPS of $3.32, both ahead of expectations. AI semiconductor revenue reached $16.7 billion, up 221% year-over-year. However, fourth-quarter revenue guidance of approximately $34.8 billion came in slightly below some elevated expectations, sending shares lower in after-hours trading. The results underscored continued strong demand for custom AI accelerators and networking while highlighting the market’s limited tolerance for any perceived slowing in the growth trajectory.

Oil prices held near recent highs, with Brent trading around $95 and WTI near $90–$91. Renewed military exchanges between the United States and Iran, including further strikes and reports of tanker incidents near the Strait of Hormuz, kept the geopolitical risk premium elevated. Shipping volumes through the strait remain well below pre-conflict norms, sustaining concerns about potential supply disruptions. The 10-year U.S. Treasury yield briefly rose above 4.82%, its highest intraday level since late 2023, as the global bond market continued to reflect inflation and fiscal concerns. Japan’s 10-year yield, which had touched 3% earlier in the week, remained elevated as part of the broader rise in long-term rates.

Overall, the session illustrated a market attempting to stabilize after recent pressure from higher yields, elevated energy prices, and geopolitical uncertainty. Strong corporate results in parts of the technology complex, particularly around AI infrastructure, continue to provide fundamental support. At the same time, valuation sensitivity, the path of monetary policy, and the unresolved situation in the Middle East remain important moderating factors.

We maintain a constructive longer-term view on technology and AI-related equities, supported by resilient demand for advanced semiconductors, data-center infrastructure, and related hardware and networking solutions. Concentration risk, elevated valuations, residual geopolitical uncertainty around energy supply, and the possibility of further monetary tightening warrant discipline. Our preference remains selective exposure to quality companies with clear visibility into AI-driven revenue growth, genuine pricing power, and strong free-cash-flow generation.

Looking ahead, residual technology earnings, labor market data, ISM surveys, and continued developments around the Strait of Hormuz and bond yields will remain key. We will monitor these factors closely and keep you updated as conditions evolve.
