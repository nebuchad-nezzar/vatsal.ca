U.S. equities extended their pullback from recent record highs on Tuesday, with technology and semiconductor stocks leading the decline amid rising bond yields, firmer oil prices, and a further hardening of the geopolitical backdrop. The S&P 500 fell 0.7% to 7,691.76, the Nasdaq Composite dropped 1.3% to 26,289.71, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average slipped 0.2% to 53,343.40. The session marked a third consecutive modest loss for the S&P 500 since its all-time high last Thursday. Health care and energy provided some offset, but heavyweight AI-related names weighed heavily on the broader market.

Nvidia, Micron Technology, and Broadcom were among the largest drags as the semiconductor complex came under renewed pressure. A closely watched gauge of chip stocks declined roughly 5%, reflecting ongoing debate about elevated valuations and the sustainability of AI-related capital spending. Treasury yields remained elevated, with the 30-year yield hovering near multi-decade highs and the 10-year yield firm. Oil prices strengthened further as geopolitical risks around the Strait of Hormuz intensified.

On the geopolitical front, the 60-day memorandum of understanding between the United States and Iran expired without extension. President Trump stated that no talks or conversations with Iran are ongoing or scheduled and reiterated that the U.S. naval blockade of Iranian ports remains in full force. Iran maintained that the Strait of Hormuz will remain closed until Washington lifts the blockade, releases frozen assets, removes oil sanctions, and ceases military operations. A vessel was struck by an unidentified projectile while transiting the strait, resulting in a crew casualty, and shipping traffic continued to run at a fraction of pre-conflict levels. The United Arab Emirates announced it was cutting off trade, commercial exchanges, and financial transactions with Iran. These developments reinforced the risk premium in energy markets and contributed to the more cautious tone across risk assets.

Asian markets finished mostly lower, with technology and semiconductor names under particular pressure in Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan. European equities were mixed to softer. On the corporate front, Keysight Technologies reported strong third-quarter results, delivering record revenue of $1.85 billion, up 36% year-over-year, with robust growth in Communications Solutions and Electronic Industrial Solutions. The company also improved its full-year outlook. Despite the solid print from Keysight and earlier strength in parts of the semiconductor equipment complex, the broader technology and semiconductor complex remained under pressure amid valuation concerns and rising yields.

The combination of rising long-end yields, higher energy prices, an unresolved Hormuz standoff, and selective profit-taking in crowded AI and semiconductor positions created a more cautious tone across risk assets. While recent inflation data have reduced near-term odds of a Federal Reserve rate hike, the persistence of elevated oil prices and the backup in bond yields continue to complicate the outlook for both growth and multiples.

We continue to expect the global equity rally to extend over a longer horizon, supported by broad earnings strength, structural growth themes around AI infrastructure, defense, electrification, and energy security, and an improving cyclical backdrop in several regions outside the United States. Elevated concentration risk in U.S. mega-cap technology reinforces the case for diversified exposure across Europe, Japan, and Asia ex-Japan. Our preference remains for quality cash-flow generators with genuine pricing power and selective relative-value opportunities rather than aggressive directional positioning.

This week’s remaining retail earnings, including reports from Target, Lowe’s, TJX, and Walmart, will provide further insight into the health of the U.S. consumer. We will continue to monitor residual earnings, developments around the Strait of Hormuz, and the path of bond yields and energy prices, and will keep you updated as conditions evolve.
