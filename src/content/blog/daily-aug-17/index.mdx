U.S. equities drifted lower on Monday as investors adopted a cautious stance ahead of key retail earnings and oil prices firmed amid ongoing U.S.–Iran tensions. The S&P 500 declined 0.5% to 7,745.06, the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 0.5% to 53,459.78, and the Nasdaq Composite slipped 0.3% to 26,644.91. Energy was the sole S&P 500 sector to finish higher, while communication services and consumer staples led the declines. Markets remain near recent record levels but showed little appetite for further upside in a relatively quiet session.

Oil prices strengthened, with Brent approaching the $90 level at one point, as shipping through the Strait of Hormuz remained restricted and diplomatic efforts showed no meaningful progress over the weekend. The 30-year Treasury yield climbed to 5.31% — its highest level since 2007 — while the 10-year yield edged higher near 4.68%. Gold also advanced. The combination of firmer energy prices and rising long-end yields kept risk appetite in check even as recent inflation data have reduced the probability of a near-term Federal Reserve rate hike.

Asian markets were mixed. Japan’s Nikkei advanced modestly, supported by semiconductor and technology names, while Hong Kong and mainland China posted gains. South Korea’s market was closed for a holiday. European equities closed modestly lower, with luxury stocks weighing on the region.

During the session, memory-chip related names showed relative strength, with Sandisk rising nearly 9%, highlighting continued selective interest in AI infrastructure components even as broader technology faced pressure. Meta Platforms declined more than 3% amid weakness in communication services, a reminder that not all mega-cap growth names are moving in lockstep. Space Exploration Technologies rebounded over 4% on constructive analyst commentary, illustrating how individual catalysts can still drive outsized moves in a low-conviction session. The sharp rise in the 30-year Treasury yield to multi-decade highs stood out as a notable undercurrent, underscoring persistent concern about the long-term fiscal and inflation backdrop even as shorter-term rate-hike odds have eased.

Looking ahead, this week’s retail earnings — including reports from Home Depot, Target, and Walmart — will provide important insight into the health of the U.S. consumer following last week’s soft retail sales print. We continue to expect the global equity rally to extend over time, supported by broad earnings strength, structural growth trends particularly around AI, infrastructure, and energy security, and an improving cyclical backdrop in several regions. Elevated concentration risk in U.S. mega-cap technology continues to argue for diversified exposure across Europe, Japan, and Asia ex-Japan.

European banks, industrials, health care, and “European Leaders”; high-quality Japanese companies linked to AI, semiconductors, and rising power demand; and Asia’s AI hardware supply chain alongside longer-term growth stories in India. Our preference remains for quality cash-flow generators and relative-value opportunities rather than aggressive directional exposure.
