U.S. equities navigated a more cautious session on Friday as investors digested Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh’s first Jackson Hole speech and weighed the implications for monetary policy against the strong technology earnings delivered earlier in the week. Markets opened with residual strength from Thursday’s tech-led rally but faced pressure as Warsh’s remarks reinforced concerns that inflation remains uncomfortably elevated. The S&P 500, Nasdaq Composite, and Dow Jones Industrial Average finished the day with mixed-to-modest changes, consolidating recent gains while the probability of a rate hike later this year moved higher in market pricing.

Technology remained a focal point. The powerful follow-through from Nvidia’s blockbuster results, alongside strong prints from Salesforce, CrowdStrike, Okta, Marvell, and Workday earlier in the week, continued to support selective strength in semiconductors, software, and cybersecurity. Nvidia’s guidance for roughly 70% revenue growth into fiscal 2028 and Marvell’s record data-center performance had provided important confirmation that AI infrastructure demand remains robust. However, the broader market showed more restraint on Friday as attention shifted from corporate fundamentals to the policy outlook.

The centerpiece of the day was Chair Warsh’s keynote address at the Jackson Hole Economic Policy Symposium. In measured but firm remarks, Warsh emphasized that inflation is still running above the Federal Reserve’s 2% target and that recent readings, while better than some feared, do not yet demonstrate that underlying trends have meaningfully improved. He stated clearly that policymakers “must be confident that underlying inflation is moving to our objective, clearly and at sufficient speed. Otherwise, we have work to do.” Warsh also noted that credit and loan markets are showing limited signs of policy restraint at current rate levels and called for a “quieter” Federal Reserve that does not encourage markets to look primarily to the central bank for the next trade. The tone was interpreted as opening the door more explicitly to the possibility of further rate increases if inflation progress stalls. Market-implied odds of a September hike rose following the speech.

On the geopolitical front, developments around the Strait of Hormuz remained fluid. Iran and Oman continued technical discussions on a temporary joint maritime corridor and revenue-sharing arrangements, with Qatar also engaging in mediation efforts. While these diplomatic steps have contributed to a reduction in the extreme risk premium that had driven oil prices higher earlier in the month, actual shipping volumes through the strait remain far below pre-conflict norms. Isolated incidents involving commercial vessels continue to be reported, and Iran has linked any broader reopening to conditions including sanctions relief. Oil prices traded in a relatively contained range, with Brent hovering near the high $80s and WTI in the low-to-mid $80s, reflecting the balance between diplomatic progress and persistent physical constraints.

Treasury yields responded to the Warsh comments with a modest firming, particularly at the front end, as the higher-for-longer narrative regained some traction. The combination of resilient growth indicators, still-elevated inflation, and a Federal Reserve chair signaling readiness to act if necessary has reduced expectations for near-term policy easing.

Stepping back, the week delivered important fundamental confirmation for the technology and AI complex through a series of strong earnings reports, most notably from Nvidia. At the same time, the policy backdrop has grown modestly more challenging. Elevated valuations in mega-cap technology, residual geopolitical risk around energy supply, and the possibility of further monetary tightening argue for continued selectivity.

We maintain a constructive longer-term view on technology and AI-related equities, supported by resilient demand for advanced semiconductors, data-center infrastructure, and enterprise software platforms. Concentration risk and elevated valuations warrant discipline. Our preference remains selective exposure to quality companies with clear visibility into AI-driven revenue growth, genuine pricing power, and strong free-cash-flow generation, while maintaining caution toward pure duration and highly leveraged balance sheets.

Looking ahead, residual technology results, further developments around the Strait of Hormuz, the path of bond yields and energy prices, and incoming inflation and labor data will remain key. We will continue to monitor these factors closely and keep you updated as conditions evolve.
