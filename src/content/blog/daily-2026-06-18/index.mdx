June 18, 2026

The results out of the Asian session last night fall into the “it could have been a lot worse” category. The Hang Seng and stocks in mainland China along with the ASX traded lower but the Kospi jumped more than 2% trading over 9000 for the first time while the Nikkei was up 1.6% taking out the 71000 level. Clearly the U.

S.-Iran MOU signing lifted sentiment and lower oil prices/more free flowing crude through the Strait of Hormuz will certainly help energy starved Asian economies. A weaker Yen and Korean Won help exports and let’s face it, for now demand for chips/AI related products remains immune to higher interest rates. The Nikkei and Kospi are up 41% and 115% YTD respectively, maybe the Iran deal keeps the party going a little longer.

European bourses started on the backfoot, but recovered with modest gains. This is playing a little catch-up with yesterday’s late-day North American move more than anything else. Then again, unlike Asia, Europe is not exactly a semiconductor/chip/AI hotbed.

Lower energy prices will undoubtedly help the European economy but rate hikes would definitely hurt and various central banks continue to warn against possible second round inflation effects. The ECB’s Kocher said today that inflation would stay higher for some time and that the ECB stands ready to act at any time, while this morning the BoE held rates steady as expected but it was a 7-2 vote with the Bank noting they also “stand ready to act”. Really both are hoping the deal in Iran lasts and energy prices continue on a lower path so they don’t have to hike again.

North American stock markets rose on Thursday, erasing most of their losses from a day earlier to achieve weekly gains. The market’s reversal was powered by sharp gains for big technology companies. The decline on Wednesday was driven by anticipation that the Federal Reserve will likely raise interest rates this year to fight inflation.

On Thursday, stocks faced less pressure as bond yields eased and oil prices fell for most of the day. U. S. markets will be closed Friday for Juneteenth.

Technology stocks had some of the biggest gains and the most influence on the broader market’s rise. Intel surged 10.6 per cent after President Donald Trump announced that the semiconductor giant will make chips for Apple in the U. S.

Other big semiconductor companies gained ground. Nvidia rose 3 per cent and Micron Technology jumped 8.7 per cent. On the losing end, SpaceX fell for the second straight day since its ballyhooed debut on the U.

S. stock market last week. The Elon Musk-led rocket maker and AI company was down 3.6 per cent following a 4.9 per cent loss on Wednesday. Oil prices wavered after the United States and Iran signed an agreement to end their war and reopen the Strait of Hormuz to oil tanker traffic.

Brent crude, the international standard, spent most of the day lower before settling 0.4 per cent higher at US$79.85 per barrel. U. S. benchmark crude fell 0.2 per cent to US$75.85 per barrel.

The yield on the 10-year Treasury fell to 4.45 per cent from 4.49 per cent late Wednesday. The yield on the 2-year Treasury, which more closely tracks action by the Fed, fell to 4.18 per cent from 4.20 per cent late Wednesday.
