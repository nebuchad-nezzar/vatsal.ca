February 27, 2026

The U.S. Supreme Court bombshell on Friday, February 20th prompted the White House to step up its trade rhetoric this week, immediately imposing a 10% global tariff that will soon rise to 15% under another authority.

Various world leaders called on the U.S. to honor commitments made under newly negotiated trade agreements, while a number of corporations declared they will seek refunds of IEEPA levies already paid. Trump largely stuck to the script during the State of the Union address on Tuesday, offering no big surprises for the geopolitical landscape, though by Friday in a Truth Social post he asked the high court for a ‘rehearing or readjudication’ of the tariff ruling.

Diplomatic efforts between the U.S. and Iran took much of the market’s focus, sending crude futures initially lower and then higher as the prospects of war and peace were weighed. Friday’s US PPI report came in hotter than expected across most metric, following on the heels of stronger than expected CPI data across Europe.

The overall results of these developments was a flight to safety, which brought the 10-year Treasury yield back below 4.00% for the first time since late November, while gold prices continue to levitate above $5,200/oz. Equities lost ground this week, with the S&P down 0.4%, the DJIA off 1.3%, and the Nasdaq giving up 1%. European bourses advanced; the German DAX gained 0.01% and the French CAC 40 rallied 0.08%.

Asia Pacific stock markets also rallied with mainland China exchanges gaining 2% and Japan surging 3.6%. Your BSD Global Tech Hedge Fund retreated 1.1% this past week. Corporate earnings continued to pour in, but nothing could reignite the stock rally amid high valuations and worries over geopolitics and AI disruption.

FedEx became one of the first big name firms to file suit against the Trump Administration, demanding a refund of IEEPA tariffs in the wake of last week’s Supreme Court ruling. The circular AI investment thesis was alive and well this week with a new equity investment from Meta into AMD, and Amazon confirming a $50B investment arrangement with OpenAI, securing advanced chip supplies. Market skepticism about the AI buildout continued to be seen in the reaction to Nvidia’s earning report – stellar numbers despite continuing to exclude any China business only led to a fresh sell off in shares.

M&A chatter suggested PayPal could be getting a look, and there were also reports that Fortune 500 global power firm AES is in late stage talks to be acquired. Also Netflix finally called off its deal with WBD, saying it did not want to extend a bidding war with Paramount Skydance for the HBO parent. Investors will start this week focused on geopolitics after the U.S. and Israel carried out attacks against Iran that killed supreme leader Khamenei and other senior leaders. The U.S. and Israel have called for regime change.

The oil market will see the biggest direct impact; the traders braced for volatility due to the uncertain future of an OPEC member. On the economic front, the major reports, headlined by the February jobs report, will be released Friday. January retail sales figures are also due.

Alibaba, CrowdStrike, and Broadcom are among the major companies reporting earnings. Next week should be exciting for APAC traders. Excluding geopolitics, macro traders will await three PMI reports for China.

The data has dropped surprisingly lately, and the PBoC is discussing supporting their economy with targeted assistance rather than market-wide stimulus. It will be interesting to see how the data plays out. In Japan, traders will listen closely to Governor Ueda’s speech on Monday night.

The Bank of Japan could implement a hike toward mid-month, and he recently strengthened his tone regarding that possibility. The heavy slate of data also spreads to Europe with the release of inflation data (consumer prices and producer prices). Friday will also see the yearly data for EU GDP, so keep an eye on this one.

But the largest factor will be the Swiss inflation report, which should confirm whether negative rates will continue. A tough test for FX markets.
