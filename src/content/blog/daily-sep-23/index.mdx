Equities gave back a portion of recent gains on Wednesday as a renewed rise in Treasury yields and a rebound in oil prices reversed the constructive tone that had developed earlier in the week. The S&P 500 declined 0.75% to 7,706.03, the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 0.7% to approximately 51,512, and the Nasdaq Composite retreated 1.1% from its record close. The session reflected profit-taking after the strong technology-led advance of the prior two days, alongside a reassessment of the interest-rate and energy backdrop.

The 10-year Treasury yield moved back above 5%, reaching levels not sustained in nearly two decades. The rise in yields reasserted the higher-for-longer narrative that followed last week’s Federal Reserve decision. Mortgage rates correspondingly firmed, with the average 30-year fixed rate reported near 7.1%, reinforcing the ongoing pressure on housing affordability. The market continues to price a meaningful probability of further policy tightening later this year.

Crude prices reversed their multi-day decline, with Brent moving back above $100 and at times approaching $102. The rebound limited the relief that lower energy prices had provided to inflation expectations and bond yields earlier in the week. Diplomatic signals around the Iran conflict remained mixed. President Trump described recent U.S.-Iran discussions on the sidelines of the United Nations General Assembly as productive and indicated that another meeting is expected, while reiterating that a broader resolution is more likely after the midterm elections. Reports of Saudi efforts to restore East-West pipeline flows and intermittent improvements in Hormuz transit have reduced the most extreme risk premium, yet the underlying constraints on Middle East energy infrastructure have not been removed.

Technology, which had led the rebound, experienced the sharpest consolidation. Semiconductor names gave back a portion of recent gains as investors locked in profits following the surge that had pushed several AI-related equities, including AMD, to elevated valuations. Meta continued to show relative resilience on sustained interest in its AI initiatives, but the broader complex faced selling pressure. The divergence between high-duration growth assets and the rising cost of capital remains a central tension in portfolio construction.

Attention is now focused on the Trump-Xi meeting, which begins against a backdrop of trade and artificial-intelligence discussions. Any signals on tariff policy, technology cooperation, or broader strategic stability will be closely watched for implications across both equity and fixed-income markets.

From a portfolio perspective, the environment continues to favor selectivity. The combination of a Federal Reserve that has shifted into tightening mode, long-term yields again testing multi-year highs, and incomplete resolution of energy-supply risks argues against broad directional exposure. Preference remains for companies with durable free-cash-flow generation, genuine pricing power, and clear linkage to structural demand in semiconductors and AI infrastructure. Concentration risk and elevated valuations require ongoing discipline, particularly while the cost of capital remains elevated and geopolitical uncertainty persists.
