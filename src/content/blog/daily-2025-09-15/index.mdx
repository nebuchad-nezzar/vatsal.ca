September 15, 2025

Japan was closed for holiday so volumes were a bit lighter than normal in Asia overnight in what was a mixed session. South Korea’s Kospi outperformed hitting another record high and is now up over 50% from the April lows. The Hang Seng managed to hang on to small gains while stocks in mainland China underperformed after a less than inspiring data dump.

All the numbers were softer than expected with the slowest industrial production in a year, the softest retail sales since last November and fixed assets growth at just 0.5%, the weakest since 2020. I suppose you have to keep things in perspective, industrial production and retail sales were still up 5.2% and 3.4% respectively, it’s simply that the market expects a lot from the Chinese data. The back and forth on U.S.-China trade continues with China and the U.S. meeting for a second day in Spain today. Treasury Secretary Bessent said that his team had made good progress with China but it might not all be smooth sailing.

Over the weekend China declared that a preliminary antitrust probe into Nvidia found that the company violated its laws. The result could lead to fines of up to 10% of Nvidia’s $1.5bio Chinese revenue. Not much of a dent for NVDA I suppose, but 15% to the U.S. government, 10% to China, it is starting to add up. Just another trade bargaining chip I suppose. After the close Friday, Fitch downgraded France from AA- to A+ citing high debt and a weak fiscal record.

What did the market do? Stocks rallied and the Euro is higher today! Clearly, the downgrade was priced in and on the bright side, both Spain and Portugal saw their ratings actually get upgraded over the weekend.

North American stock markets moved mostly higher during trading on Monday, adding to the strong gains posted last week. With the continued upward move, the Nasdaq and the S&P 500 reached new record closing highs. The strength came following positive comments from President Donald Trump about trade talks between top U.S. and Chinese officials in Europe. In a post on Truth Social, Trump said that the talks have “gone VERY WELL!” adding that a “deal was also reached on a ‘certain’ company that young people in our Country very much wanted to save,” likely referring to Chinese-owned social media platform TikTok. Trump also said that he will be speaking with Chinese President Xi Jinping on Friday and described the relationship as “a very strong one.” Meanwhile, traders also continued to look ahead to the Federal Reserve’s monetary policy announcement on Wednesday.

With recent data showing relatively subdued inflation and a weakening labor market, the Fed is widely expected to lower interest rates by at least a quarter point. CME Group’s FedWatch Tool is currently indicating a 96.4 percent chance the Fed will cut rates by 25 basis points and a slim 3.6 percent chance of a half-point rate cut. Traders are likely to pay close attention to the Fed’s accompanying statement as well as Fed Chair Jerome Powell’s post-meeting comments for clues about the likelihood of further rate cuts.

Currently, the Fed is widely expected to lower rates by another 25 basis points at both its October and December meetings, although Powell is likely to say future rate cuts will depend on incoming economic data. Computer hardware stocks turned in some of the market’s best performances on the day, with the NYSE Arca Computer Hardware Index surging by 2.5 percent to a record closing high. Significant strength was also visible among networking stocks, as reflected by the 2.3 percent jump by the NYSE Arca Networking Index.

In the bond market, treasuries regained ground following the pullback seen during last Friday’s session. Subsequently, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, fell 2.7 basis points to 4.034 percent. While trading on Tuesday may be impacted by reaction to reports on retail sales, industrial production and import and export prices, activity is likely to be somewhat subdued ahead of the Fed’s monetary policy announcement on Wednesday.
