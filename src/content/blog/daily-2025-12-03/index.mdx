December 3, 2025

Overnight, Asian equity indices were mixed. The Nikkei and Kospi each rose by more than 1% as tech rebounded (SoftBank up 6%). The Hang Seng/stocks in mainland China finished the session lower despite Chinese services PMI data remaining in its 33rd straight month of expansion.

China’s Ministry of Culture and Tourism said they will unveil plans to boost the tourism and aviation sectors but “plans” won’t offset ongoing concern around China’s property market. European bourses closed modestly higher today. Maybe there remains some optimism around the prospect for peace in Ukraine but that might be categorized as “irrational exuberance”.

The U. S. delegation went home pretty much empty handed yesterday with the Kremlin agreeing to a few points but nothing that really matters. Putin has been quoted as saying “Europeans are making demands that are not acceptable to Russia, if Europe wants to fight a war, we’re ready now.

We’ll increase strikes on Ukrainian facilities and vessels and we’ll take measures against tankers of those countries that help Ukraine.” That doesn’t sound like someone in a peaceful mood. On a positive note, services PMI data out of Europe was mostly stronger than expected this morning and the details seem pretty encouraging. Spain remains hot noting firms can’t meet workloads, Italy posted its strongest expansion in two and a half years and even France saw its first private sector growth in more than a year.

After showing a lack of direction early in the session, North American stock markets moved mostly higher over the course of the trading day on Wednesday. While the Nasdaq and the S&P 500 posted modest gains, the narrower Dow showed a more notable move to the upside. The advance by the Dow came as shares of UnitedHealth spiked by 4.7 percent, while Goldman Sachs, McDonald’s and Amgen also posted strong gains.

On the other hand, shares of Microsoft slumped by 2.5 percent after a report from The Information said the software giant lowered growth targets for artificial intelligence software sales. The strength in the broader markets came following the release of a report from payroll processor ADP showing an unexpected decrease in private sector employment in the month of November. ADP said private sector employment fell by 32,000 jobs in November after climbing by an upwardly revised 47,000 jobs in October.

Economists had expected private sector employment to inch up by 10,000 jobs compared to the addition of 42,000 jobs originally reported for the previous month. The data added to recently renewed optimism the Federal Reserve will once again lower interest rates at its monetary policy meeting next week. CME Group’s FedWatch Tool is currently indicating an 89.0 percent chance the Fed will cut rates by another quarter point next week.

Meanwhile, a separate report from the Institute for Supply Management showing an unexpected increase by its reading on service sector activity. The ISM said its services PMI inched up to 52.6 in November after climbing to 52.4 in October, with a reading above 50 indicating growth. Economists had expected the index to edge down to 52.1.

With the unexpected increase, the services PMI reached its highest level since hitting 53.5 in February. In the bond market, treasuries moved to the upside in reaction to the private sector jobs data. As a result, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, fell 2.9 basis points to 4.057 percent. Trading on Thursday may be impacted by reaction to a report on weekly jobless claims, which may shed additional light on the strength of the labor market.
