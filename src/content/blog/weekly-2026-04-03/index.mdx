April 3, 2026

Global equity markets found support levels this past week despite continued uncertainty surrounding the Iran war, which entered a fifth week. The S&P bounced forcefully off of 6400. Aggressive compression in PE metrics throughout March offered a decent entry point for some value investors.

More broadly, investors welcomed messaging from the White House indicating Trump is considering ending the war even without a resolution regarding the Strait of Hormuz. Nevertheless, President Trump continued to threaten escalation while simultaneously saying talks were going well. Iran still wouldn’t confirm any direct negotiations have taken place, though Tehran finally acknowledged that some messages had been exchanged via intermediaries.

By Wednesday night’s primetime address, Trump had evolved his message to say that hostilities will conclude in 2 or 3 weeks, and after that the Strait of Hormuz might open ‘naturally’ due to forces of commerce or other countries [not the U. S.] will have to take the lead in policing the Strait. With the war’s end potentially in sight, Treasury yields retreated alongside the improving sentiment for stocks in a shortened trading week which leads into the President’s post-Easter deadline that threatens to expand energy infrastructure attacks.

Oil prices remained elevated with WTI trading above Brent, north of $110/barrel into Friday’s session (as May and June front month contracts overlapped). Aluminum prices surged after Iran hit a UAE processing facility. Metals markets and firms were also impacted by a White House announcement rejiggering its sectoral tariffs on steel, aluminum and copper, which came alongside a separate tariff reboot on pharmaceutical imports.

The U. S. dollar index touched its highest level in nearly a year with USD/JPY briefly topping that key 160 level. For the week, the S&P gained 3.4%, the DJIA rose 3%, and the Nasdaq rebounded 4.4%.

European bourses rallied; the German DAX advanced 3.9% and the French CAC 40 gained 3.4%. Asia Pacific stock markets remained under pressure with mainland China exchanges and the Hong Kong Hang Seng retreating 1%. Your BSD Global Tech Hedge Fund rose 2.7% this past week.

Corporate news this week continued the major themes of the year so far: Some disappointing earnings, notable M&A and investment stories, and continued pressure on the private credit market. Nike put up a better than expected Q3 result, but margins weakened and Q4 guidance was just awful as the Shoe Dog’s company continues to struggle in its turnaround plan. In deals news, Marvell got $2B from Nvidia — the tech giant’s latest investment to solidify its position at the center of the AI hardware revolution.

Elsewhere, McCormick agreed to combine itself with Unilever’s Foods business in a $44.8B deal that largely underwhelmed investors. Private credit continued to be under the microscope, even as some Fed officials downplayed the risks. Blue Owl disclosed that two of its funds would cap redemptions at 5% after receiving requests for 21.9% and 40.7% of their respective holdings.

On Thursday, space launch and satellite tech stocks rocketed higher as traders hungered for more information about the record SpaceX IPO coming later this year, which is now expected to be valued at over $2 trillion. Investors head into the new week focused on inflation data and energy. Oil will be an early driver, with OPEC+ meeting Sunday to decide on output policy amid the surge in prices, the closure of the Strait of Hormuz and whipsawing expectations for either escalation or de-escalation in the Iran war.

The macroeconomic spotlight will focus on inflation reports, with the core PCE index due Thursday and the March Consumer Price Index on Friday. Economists expect core CPI to hold at 2.5% annually, making the data critical for shaping expectations around Federal Reserve policy. Fed minutes on Wednesday and remarks from policymakers, including Austan Goolsbee, will also be closely watched.

Earnings are led by Delta Air Lines, Constellation Brands, Levi Strauss, and BlackBerry, offering insight into travel demand, consumer trends, and enterprise spending. In tech, the HumanX AI Conference in San Francisco will feature companies including Nvidia, Microsoft, Amazon, and Alphabet, keeping AI momentum in focus.
