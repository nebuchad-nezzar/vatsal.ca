Another choppy session on the Street today, and if you're feeling like markets can't decide whether to be scared of inflation or excited about AI, you're reading the tape correctly. We closed in a fairly narrow band, but the internals tell a much more interesting story than the headline indices suggest.

Let's start where the real action is — rates, not stocks. The 10-year Treasury yield jumped to 5.11%, its highest level since 2007, while the 30-year hit a post-2004 peak above 5.4%. That's the kind of move that should have every equity allocator recalibrating discount rates this weekend. The selloff in bonds deepened further into the evening, pushing the 10-year eight basis points higher to 5.20%, and an expanded Treasury buyback operation failed to meet investor expectations — not a great signal for demand at the long end. Markets are now pricing in a 66% chance of a quarter-point Fed hike at the October meeting, up sharply from just 8.8% a month ago. That repricing has been the dominant force in this tape all week, and frankly, it's the story every other headline is competing with for attention.

Here's the piece I think is getting under-discussed: the yen carry trade is quietly being squeezed from both sides, and it's worth having on your radar. The BOJ hiked its policy rate to 1.25% on September 18 — a 31-year high — and even though the yen initially sold off past 156 because the move was fully priced in, the policy trajectory has clearly turned. The rate gap with the Fed has narrowed to roughly 275 basis points, down materially from the 300+ bps world we were in earlier this year, and futures markets are now pricing real odds of another BOJ hike in December. Layer that against a Fed that's suddenly back in hiking mode rather than cutting, and the funding-cost cushion that's kept short-JPY, long-risk-asset trades profitable is compressing from both directions simultaneously. This isn't a 2024-style disorderly unwind yet — desks are better hedged and expectations are more anchored this time — but it's exactly the kind of dual-tightening setup that has historically preceded volatility spikes in carry-funded positions, and it's a genuine tail risk sitting underneath an already jumpy rates market.

Tech had a real split-screen day, and it's worth noting there was no earnings catalyst driving any of it — the big mega-cap reports are behind us for the quarter, and the next real test doesn't land until Micron reports on September 29, which will be the first genuine read on AI-memory demand since the summer. So today's moves were pure positioning and headline risk rather than fundamentals. Oracle was the session's biggest casualty, falling 3.5% after declaring force majeure over setbacks to a data center development in New Mexico — a reminder that the AI infrastructure buildout isn't a straight line, and execution risk on these mega-projects is real. Chipmakers traded mixed amid heavy debt issuance tied to AI infrastructure financing, which is worth watching as more of this capex gets funded with leverage rather than free cash flow — and it's worth noting that a chunk of that debt issuance is happening against the backdrop of the same rising-rate environment squeezing carry trades, which raises the cost of that leverage in real time. On the brighter side, Alphabet added roughly 1.5% as AI hyperscalers rebounded from earlier losses this week, and Meta continues to be the AI story of the month, with shares up roughly 12% for the week on strong reception to its new AI assistant, Muse, which analysts believe could start pressuring banks, retailers, and online shopping platforms on their own turf. Microsoft and Amazon were largely directionless, drifting with the broader tape rather than driving it. The read-through here is that capital is still chasing the AI theme, but investors are getting pickier about which exposure they want — infrastructure execution risk is being punished, while product and distribution wins are being rewarded.

The Middle East remained a major swing factor. Brent crude settled above $106 after briefly paring its rally on reports that the US and Iran are exploring a phased deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. That headline alone took some of the edge off what had been a sharper risk-off move earlier in the session, helping pull the major indices off their lows. This is the kind of setup where headline risk cuts both ways intraday — any breakdown in those talks likely sends oil right back toward the highs and reignites the inflation-fear, rate-hike narrative that's been weighing on risk assets all week.

The other geopolitical story investors should have front and center today: Xi Jinping's first U.S. state visit in over a decade culminated in a White House meeting with Trump, and the tangible outcome was an extension of the U.S.-China trade truce by two months, to January 10. That matters directly for the rare earth story — the truce is what's been keeping Beijing's export controls on critical minerals suspended, so pushing the expiry out removes a near-term supply chain flashpoint that had been sitting on the calendar for mid-November. Beyond that, expectations were low going in and reporting suggests they were met — no major breakthroughs on Taiwan or the AI rivalry between the two countries, both of which remain unresolved fault lines. Still, in a week this jumpy, "nothing got worse, and the trade clock got extended" is itself a mildly risk-positive data point, and it's likely part of why equities found some footing off the lows today alongside the Hormuz headlines.

We're in a market where five forces are fighting for control: sticky inflation and rising real rates, a still-strong but increasingly selective AI capex cycle, geopolitical tail risk out of the Middle East, a global tightening cycle that's now squeezing carry trades from Tokyo to Washington, and a fragile but extended U.S.-China truce that's buying time rather than resolving anything. None of these are resolving cleanly, which is exactly why we're seeing this kind of rangebound, high-dispersion price action rather than a clean directional trend. My view heading into Friday is to stay selective within tech, favoring names monetizing AI through product, advertising, and cloud over those bearing the infrastructure buildout risk, keep a close eye on the long end of the curve, and treat the yen as a genuine cross-asset signal rather than a side conversation — a disorderly move there has a track record of spilling into global equities fast.

A few catalysts worth flagging for readers before they head into the weekend. Micron reports fiscal Q4 earnings on September 29 — the first real read on AI-memory demand since the summer, and a likely swing factor for the whole chip complex. The Fed's October meeting is now a coin-flip-plus, with markets pricing a 66% chance of a hike, and any incoming inflation data between now and then will move that number fast. On the currency side, the BOJ's December meeting is where the market will find out whether the yen's policy trajectory is real or overpriced, which matters for anyone with carry-trade-adjacent exposure. And the Strait of Hormuz talks remain the single biggest swing factor for oil and, by extension, the inflation narrative — a breakdown there could undo a lot of this week's relative calm overnight.
