January 21, 2026

Overnight, Asian equity indices were mixed which is an accomplishment given the current backdrop. Japanese Finance Minister Katayama seems to have soothed the fixed income market at least temporarily. She spoke from Davos saying she was confident the bond market situation will improve, that fiscal sustainability will be maintained and that the focus is on higher tax revenues as the economy shifts to growth oriented mode.

Of course, what else would you expect her to say. We have heard this many times previously from many different politicians “don’t worry, economic growth will be so incredible, everything will be alright”. Time will tell.

Elsewhere, President Xi stated that China planned to make domestic demand the main driving force for economy. The Korean Won weakened past 1480 (we haven’t seen 1500 since the GFC) before turning around after FX authorities said they expect the exchange rate to be around 1400 in one to two months (intervention an option no doubt). Finally, a little bit of good news out of Asia: Taiwan exports increased 43.8% YoY, the fastest growth since February 2021.

European bourses were mixed today. CNN reports EU leaders are looking for possible Greenland offramps including an expanded military presence on the island, economic and investment agreements and restrictions on Chinese investment in the territory but it seems that the President has little interest in turning back now. After showing a strong move to the upside early in the session, North American stock markets saw significant volatility over the course of the trading day on Wednesday.

The major averages largely gave back their early gains in late morning trading before experiencing an afternoon resurgence and ending the day sharply higher. The volatility came as traders reacted to President Donald Trump’s latest remarks about his efforts to take control of Greenland. Early buying interest was generated in reaction to Trump’s speech at the World Economic Forum in Davos, Switzerland, where he ruled out the use of military force to take control of Greenland.

Rather than using military force, Trump called for “immediate negotiations” with Denmark to “discuss the acquisition of Greenland by the United States.” However, buying interest waned over the course of the morning amid lingering concerns about trade between the U. S. and Europe due to the dispute. Buying interest re-emerged in the afternoon after Trump said in a post on Truth Social that the “framework” of a deal on Greenland was formed during a “very productive” meeting with NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte.

Based upon this understanding, Trump said he would not be going forward with the tariffs he threatened to impose on several European nations if they opposed his attempt to purchase the Danish territory. Oil service stocks turned in some of the market’s best performances amid an uptick by the price of crude oil, with the Philadelphia Oil Service Index soaring by 4.8 percent to its best closing level in over a year. Substantial strength was also visible among computer hardware stocks, as reflected by the 4.4 percent surge by the NYSE Arca Computer Hardware Index.

Biotechnology stocks also showed a significant move to the upside, driving the NYSE Arca Biotechnology Index up by 3.6 percent. Semiconductor, transportation and housing stocks also saw considerable strength, while software and gold stocks bucked the uptrend. In the bond market, treasuries regained ground after moving sharply lower over the past few sessions.

Subsequently, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, decreased by 4.2 basis points to 4.253 percent. Trading on Thursday may be impacted by reaction to typically closely watched readings on consumer price inflation, although the data for October and November may be seen as old news.
