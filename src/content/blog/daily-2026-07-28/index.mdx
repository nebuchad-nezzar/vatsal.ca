July 28, 2026

The overnight session started off on a pretty poor note (for Korea at least) with the Kospi falling more than 10%. For those keeping score at home, that is now a 35% drop from the high set back on June 19. Of course, the index is still up 86% over the last year but as they say, timing is everything.

As we noted yesterday with respect to the Kospi, the positive reaction to news that South Korea announced $950 billion in new AI initiatives over the weekend was pretty muted and clearly it demonstrates that the bar is getting set higher and higher for tech. Sentiment wasn’t helped by reports that South Korean regulators continue to clamp down on single stock ETFs and ETF margins and to top it off, China has apparently begun mass production DUV (Deep Ultraviolet) tools. This has been the missing piece of China’s chip making technology (and DUV has been dominated by ASML up until now).

China is said to be targeting production of up to 5 DUV machines this year and 20 by 2027. So now China will be able to make more advanced chips and has developed AI that might not be as far behind the U. S. as previously thought, and is looking at “flooding the world” with cheaper open source AI.

I am just a lowly FX person, but if you didn’t question AI valuations before, you have to be thinking a bit more about them now? On a more positive note, Axios reports today that “AI revenue is accelerating so fast that it’s starting to dwarf some of the world’s most recognizable brands”. OpenAI and Anthropic combined are set to generate about $120 billion in revenue which would put both in the top 100 companies by revenue in the Fortune 500.

I guess the question is whether they can keep it up. The Nikkei dropped 4% with tech under pressure. PM Takaichi reportedly may order the food sales tax cut as soon as Thursday and said Japan must exit excessively tight fiscal policy and put the economy on a growth path.

The problem: the market is becoming increasingly concerned about the cost of doing so. Elsewhere, things weren’t quite as bad; the ASX and Hang Seng managed small gains although stocks in mainland China fell. Let’s call it the hangover from the CXMT IPO.

Sentiment was more upbeat in Europe with equity indices trading mostly higher today. Of course, Europe doesn’t have anywhere near the tech exposure of Asia and oil continues to drift lower which also helps. North American stock markets opened lower as technology continued to sell off, but rebounded with strong volume on fresh hopes about a U.

S.-Iran peace agreement getting back on track. The continued lull in Middle East hostilities allowed WTI crude futures to decline a bit further to just under $80/bbl, while Treasury yields also continued to ease off. For its part, Iran continued to deny that there were any ongoing peace talks.

Weaker than expected readings for July U. S. consumer confidence and Richmond Fed manufacturing didn’t shift the odds-making for tomorrow’s FOMC decision: futures still show about a 1 in 3 chance of a hike on Wednesday, though the Fed’s historic tendency not to surprise markets bodes well for a hold. Boeing, UPS, and TransUnion were among the companies reporting earnings that offered encouraging commentary about the business environment.
