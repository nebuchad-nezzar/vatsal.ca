December 7, 2025

The overnight session kicked off with some very mixed results in Asia. The Hang Seng was the underperformer finishing the session down 1.2% (maybe some property market concerns hangover). Meanwhile stocks in mainland China ended in the green helped by some better than expected trade data as Chinese exports rebounded sharply in November.

The healthier exports helped to push China’s trade surplus past a record $1 trillion. Chinese authorities say their number one goal for 2026 is to improve domestic demand. European bourses were pretty much flat today, treading some water ahead of this week’s FOMC meeting.

We had two ECB speakers today and each took a rather different view on the central bank’s potential rate path. The ECB’s Rehn said that inflation risks were slightly tilted to the downside and that “full freedom of action” on rates was needed, while the ECB’s Schnabel said that she was comfortable with investors pricing in that the next ECB move in 2026 will be a hike rather than a cut. Meanwhile, a Russia-Ukraine peace deal remains front and center with UK PM Starmer hosting President Zelensky, President Macron and Chancellor Merz today in London.

Some on the U. S. side say a deal is “very close” but it only seems close if Ukraine gives up an awful lot. Not sure if the rest of Europe can accept setting that precedent.

North American stock markets moved to the upside at the start of trading on Monday but moved lower over the course of the trading session. The major averages pulled back off their early highs and into negative territory. The modest pullback may partly have reflected profit taking following recent strength in the markets, which saw the Nasdaq and S&P 500 reach their best closing levels in a month last Friday.

Overall trading activity was somewhat subdued, however, as traders looked ahead to the Federal Reserve’s monetary policy decision on Wednesday. With the Fed widely expected to lower interest rates by another quarter point, traders are likely to pay close attention to the accompanying statement for clues about the likelihood of further rate cuts next year. CME Group’s FedWatch Tool is currently indicating an 89.4 percent chance the Fed will lower rates by a quarter point on Wednesday but a 60.5 percent chance the central bank will leave rates unchanged in January.

In the bond market, treasuries are extending the downward move seen over the two previous sessions. Subsequently, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, is up by 4.3 basis points at 4.182 percent. A report on job openings in the month of October may attract attention on Tuesday, although trading activity may remain somewhat subdued as the Fed’s two-day meeting gets underway.
