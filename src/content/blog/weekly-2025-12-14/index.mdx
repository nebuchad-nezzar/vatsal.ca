December 14, 2025

Despite clearly waning enthusiasm around the AI trade, U. S. stocks traded at or near fresh all-time highs this week. Thanks in large part to a U.

S. Federal Reserve decision that wasn’t nearly as hawkish as many investors were positioned for. A larger than expected print for October JOLTS and a snapback in weekly ADP employment data helped drive the U.

S. benchmark 10-year yield to test 2-month highs before the FOMC meeting. Corporate managements conveyed mostly status quo messaging regarding a still resilient consumer and economy at the last key investment conferences of 2025. Oracle and Broadcom quarterly results were greeted with a thud despite continued explosive demand for their AI product lines.

OpenAI launched GPT-5.2 in hopes of fending off mounting competition from Google’s Gemini. Equity weakness was largely confined to technology shares. Importantly, the Russel 2K, Dow Jones Industrials and Transports remained significantly bid in a welcomed sign of broadening.

Commodity prices saw a notable rise in overall volatility. Energy remained under pressure, led by careening U. S. natural gas prices, while precious metal prices continued to rise, led by soaring silver prices.

Ahead of next week’s expected BoJ rate hike, the Yen lost additional ground despite higher JGB yields suggesting ongoing apprehension surrounding Japan’s fiscal trajectory. For the week, the S&P lost 0.6%, the Nasdaq dipped 1.6%, and the DJIA climbed 1.1%. For the past week, the S&P lost 0.6%, the Nasdaq dipped 1.6%, and the DJIA climbed 1.1%.

European bourses finished the week mixed, with Germany down 0.65% while France rose 0.57%. Asia Pacific stock markets were also bifurcated; Japan retreated 0.68% and the Hong Kong Hang Seng rose 0.42%. On Wednesday the Bank of Canada left rates unchanged before the Fed followed through and cut the U.

S. Fed funds rate by 25 bps, both as expected. There were three dissenters on the FOMC, one dove and two hawks which wasn’t viewed as overly surprising either.

What did get people’s attention was a not entirely unexpected tweak in statement language that alluded to a pause, or at least a higher bar for additional rate cuts as we move into 2026. Importantly, Fed officials also announced they would start T-bill purchases next week to manage for “ample” reserves. The size, $40B, and timing did catch some off guard with the abrupt move back to adding liquidity into the system via a “QE-lite” scenario viewed by some as a dovish signal that will also provide insurance against steeper deterioration in the labor market.

Powell and company pushed back on that notion, emphasizing their mantra that balance sheet adjustments should not be viewed in the context of a monetary policy lever. Nevertheless, many still viewed the move as a clear positive signal for risk appetite moving forward. The U.

S. dollar continued losing ground, falling to about a two month low while the U. S. yield curve steepened considerably. Ahead of a slew of key economic data next week, futures markets now project the Fed is likely on hold at the January FOMC meeting, and potentially through the first quarter.

A Friday afternoon WSJ interview with Trump revealed the President has yet to decide between the two Kevins, Warsh and Hassett, but that he wants his eventual candidate for Fed Chairman to consult with him on rates, which he thinks should get down to 1% or lower next year. Corporate news was once again dominated by the increasingly complex AI infrastructure narrative. Earnings reports from Broadcom and Oracle beat analyst estimates but growth commentary wasn’t strong enough to get investors excited again.

Likewise, reports that Nvidia was getting the U. S. government greenlight to sell its H200 chips into China was not enough to reignite bullish sentiment in the chip giant’s shares. Nvidia also had to deal with reports it might face delays in its next-gen Rubin chip due to technical difficulties in production of companion products, while Oracle faced new questions about its trajectory as a report emerged that it might have to delay some OpenAI datacenters by a year due to labor and material shortages (Oracle rebutted the report in a carefully worded response).

OpenAI was making moves this week, releasing its latest version of ChatGPT and partnering with Disney to use the Mouse House’s iconic characters in 30 second clips with video creation tool Sora. In M&A news, the WBD sale process got spicier as Paramount Skydance and the Ellison family went directly to shareholders with a hostile bid of $30/shr in cash, about $18B more than the cash component of the Netflix deal. Lululemon shares stretched higher following solid earnings and a report that the founder, Chip Wilson, is plotting a proxy fight as CEO McDonald steps down.

As the year winds down and the holidays approach, investors will be watching a busy slate of economic data this coming week. Monday brings the NAHB Housing Market Index. Tuesday is the heavy hitter, with ADP employment, building permits, housing starts, core retail sales, and the unemployment rate.

Wednesday includes MBA mortgage applications, business inventories, and the Atlanta Fed GDP update. Thursday delivers continuing jobless claims, core CPI, and the Philadelphia Fed Manufacturing Index. And Friday rounds out the week with core PCE, personal income, personal spending, and Michigan consumer sentiment. On the earnings front, companies reporting next week include Accenture, Nike, Cintas, FedEx, CarMax, BlackBerry, Paychex, Carnival, Conagra Brands, Lamb Weston, Lennar, Worthington Industries, and Duluth Holdings.
