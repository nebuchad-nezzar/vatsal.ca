October 5, 2025

Global stock markets continued to climb this week into what appears likely to be a rather lengthy impasse in Washington DC. Electronic Arts confirmed it was being taken private by a consortium led by Silver Lake in the largest LBO deal in history. That deal headlined another active Monday morning in terms of issuance, M&A announcements and notable C-Suite changes.

Vibrant animal spirits also remained evident in ongoing AI announcements, particularly how well they continue to be received by investors across the tech stack. Nvidia’s market cap topped $4.5T for the first time while OpenAI saw its private market valuation reportedly reach $500B. By Friday the U.S. government shutdown entered day three and prediction markets saw it likely to last well into October. While the President threatened to make drastic cuts to “democrat” government payrolls as soon as next week. September ADP data printed the first negative back to back prints since the pandemic solidifying expectations the Fed will cut two more times this year despite notably more circumspect commentary from several Fed officials.

ISM services data was particularly disappointing illustrating the difficulty facing Fed officials with both sides of the dual mandate under duress. WTI crude moved lower ahead of this weekend’s OPEC+ meeting where producers are expected to announce another production hike. Gold hit fresh record highs approaching $3,900 helped in part by another step up in rhetoric over the Ukraine conflict and perhaps ever lofty equity valuations.

The S&P traded up to 22.5x next year’s estimates while comparisons to the late 90’s tech bubble lingered. For the week, the S&P gained 1.1%, and the Nasdaq rose 1.3%. European bourses surged; the French CAC40 and the German DAX both advanced 2.7%.

Asia Pacific stock markets also participated in the investor enthusiasm with Japan gaining 0.9% and the Hong Kong Hang Seng rallying 3.9%. Your BSD Global Tech Hedge Fund gained 1.3% this past week. Nike beat Q1 expectations on nearly all accounts but offered up very conservative guidance citing worse than expected tariff headwinds.

Tesla reported significantly stronger than expected Q3 deliveries but much of that was likely driven by the pull forward of the expired EV tax credit. Equity indices rolled off of early highs led by Tesla. Credit score companies came under pressure following news FICO is going to allow tri-merge resellers the option to calculate and distribute scores directly to their customers eliminating reliance on the three nationwide credit bureaus.

Pfizer shares jumped 15% this week after reaching an agreement with the Trump Administration to lower drug costs through Medicaid. Money rotated into a wide array of healthcare stocks in hopes the Pfizer deal could serve as a blueprint for other companies to avoid worst case scenarios. Investor’s focus this coming week will be on a scheduled speech from Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell and on earnings, with economic data taking a backseat due to being delayed by the U.S. government shutdown. Powell is set to speak at a banking conference in Washington, D. C., on Thursday.

Traders will be hearing from several other Fed policymakers, including Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle Bowman and Governor Stephen Miran. This week also marks the final one before the third quarter earnings season begins. Number one U.S. carrier Delta Air Lines and the world’s third-largest soft drinks company, PepsiCo, highlight this week’s reports.
