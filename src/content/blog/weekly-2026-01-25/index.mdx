January 25, 2026

The numerous moving parts investors contended with last week resulted in a fresh round of volatility. Earnings season picked up with a fairly wide swath of disappointments that included 3M, GE, Netflix and Intel. But once again, much of investors’ angst centered on President Trump, in particular his play for Greenland heading into an appearance in Davos.

His threat to reignite a trade war, soured investor sentiment through the MLK holiday. UST yields jumped coming out of the break, perhaps on renewed talk of de-dollariztion trades, but more likely directly tied to the continued surge in Japanese government bond yields. The U.

S. 10-year broke above 4.2% for the first time since September. The anxiety was relatively short lived though, after Trump walked back the idea of using overwhelming force in his speech, and then emerged from a meeting with NATO Secretary-General Rutte saying a tentative deal had been reached and calling off the February 1st tariffs. Stocks surged on the news, and the U.

S. dollar recovered, while the buying back of UST was much more subdued. Also helping matters, JGB bond yields turned lower after the BoJ left rates unchanged and market expectations solidified around a June BoJ rate hike. Silver prices touched $10/oz for the first time while gold seems destined to trade at $5K/oz.

Natural gas prices jump 60% w/w ahead of an impending major winter storm and a historic arctic cold blast set to hit much of the U. S. Government and private sector officials alike espoused proactive steps being taken to secure the power grid in front of a demand spike.

Following the BoJ meeting the Yen surged on two different occasions prompting the latest round of rate check speculation which Japanese officials never corroborated. U. S.

Treasury yields recouped some of the losses earlier in the week helped by PCE that was inline with expectations. Nevertheless, the 10-year yield finished the week at ~4.25% for the first time since August. For the week, the S&P lost 0.4%, the DJIA fell 0.5%, and the Nasdaq slipped 0.1%.

European bourses retreated with the German DAX and French CAC40 falling 1.4%. Asia Pacific stock markets were once again bifurcated, with mainland China gaining 0.8% while Japan was lower by 0.2%. Your BSD Global Tech Hedge Fund fell 1.3% this past week.

A wide variety of earnings reports this week ended up mostly on the disappointing side of the ledger. The hot streak for Intel shares ended as the chipmaker produced solid Q4 results but provided tepid guidance, including for capex to be flat to down in the new fiscal year. Netflix, already weighed by its massive acquisition plan for Warner Brothers Discovery, turned in a merely in-line quarter and weak Q1 guidance.

Despite a bustling travel market, United Airlines shares fell after reporting better than expected earnings tempered by lackluster guidance. 3M Company reported mixed results as revenue missed the mark, and guided for only 3% organic sales growth in the new year. In the energy sector, Haliburton was a bright spot with a top and bottom line beat, even as management prevaricated over its part in Trump’s plans for ramping up oil operations in Venezuela. JNJ also kept shareholders happy with a decent beat on guidance expectations, weighted toward the second half of the year.

In M&A this week, Capital One took a more aggressive step into fintech as it purchased AI-powered corporate credit card and expense management startup Brex for $5.2 billion. The coming week will put Big Tech earnings and the Federal Reserve firmly in focus, with markets bracing for key signals on growth, inflation, and policy direction. The Bank of Canada will begin the celebrations on Wednesday morning (9:45 A.

M.) with its rate decision. While this should be a non-event, recent volatility makes it very interesting to see how Governor Macklem tackles the situation. Shortly after on Wednesday (14:30), the classic FOMC will take control of the markets.

Of course, keep a very close eye on any announcements regarding the Next Fed Chair as the decision could be released anytime and it will be market-moving! Apple, Microsoft, and Meta Platforms headline a heavy earnings calendar, alongside results from Tesla, Boeing, Visa, Mastercard, Exxon Mobil, and Chevron. Microsoft’s report will be closely watched for updates on AI-driven Azure growth and margins, while Apple’s results should offer insight into iPhone demand and its slower-moving AI strategy.

Markets will also track consumer confidence, durable goods orders, jobless claims, PPI data, and the Chicago PMI for confirmation on the inflation and growth outlook. Beyond earnings and the Fed, catalysts include several fintech IPOs, key M&A shareholder votes, and notable Investor Days, adding to what shapes up as a potentially volatile week for markets. Next week should be calmer for APAC and European traders, nevertheless, one can never be too innocent as assets and currencies fluctuate.

Inflation and business expectations for Switzerland and the Eurozone will be published throughout the week. To accompany the releases, Friday should be quite active with GDP releases for many EU nations including France, Germany, and preliminary GDP data for the Eurozone. In between GDP releases, keep an eye on Unemployment Rate data and CPI for Germany.
