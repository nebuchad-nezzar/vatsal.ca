Month-end, quarter-end, and the market gave us a genuinely mixed signal to close it out — which, given how September has gone, feels about right. Stocks opened higher across the board on a softer-than-expected inflation print, rallied hard through midday, and then faded into the close, leaving the Nasdaq as the lone index to finish green while the Dow and S&P 500 slipped back into the red. That kind of reversal — strong open, weak close — is usually a tell that conviction behind a rally is thinner than the morning headlines suggest, and today fits that pattern.

The catalyst was the PCE report, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, which showed price growth cooling more than economists had expected for September — coming in below the 0.3% monthly and 3.7% annual estimates that had been the consensus. Treasury yields eased on the print, and October rate hike bets got pared back meaningfully from where they stood earlier this week. That's a genuine, fundamentals-driven reason for optimism, and it's why the market opened strong. But the fact that equities couldn't hold onto those gains into the close tells you the bond market's broader message — that we're still in a higher-for-longer regime — hasn't actually been undone by one soft data point. One good inflation print doesn't offset a month where the 10-year touched levels not seen since the financial crisis.

Zooming out to the month itself: it was not a kind September for the broader market, even if the headline indices don't fully capture the pain. The Dow closed out the month down roughly 4.9%, its worst month in some time, and lost close to 2.7% for the quarter. The S&P 500 was more modestly lower, down about half a percent to 0.7% on the month. The Nasdaq was the standout, gaining close to 2% for the month — a reminder that this entire period has really been a story of tech and AI-adjacent names carrying the broader market on their backs while cyclicals and rate-sensitive sectors absorbed the damage from the bond selloff.

On rare earths, nothing new to flag today specifically — the U.S.-China trade truce extension from last week continues to keep Beijing's export controls suspended, and a quiet week on that front is itself the story. Worth noting alongside that: Chinese factory activity snapped a two-month contraction streak in September, a mildly encouraging signal for global growth that got relatively little attention today given everything else on the calendar, but one worth filing away — a stabilizing China is a meaningful input into both the rare earth and broader global-growth narratives heading into Q4.

Sector-wise, communication services and utilities led today's gainers, while financial services and consumer defensive names lagged — an interesting pairing, since it suggests investors were rotating toward both growth (comm services, largely AI-adjacent) and defensive yield (utilities) simultaneously, while avoiding cyclical exposure tied to the economy's near-term health. That dovetails with a softer note buried in today's data: the Consumer Confidence Present Situation subindex fell to 109.3 from 117.2 in August, a meaningful drop in how consumers are rating current business and labor conditions, even as headline inflation data improved. That's worth sitting with — it's a genuine crack in the "soft landing" narrative that the PCE print alone doesn't paper over.

All eyes now turn to Micron, reporting fiscal Q4 earnings after today's close — the first real read on AI-memory demand since the summer, and arguably the most important single data point of the week for the AI trade broadly. Given how much of this month's relative strength has been concentrated in tech, a strong Micron print could extend the Nasdaq's outperformance into October; a disappointing one would likely hit the chip complex hard at a moment when the broader market has little cushion left to absorb bad news.

September was a month that rewarded selectivity and punished broad market exposure — the Nasdaq's gain sitting next to the Dow's near-5% loss is about as clean a picture of dispersion as you'll see. Today's reversal, strong open into a weak close despite genuinely good inflation news, tells me the market isn't fully convinced the rate story is behind us, and I'd trust that instinct over one data point. Heading into October, I'm maintaining a bias toward AI names with real monetization and staying cautious on rate-sensitive and consumer-exposed cyclicals — today's confidence data is a small but real signal that the consumer isn't as resilient as the market wants to believe. Micron tonight is the swing factor for how the next few sessions trade.

Micron's earnings tonight are the immediate catalyst — watch AI-memory demand commentary closely, as it'll set the tone for chip names into October. The October Fed decision is now a genuinely live question again after today's softer PCE pared back hike odds, so expect volatility around any incoming data between now and the meeting. Keep an eye on Chinese PMI follow-through — a second consecutive month of expansion would meaningfully firm up the global growth narrative. And don't lose sight of consumer confidence data in the weeks ahead; today's Present Situation drop is worth tracking for confirmation or reversal before drawing conclusions about the health of the U.S. consumer heading into Q4.
