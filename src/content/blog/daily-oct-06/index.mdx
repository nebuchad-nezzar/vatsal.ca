U.S. equities extended their record run for a second consecutive session today, though the more instructive story may lie in what didn't keep pace. The S&P 500 closed up 0.58% to 7,818.93, a fresh record and its first new high since August. The Nasdaq Composite added 0.45% to 27,599.79 — its 23rd record close of the year — while the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 0.49% to 51,521.28. The rally carried internationally as well: European benchmarks gained roughly 0.8% across the board, and Japan's Nikkei pushed past 70,000 for the first time. South Korea broke from that pattern, slipping as Samsung and SK hynix declined — a useful reminder that even within a single global theme, the AI trade is not moving as one.

Nvidia remains the story investors cannot look away from. Shares closed at a fresh all-time high of $239.11, having touched $240.10 intraday, up 2.2% on the session and pushing the company's market value to the doorstep of $6 trillion. The move was underpinned by a $150 billion buyback expansion and a fresh upgrade from Morgan Stanley. AMD also had a strong day, adding 2.8%. The AI theme continued to broaden elsewhere too — Constellation Energy's new agreement to use Google's Gemini Enterprise platform for site selection, outage management, and infrastructure protection is a small but telling data point: the capital being deployed around AI is no longer confined to chips and hyperscalers, it is actively reshaping how adjacent industries plan and operate.

The more consequential story, however, sits in fixed income, and it is one we have been flagging for several sessions now. The 10-year Treasury yield closed near 5.3% — its highest closing level in 24 years — even after easing slightly from Monday's intraday peak above 5.34%. Deutsche Bank put a sharper point on it today than most, describing equities and bonds as effectively pricing two different economic realities, and arguing that gap cannot hold indefinitely. We share that view, and would go a step further: a single company approaching a $6 trillion valuation, set against a risk-free rate not seen since 2002, is a combination that leaves almost no room for disappointment. The AI growth story has to keep delivering at an extraordinary pace to justify current pricing, because the discount rate being applied to it has rarely been higher.

Oil offered the market some breathing room, retreating below $100 a barrel and taking pressure off the inflation narrative that has dogged sentiment for weeks. We would treat that relief with some caution rather than take it as confirmation of a resolved risk. Iran is reportedly awaiting a U.S. response today on a proposal tied to the Strait of Hormuz, and crude had in fact climbed again on that very uncertainty before today's pullback. The underlying situation remains fluid, not settled, and today's price action reflects a pause in the conversation more than an answer to it.

Currency and policy dynamics deserve attention as well. The Bank of Japan's Ueda is back under scrutiny, with markets now pricing real odds of a second consecutive October rate hike. Morgan Stanley turned neutral on the dollar today with a bullish tilt while staying outright bearish on the yen — consistent with the funding-cost pressure we have been tracking in the carry trade, where a steadily narrowing Fed-BOJ rate gap continues to erode the economics of short-yen, long-risk positioning faster than current market pricing appears to acknowledge.

Pulling this together: the fundamentals supporting this rally remain genuinely strong — Nvidia's scale, the continued broadening of AI adoption into names like Constellation, and sustained international participation all point to a real earnings story, not a purely sentiment-driven one. But the bond-equity divergence is no longer a minority view; it is being voiced by a widening circle of institutional desks, and that alone raises the odds it becomes the market's next organizing concern rather than remaining background noise. We would continue to favor AI exposure concentrated in names with demonstrated monetization and genuine pricing power, while keeping portfolio duration deliberately disciplined. A further leg higher in long-end yields, a breakdown in the Hormuz talks, or a hawkish BOJ surprise would each be a credible trigger for a sharper repricing than this market has experienced so far this cycle.

Heading into the balance of the week, the U.S. response on the Hormuz proposal is the most immediate catalyst, with direct implications for oil and the broader inflation path. The BOJ's policy signaling deserves equally close attention given its currency and carry trade consequences. And with Nvidia now effectively a bellwether for the entire AI complex at a $6 trillion scale, any incremental read on capex sustainability — hyperscaler commentary, chip demand data, or shifts in financing terms across the sector — is likely to move markets more than usual given just how concentrated this rally has become.
