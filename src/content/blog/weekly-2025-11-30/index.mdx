November 30, 2025

Equity markets took their usual risk-on turn during the U. S. holiday-shortened week. Tech regained some of the ground lost during the last few weeks and Bitcoin rebounded to the low $90Ks, while precious metals continued to climb with silver attaining a fresh record high on Friday.

Early reads on holiday shopping appeared strong; Adobe analytics reported that consumers spent $6.4B online on Thanksgiving day, and record Black Friday spending was expected to rise 8.3% y/y. The week also saw a mixture of new and older data releases that had been postponed by the government shutdown, with the overall picture mixed but with enough inflation stability to push expectations for a Fed rate cut next month closer to a done deal. Dovish indications from Fed speakers provided a boost that rate-setters will lean more to the job preservation and creation side of the dual mandate in the near term, and a report that dovish Trump partisan Kevin Hassett is the leading candidate to replace Fed Chairman Powell certainly didn’t hurt the cheaper borrowing narrative.

In foreign affairs, the Trump Administration said the Ukraine peace deal is shaping up, though the Kremlin appeared to just be going through the motions as it engaged with the White House but continued to largely shut Europe out of the process. Trump also renewed his threat of imminent action against drug cartels ‘on land’ inside of Venezuela. Canada PM Carney confirmed trade talks with the U.

S. have not restarted yet as he cast his eye toward strengthening ties to other trade partners. U. S.

Treasury yields trended lower as the 10-year danced around the 4.00% mark. Stocks traded higher and for the week, the S&P gained 3.7%, the DJIA rose 3.2%, and the Nasdaq rallied 4.9%. For the past week, the S&P gained 3.7%, and the Nasdaq rallied 4.9%.

European bourses finished the week higher; France rose 1.8% and Germany advanced 3.2%. Asia Pacific stock markets also felt the positive sentiment; Japan gained 3.4% and the Hong Kong Hang Seng rose 2.5%. Your BSD Global Tech Hedge Fund surged 3.7% this past week.

U. S. stocks were able to find new data points that helped shrug off the AI bubble discourse of recent weeks, and while some information came from firms in that very sphere, retail and consumer goods contributed to relief heading into the holiday season and early 2026. Dick’s, Kohl’s, Urban Outfitters and Abercrombie & Fitch earnings were a few that buttressed Walmart’s comments from last week on a rosier view of the U.

S. consumer. In business services, Zoom, Intuit, Autodesk, Zscaler and Workday turned in top-line beats and made it clear that they see AI investment and adoption as yielding real results. For equities more directly exposed to AI capex, Dell, Analog Devices, HP quarterly results and comments indicated confidence in backlog growth and future spend.

Aside from earnings color, AI names got a further boost from news that the White House is considering looser restrictions on Nvidia H200 chips to China and a noticeably China-friendly Trump tweet regarding a call with Xi on November 24. One bellwether of America’s changing trade landscape that couldn’t escape recent difficulties and a gloomy outlook was farm equipment maker Deere, which forecasted a $1.2 billion tariffs hit and took price-target cuts from at least four analysts after fourth-quarter results. Health insurers were also volatile this week as President Trump’s purported plan to extend ACA subsidies for two years was hastily withdrawn after an uproar from Congressional Republicans, even as reports emerged that ACA enrollments have fallen off by as much as a third amid surging 2026 premiums.

The week ahead will see the final stretches of economic data before the December FOMC meeting /announcement next week. With American traders coming back to their screens, we should see if this week’s flows get anchored. Expect hesitation until Friday’s PCE release.

For other data points, U. S. and Canadian PMIs will occupy North American traders from Monday to Thursday. Of course, don’t forget the monthly ADP private jobs release on Tuesday, University of Michigan consumer sentiment and Canadian employment data releasing Friday morning.

Elsewhere, Bank of Japan Ueda will appear at a business leaders conference in Nagoya Sunday evening, with comments regarding his recent meeting with PM Takaichi and the blushing Japanese CPI. Inflation data throughout the European continent will be sure to impact the currency markets. A lot of growth data will also be released for the Eurozone, with their PPI, retail sales and GDP going back-to-back-to-back towards the end of the week.
