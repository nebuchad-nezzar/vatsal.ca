U.S. equities staged a strong rebound on Monday, with the S&P 500 rising 1.5% to 7,764.70 — pulling within 0.4% of its record close — the Dow Jones Industrial Average advancing 0.7% to 52,048.83, and the Nasdaq Composite climbing 2.3% to 27,122.09, a new record. The move was driven by a sharp decline in oil prices, an easing in Treasury yields, and a renewed bid for technology and AI-related equities following last week’s post-Fed volatility.

The 10-year Treasury yield retreated to approximately 4.95–4.96%, moving further below the 5.04% multi-year high reached last week. The decline in yields reflected reduced near-term inflation pressure from lower energy prices and a modest improvement in risk sentiment. Markets continue to price a meaningful probability of further Federal Reserve tightening, with odds of another 25-basis-point increase at the October meeting remaining elevated following last week’s unanimous hike to 3.75%–4.00% and Chair Warsh’s hawkish guidance.

Crude prices extended their decline. Brent fell toward the $100–101 area and WTI moved below $98–$100, marking multi-day lows. The pullback was supported by reports of improved tanker transit under U.S. naval protection in the Strait of Hormuz, signals that Saudi Arabia may partially restore flows through the East-West pipeline, and diplomatic developments surrounding the United Nations General Assembly. President Trump indicated openness to a meeting with Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian on the sidelines of the UN gathering. Houthi threats to Saudi Red Sea export routes persisted, however, and the broader conflict remains unresolved. The reduction in the energy risk premium contributed directly to the easing in bond yields and the risk-on tone across equities.

Technology and communication services led the advance. AMD rose sharply and briefly reached a $1 trillion market capitalization, reflecting continued strength in AI-related semiconductor demand. Meta Platforms advanced strongly after a price-target increase and positive commentary around its AI initiatives. Intel, Micron, and broader semiconductor names participated in the rebound, extending the recovery that began late last week after the prior selling related to AI-safety concerns. Server and infrastructure-related names also found support. Energy was the clear laggard as crude declined.

Cross-asset moves were consistent with improved risk appetite. Global equities rose in sympathy, volatility moderated, and the dollar eased modestly from recent highs. Attention is now focused on the Trump-Xi meeting later this week, which is expected to cover trade and AI-related issues, alongside ongoing diplomatic activity at the UN General Assembly.

The session marked a clear reversal of last week’s pressure, which had been driven by the Fed’s first rate hike in three years, a 10-year yield above 5%, and elevated oil prices. While the rebound is constructive, the fundamental constraints remain in place: policy is in tightening mode, long-term yields are still near multi-year highs, housing affordability continues to be challenged by elevated mortgage rates, and geopolitical risk around energy supply has only partially eased. Selective exposure to companies with durable free-cash-flow generation, pricing power, and clear exposure to structural demand in semiconductors and AI infrastructure remains preferred. Concentration risk and a higher cost of capital continue to warrant disciplined positioning.

Attention turns to the Trump-Xi meeting, residual data, and further developments in the Middle East and the path of yields.
