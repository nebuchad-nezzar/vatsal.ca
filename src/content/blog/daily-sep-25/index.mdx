Wall Street closed out a genuinely volatile week on a high note, and the way it did it says a lot about where sentiment actually sits beneath the surface. The Dow jumped 478 points, or 0.93%, to 51,828.62, snapping a three-week losing streak, while the S&P 500 added 0.51% to 7,743.41 and the Nasdaq gained roughly 0.5% to 27,068.72. All three indices notched weekly wins despite a week that included a near-panic bond selloff and a 5-year yield hitting levels not seen since 2007. That's the story of this market right now ?" real stress under the hood, but risk appetite keeps finding a reason to buy the dip.

The bond market hasn't actually calmed down, even though equities are behaving as if it has. The 10-year Treasury yield pushed to 5.18% today, its highest level since the global financial crisis, and traders still can't agree on why ?" stubborn inflation, surprisingly strong growth, or the exploding deficit are all plausible explanations, and honestly it's probably some combination of all three. What matters for positioning is that "higher for longer" has stopped being a hypothesis and started being consensus, and equities rallying into that kind of move is worth watching closely rather than taking as an all-clear signal.

Oil did a lot of the heavy lifting for today's rally. WTI crude dropped 2.33% to $92.41 and Brent fell 2.14% to $104.32 on reports that Iran has asked the U.S. to return to the June memorandum of understanding that had previously failed to end the Middle East conflict ?" genuine optimism, even if tentative, that the Strait of Hormuz standoff could de-escalate. That single headline did more to lift the tape today than anything coming out of earnings or Fed-speak, which tells you how much of this market's recent stress has been oil-and-geopolitics-driven rather than purely rates-driven.

On rare earths, there's nothing new to report today specifically, but it's worth keeping yesterday's development front of mind: the U.S.-China trade truce extension to January 10 is what's currently keeping Beijing's export controls on critical minerals suspended. No fresh headlines on that front today is itself the point ?" a quiet rare earth market is a good outcome after a week this jumpy elsewhere, and it's a direct downstream benefit of how the Trump-Xi summit landed.

Speaking of which, today marked the final day of Xi Jinping's U.S. state visit ?" Trump and Xi were set to have tea together and visit the National Archives before Xi departs, capping a visit that included a Thursday state dinner attended by Nvidia's Jensen Huang and Tesla's Elon Musk, among other top tech executives. The tone today was notably ceremonial rather than substantive, suggesting both sides are content to let the truce extension stand as the visit's headline deliverable. No new surprises, which in this market counts as a mild positive.

Tech had a genuinely good day, led by a name that doesn't usually make these letters: Akamai jumped 3% after announcing a multiyear deal with Anthropic ?" a reminder that the AI infrastructure trade is broadening out beyond the usual mega-cap names into the picks-and-shovels layer underneath them. Meta was the standout large-cap mover, up 4.5% on the day and roughly 13% for the week, as enthusiasm around its new AI agent, Muse, continues to build. Alphabet added another 1.3%, extending this week's rebound. On the softer side, Nvidia, Microsoft, and Tesla were all modestly lower, which fits the pattern we've been tracking all week ?" capital rotating toward AI monetization and distribution stories over the infrastructure-heavy names still absorbing execution risk and financing costs.

A few other movements worth flagging that didn't make the headlines but are worth having on your radar: the VIX actually fell today, down to the 15 handle after spiking earlier in the week ?" a signal that fear is unwinding faster than the underlying risks are resolving, which is a gap worth watching rather than trusting outright. Gold edged up about 0.5% to roughly $4,318, a modest safe-haven bid even as equities rallied, which is a bit of a tell that not everyone is fully convinced by today's risk-on tone. Bitcoin was essentially flat to slightly lower, continuing to sit out both the bond stress and the oil-driven relief rally ?" crypto remains oddly disconnected from the macro story right now. And under the surface, market breadth was mixed rather than broad ?" this was very much a large-cap, AI-adjacent rally rather than a genuine risk-on day across the board.

Putting it all together, this was a week where the bond market screamed "something's changing" and equities largely shrugged, closing higher across the board. That's not irrational ?" oil cooling off and the U.S.-China truce holding are both genuinely constructive developments ?" but a 5.18% 10-year and a market treating that as background noise is the kind of disconnect that tends to resolve itself eventually, usually not gently. My positioning bias: stay constructive on AI names with real monetization and now real infrastructure deal flow (Meta, Alphabet, Akamai) while keeping duration exposure tightly managed ?" this is not the market to be reaching for long-duration risk in. Gold's quiet bid and Bitcoin's non-participation are both small tells that conviction behind this rally is thinner than the index-level numbers suggest.

Micron reports fiscal Q4 earnings on Monday, September 29 ?" the first real read on AI-memory demand since the summer and a likely catalyst for the whole chip complex. Watch for any follow-through on the Iran/Strait of Hormuz de-escalation headlines ?" a real diplomatic breakthrough would extend today's oil relief, while a stall would send crude right back toward the highs. Keep an eye on the 10-year ?" a push meaningfully past 5.2% would likely force equities to finally acknowledge the rate story rather than shrug it off. And with Xi's visit concluded, watch for any follow-up detail on the trade truce terms, particularly anything specific on rare earths and AI, since Thursday's readout was light on specifics beyond the truce extension itself.
