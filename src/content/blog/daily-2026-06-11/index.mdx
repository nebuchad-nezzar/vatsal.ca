June 11, 2026

A couple of interesting notes out of Asia. The Kospi did manage to finish a bit higher last night and chips continue to support the entire economy it seems. South Korea’s semiconductor exports were up over 200% on a YoY basis for the first 10 days of June.

Elsewhere things are less stellar with respect to Korea’s labour market where jobs contracted by 40k YoY the first employment decline in almost a year and a half. The drop is being blamed on Middle East uncertainty with the manufacturing sector shedding 140,000 jobs. It is definitely becoming a case of haves (tech workers who have received huge bonuses lately) versus the have nots outside of the tech sector.

Elsewhere, the FT reports that China has canceled high level meetings with the EU that were due to take place later this month. The scrapped talks were to include dialogue on digital issues and little reason was given for dropping them. European bourses closed modestly higher today.

Stock markets rallied in Canada and the U. S. after U. S.

President Donald Trump called off a threat to bomb Iran, raising hopes for a deal between the countries. Stocks immediately veered higher in midday trading after Trump said on his social media network that “discussions with the Islamic Republic of Iran have been brought to the highest level of Iranian leadership and approved” and that the time and place of a signing will “be announced shortly.” Meanwhile, big swings for AI stocks have been yanking the U. S. stock market up and down over the last week, as they went from roaring to records to suddenly turning lower.

The big concern is whether such stocks shot too high, too fast because of AI mania, and their careening moves have sometimes reversed direction hourly. AI stocks had already begun rolling back up their roller-coaster early Thursday, before Trump made his announcement on Iran.
