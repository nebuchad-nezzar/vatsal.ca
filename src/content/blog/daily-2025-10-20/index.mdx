October 20, 2025

Things kicked off on a positive note in Asia starting in Japan where Sanae Takaichi looks set to become the first female prime minister after the ruling Liberal Democratic Party agreed to a coalition deal with the Japan Innovation Party. The Nikkei jumped 3.3% to close at a fresh record high of 49,185 after the coalition announcement. We had the monthly data dump out of China and the numbers were better than expected across the board (with the exception of real estate investment).

GDP (4.8% YoY), industrial production (+6.5%) and retail sales all topped expectations. The lone negative remains the real estate sector with YTD property investment down -13.9%. The data no doubt boosted sentiment but stocks got an added lift after reports that China had replaced their chief trade negotiator Li Chenggang.

This follows criticism from U.S. Treasury Secretary Bessent that the negotiator had “gone rogue” last week.

Over the weekend Bessent had a call with Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng that is being viewed as something of a re-set between U.S.-China relations. The PBOC left rates unchanged as expected while China’s Fourth Plenum is underway until Thursday.

It is doubtful we’ll get any concrete stimulus plans from this week’s meetings in China but of course we’ll watch for headlines regarding any shift in policy. European bourses were mostly higher today. France was downgraded to A+ by S&P but the move was widely expected.

Offsetting that, Italy was upgraded by DBRS based on a reduction in vulnerabilities in its banking sector and a more stable public debt ratio. Geopolitics perhaps surprisingly, has not weighed on sentiment. President Trump sounded much more Russia-friendly, reportedly telling Zelensky he was losing the war and warning that if Putin wanted it, “he would destroy you”.

Trump also said that Russia’s economy is doing great contradicting some other recent comments. Trump is reportedly pressuring Zelensky to give up large chunks of territory to Russia while Polish PM Tusk posted on social media that “none of us should put pressure on Zelensky when it comes to territorial concessions”. Following the upward move seen over the course of last Friday’s session, North American stock markets showed a more significant move to the upside during trading on Monday.

The major averages all moved sharply higher, climbing back within striking distance of their record highs. Apple helped to lead the markets higher, with the tech giant surging by 3.9 percent to a new record closing high. The jump by Apple came after Loop Capital upgraded its rating on the company’s stock to Buy from Hold due to strong demand for the iPhone 17 series.

Positive sentiment may also have been generated in reaction to comments from National Economic Council Director Kevin Hassett, who suggested the ongoing government shutdown could end this week. Hassett told CNBC’s “Squawk Box” he expects moderate Democrats to cross the aisle to end the government shutdown, which has entered day 20 today. If Democrats continue to refuse to approve a funding bill unless it includes an extension of enhanced Obamacare tax credits, Hassett indicated the White House could take “stronger measures” in order to bring them to the table.

The rally also came after a report from the Wall Street Journal said the Trump administration is quietly watering down some of the tariffs that underpin the president’s signature economic policy. The Wall Street Journal said President Donald Trump has exempted dozens of products from his “reciprocal tariffs” in recent weeks and offered to carve out hundreds more goods when countries strike trade deals with the U.S.

Steel stocks showed a substantial move to the upside on the day, driving the NYSE Arca Steel Index up by 3.5 percent. Cleveland-Cliffs led the sector higher, skyrocketing by 21.5 percent after revealing plans to look into building a rare earths mining business. A significant rebound by the price of gold also contributed to significant strength among gold stocks, as reflected by the 3.0 percent surge by the NYSE Arca Gold Bugs Index.

In the bond market, treasuries climbed into positive territory over the course of a relatively lackluster session. Subsequently, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, fell by 2.1 basis points to 3.986 percent. Amid another quiet day on the U.S. economic front, trading on Tuesday may be impacted by reaction to the latest corporate earnings news. 3M, Coca-Cola and General Motors are among some of the big-name companies due to report their quarterly results before the start of trading on Tuesday.
