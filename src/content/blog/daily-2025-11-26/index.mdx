November 26, 2025

Overnight, Asian indices were mostly higher led by healthy gains in the Kospi (+2.6%) and Nikkei (+1.8%) with sentiment getting a boost after SoftBank managed to turnaround and trade higher following a string of recent losses. The RBNZ lowered rates by 25bps as expected and signaled they were likely done with cuts while the Oz is better bid after hotter than expected inflation data. NZD and AUD are today’s top G10 FX performers.

Stocks in mainland China underperformed over concerns about China’s  property market (again) after China’s Vanke (large residential real estate developer in China) had its bonds suspended from trading following a 20% decline (the company had previously been considered a “safe” property market bet). Meanwhile, China and Japan continue their diplomatic spat with reports that Chinese officials have asked airlines to extend flight cuts to Japan until March with added plans to extend a probe into Japanese imported beef into the new year. Finally, the PBOC fixed the Yuan at 7.0796, the strongest level since October 2024.

European bourses joined in the positive sentiment with the focus turning to Russia now that the U.S. and Ukraine seem to have come to an agreement on a peace plan. President Trump has said “tremendous progress” has been made but there remains a few sticking points including just how much land Ukraine will have to give up, that seems to be a pretty big point.

U.S. negotiators are set to head to Russia to meet with Putin but in the meantime, Russia’s Deputy Foreign Minister Ryabkov said that there are no concessions from Russia on key issues on Ukraine settlement. UK Chancellor Reeves presented the UK budget this morning and so far so good, no collapse of the UK bond market or in Sterling.

Many of the details were leaked ahead of time (a lesson learned after the 2022 budget debacle) so perhaps not as many surprises for the market. North American stock markets moved mostly higher over the course of the trading session on Wednesday, extending the upward trend seen over the past few sessions. The major averages moved to the upside in morning trading and remained firmly positive throughout the afternoon.

Stocks continued to benefit from recent upward momentum, which has helped the major averages rebound strongly from the significant pullback seen earlier in the month. Traders seem to have shrugged off the valuation concerns that recently weighed on the markets and dragged the Nasdaq and the S&P 500 down to their lowest levels in over two months. The markets have recently benefited from renewed optimism about the outlook for interest rates following dovish comments from some Federal Reserve officials.

CME Group’s FedWatch Tool indicates the chances the Fed will lower rates by another quarter point next month have soared to 82.9 percent from just 30.1 percent a week ago. According to the FedWatch Tool, the chances of a rate cut are little changed from yesterday despite the release of some upbeat U.S. economic data.

The Commerce Department released a long-delayed report this morning showing new orders for U.S. manufactured durable goods increased by more than expected in the month of September. The report said durable goods orders climbed by 0.5 percent in September after spiking by an upwardly revised 3.0 percent in August.

Economists had expected durable goods orders to rise by 0.3 percent compared to the 2.9 percent surge that had been reported for the previous month. A separate report released by the Labor Department showed an unexpected dip by first-time claims for U.S. unemployment benefits in the week ended November 22nd.

The Labor Department said initial jobless claims slipped to 216,000, a decrease of 6,000 from the previous week’s revised level of 222,000. Economists had expected jobless claims to inch up to 225,000 from the 220,000 originally reported for the previous week. With the unexpected dip, jobless claims fell to their lowest level since hitting a matching number in the week ended April 12th.

In the bond market, treasuries recovered from an early pullback to end the day roughly flat. Subsequently, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, edged down by less than a basis point to 3.998 percent, closing below 4 percent for the first time since late October. Following the Thanksgiving Day holiday on Thursday, trading activity may be somewhat subdued amid an abbreviated trading session on Friday.
