June 12, 2026

Trading in technology shares and optimism around Iran continued driving trading this past week. Tech shares rebounded to open the week, led by a significant bounce in semis and other AI-infrastructure names. Tech investors navigated disappointing earning reactions to Oracle and Adobe results, as well as a WSJ report indicating OpenAI is considering substantially cutting token pricing.

Also, SpaceX’s looming IPO continued to suck some oxygen out of the room, resulting in ongoing volatility amid speculation investors were raising funds to buy shares in the historic ~$2T IPO. The U.S. and Iran continued to trade both missiles and barbs as they inched toward an MOU that is expected to extend the cease fire for a 60-day period of consultations on a final agreement.

President Trump once again pulled the escalate-to-deescalate lever by ratcheting up attacks and threatening to take Kharg Island, before pulling that back and claiming a deal was finally in the offing. Oil prices moved lower throughout the week, mostly ignoring the ebbs and flows of rhetoric and military engagement around the Strait. By Friday, both sides appeared to be moving towards signing an agreement, perhaps as soon as this coming week.

Brent and WTI slipped to levels not seen since early March, while Treasury yields moved lower. For the week, the S&P, DJIA and Nasdaq each gained about 0.7%. European bourses were mixed with the German DAX losing 0.50% and the French CAC40 gaining 1.61%.

Asia Pacific stock markets were once again bifurcated, with Japan retreating 0.85% and the mainland China gaining 0.10%. Your BSD Global Tech Hedge Fund slipped 0.4% this past week. The ECB raised rates, as widely expected, but seemed to leave the door open to holding at the next meeting in July to allow things to play out in the Middle East.

UK GDP came in notably weak, denting BoE rate hike expectations while PM Starmer continued to face fresh turmoil regarding his leadership of the Labour Party. U.S.

May CPI data was largely in line with market expectations, allaying fears of an aggressive break higher. PPI data was not nearly as sanguine, but U.S.

Treasury yields tracked lower nevertheless, following oil lower. Existing home sales provided a glimmer of hope for the housing industry with May first time buyers rising to levels not seen since the middle of 2020. Corporate news continued to revolve around the AI-tech sphere, though some other notable stories emerged in the physical world.

Amazon sent ripples through the freight logistics sector (at least for one morning) as it announced expansion of U.S. less-than-truckload freight to any destination, including third party warehouses. At a conference, Walmart noted consumer spending is still “relatively healthy”, as the retailer saw its best traffic growth in six quarters.

Oracle and Adobe each reported lackluster quarterly results and guidance, leading shares to sell off. In the battle for AI dominance, Anthropic launched Claude Fable 5, a ‘Mythos-lite’ version that is meant to suppress the cyber threat potential for public users. Meanwhile OpenAI was said to be plotting its biggest ChatGPT overhaul since launch even as it submitted a confidential draft S-1 filing to the SEC ahead of its impending IPO.

SpaceX’s highly anticipated IPO went off without a hitch, opening about 11% above pricing, and minting Elon Musk as the first and only member of the ‘4-comma’ club. Other space-related stocks that had run up in anticipation of the record IPO saw big selloffs on Friday, led by Virgin Galactic with a 30% one-day loss. Investors head into a holiday-shortened week focused on the Federal Reserve’s June meeting, fresh consumer spending data, and developments from the G7 summit in France.

U.S. markets will be closed Friday for the Juneteenth holiday. The main event arrives Wednesday when the Fed announces its interest rate decision, followed by new Chairman Kevin Warsh’s first press conference.

Investors will be watching closely for clues on how Warsh plans to navigate persistent inflation pressures and whether he signals a more dovish approach than his predecessor. Your BSD team expects Warsh to argue that recent supply shocks are temporary and that the Fed should remain focused on longer-term disinflation trends. U.S. economic data will also be in focus. The May retail sales report is due Wednesday, while housing starts and the Philadelphia Fed manufacturing survey will provide additional readings on economic momentum. On the geopolitical front, leaders gathering at the G7 summit in Évian-les-Bains will be watched for any progress on a potential agreement between the U.S. and Iran, as energy markets remain highly sensitive to developments. Corporate highlights include HPE Discover, AWS Summit New York, and earnings from Accenture, Kroger, CarMax, and Jabil. Markets could also see heightened volatility on Thursday as triple witching coincides with major index rebalancing activity.

Elsewhere, Japan’s central bank is expected to raise its policy rate by 25 basis points. Eurozone April trade balance and industrial production data will also be published.
