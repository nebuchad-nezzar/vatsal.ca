August 17, 2025

U.S. stock markets continued to probe new all-time highs this week amid a firming consensus that the Fed will start lowering rates next month. July CPI and PPI data offered decidedly different pictures on how tariffs are working their way into the inflation picture, but in aggregate left the door open for the Fed to consider cutting interest rates should the jobs market show continued slowing.

Animal spirits remained a broad tailwind on the heels of several M&A announcements, and palpable hype surrounding the crypto-related IPO Bullish, which opened for trade on NYSE. Separately, China activity data broadly deteriorated last month with industrial production, fixed asset investment, and retail sales growth all notably below consensus, leading to another bout of speculation that the PBOC will ride to the rescue with a new round of stimulus measures. Also, as largely expected, Trump signed an executive order extending the China tariff deadline by another 90 days to early November.

Trump also set high expectations for a Ukraine peace agreement in the near future as he met with Putin in Alaska on Friday. The U.S.  yield curve steepened and stock futures firmed after the July CPI release showed tariff-related pressures did not continue to grow in July.

Core goods prices rose by 0.2% for the month, which is somewhat higher than the year-to-date average of 0.1% but not significantly worse than June. Core service prices did rise more than expected, largely due to increases in volatile categories like airfares and financial services that may not carry over to the PCE data. That was followed by a hotter than expected PPI print where food and energy prices were both much stronger than expected.

Importantly, the PPI components that feed into the core PCE price index were also strong, but like CPI driven by historically volatile categories (airfares and financial services) making it unlikely the m/m increases in July will turn into a stronger underlying trend. Following the PPI, UST yields ticked up, led by the short end, as Treasury Secretary Bessent’s call for a 50 basis point cut didn’t age particularly well in light of the data. Nevertheless, futures markets were pricing in a 25 bps cut in September heading into Powell’s Jackson Hole presentation this coming week.

Small caps and the Russell played some catch up this week relative to large caps on the hopes for lower rates. Bitcoin made new all-time highs while the U.S. dollar moved lower.

For the week, the S&P gained 0.9%, the DJIA was up 1.7%, and the Nasdaq added 0.8%. European bourses participated in the stock market rally advancing to varying degrees – the French CAC40 surged 2.3%, and the German DAX rose 0.8%. Asia Pacific stock markets also gained this past week; Hong Kong rose 3.6% and Japan advanced 1.7%.

Your BSD Global Tech Hedge Fund retreated 0.6% this past week. Nvidia’s impressive rally was blunted this week as reports emerged that advanced U.S. semiconductor makers would be subject to a 15% levy on chip sales in China, even as Beijing was urging domestic firms to abstain from using less advanced chips such as Nividia’s H20.

AMD was subject to the same stresses, but shares still saw gains after the company confirmed it just got its China export license from the U.S. government. Meanwhile, memory and storage specialist Micron raised revenue guidance, citing improved pricing and execution.

Intel shares soared on reports the Trump Administration was pondering using money from the Chips Act to fund taking a federal stake in the CPU maker. Google drew some interest on reports that AI startup Perplexity had floated a $34.5B offer for the Chrome browser, and that OpenAI founder Sam Altman is also interested in the asset. CoreWeave took a hit on a mixed earnings report and as its IPO lockup expired late in the week.

Outside of the tech sector, Deere beat Q3 numbers but issued cautious guidance, sending shares lower. Chemical and specialty materials firm Celanese was among the latest firms to give weak guidance based on global uncertainty and a weakening order book in China. Shares of UnitedHealth showed a pulse for the first time in a long time after Berkshire Hathaway disclosed a new stake in the beleaguered insurance firm, as well as stakes in homebuilders DR Horton and Lennar as Fed rate cuts loom on the horizon.

Investors’ focus this week will be on the annual Jackson Hole Economic Policy Symposium to be held from August 21 to 23. Market participants will also gear up for a deluge of quarterly results from retail companies. Meanwhile, Russia and Ukraine developments will continue to grab eyeballs, with the latter nation’s President Volodymyr Zelenskyy set to meet U.S. President Donald Trump on Monday. The Jackson Hole gathering will see some of the world’s most well-known central bankers, economists, and financial experts convene in Kansas City.

The theme this year is “Labor Markets in Transition: Demographics, Productivity, and Macroeconomic Policy.” With the Federal Reserve’s dual mandate of price stability and maximum employment coming under pressure after concerning jobs and inflation data this month, market participants will be watching the symposium keenly for any clues on the central bank’s future actions. One of the most memorable market-shaking moments from the yearly conference came in 2010, when then-Fed Chair Ben Bernanke used his Jackson Hole speech to signal a second round of quantitative easing (QE2). The hint sent risk assets surging and Treasury yields lower, cementing Jackson Hole’s reputation as a stage for major policy signals.

Turning to the earnings calendar, retail giants Walmart and Home Depot will headline the week, along with smaller rivals Target and Lowe’s. In terms of U.S. economic data, investors will be receiving the minutes of the Fed’s July monetary policy committee meeting on Wednesday, followed by S&P flash PMIs on Thursday.

Elsewhere, on Tuesday Canadian inflation will be announced. Markets will also see if the UK inflation gets a boost, which may confirm further the doubts of prolonged rate cuts from the Bank of England. In Europe, both the UK and Germany will release their PPI data on Wednesday, where we will see how producer prices moved on the other side of the Atlantic.

They might be movers for European currencies after this week’s market shaking U.S. PPI. See you next week!
