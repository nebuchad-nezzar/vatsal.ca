January 20, 2026

Asian equity markets finished the session lower across the board last night. Geopolitics no doubt weighed on sentiment but so did JGB yields which skyrocketed after a poorly received 20 year auction. The long end of the Japanese yield curve steepened sharply with 20, 30 and 40year yields up around 20-25bps which in turn pushed yields higher everywhere.

PM Takaichi has promised to suspend sales tax on food for two years, end what she calls Japan’s “excessively tight” fiscal policy while somehow lowering Japan’s debt to GDP. Elsewhere China kept its one- and five-year LPR rates unchanged as expected (despite non-existent inflation). According to Chinese press reports, China favors targeted structural cuts in interest rates and the RRR and the PBoC may leave the LPRs unchanged to protect narrowing net interest margins at commercial banks.

European bourses all closed in the red today. No surprise when the U. S. economy contracted.

By pushing to take Greenland one way or another, the existence of NATO as we know it is being threatened, and the U. S. is involved. S. is slapping tariffs on selected European countries and the EU is reportedly considering using their “trade bazooka” the Anti-Coercion Instrument in response to the recent moves by the Trump administration.

U. S. Treasury Secretary Bessent has told European leaders to “sit back and take a deep breath” over the Greenland tariff threats.

After ending last Friday’s choppy trading session modestly lower, North American stock markets moved substantially lower during Tuesday’s trading. The major averages all moved sharply lower, adding to last week’s losses. The sell-off came amid renewed concerns about a trade war between the U.

S. and Europe over President Donald Trump’s efforts to take control of Greenland. Trump has threatened to impose new tariffs on several European nations if they oppose his attempt to purchase the Danish territory, which he claims is imperative for national security. In a post on Truth Social, Trump announced plans to impose a 10 percent tariff on imports from Denmark, Norway, Sweden, France, Germany, the U.

K., the Netherlands and Finland beginning February 1st. Trump said the tariffs would be increased to 25 percent beginning June 1st and would remain in place until a deal is reached for the U. S. to purchase Greenland.

Housing stocks turned in some of the market’s worst performances on the day, dragging the Philadelphia Housing Sector Index down by 2.5 percent. Significant weakness was also visible among airline stocks, with the NYSE Arca Airline Index tumbling by 2.4 percent. In the bond market, treasuries extended the steep drop seen during last Friday’s session. As a result, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, jumped 6.4 basis points to a five-month closing high of 4.295 percent.
