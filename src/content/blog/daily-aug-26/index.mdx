U.S. equities drifted through a quiet session on Wednesday, ending nearly flat as investors digested a slightly firmer inflation reading and positioned ahead of Nvidia’s highly anticipated results after the close. The S&P 500 edged down less than 0.1% to 7,675.70, the Dow Jones Industrial Average declined 0.2% to 53,463.88, and the Nasdaq Composite slipped 0.1% to 26,130.20. Trading volumes were subdued, reflecting a market in wait-and-see mode before the week’s most important corporate catalyst.

The week had begun on a more unsettled note. On Monday, technology stocks led the market lower, with the Nasdaq falling 0.8% and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropping more than 2%. Nvidia, Micron, and Broadcom were among the notable decliners as caution built ahead of the mid-week earnings. High-beta AI and semiconductor names faced particular pressure following reports that Nvidia had informed major customers of planned price increases exceeding 15% on certain AI servers next year, reflecting ongoing memory cost pressures and capacity constraints. At the same time, U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent outlined plans for intensified economic pressure on Iran—describing the approach as an “economic D-Day”—and signaled potential secondary sanctions on trading partners. Separately, reports indicated the Treasury could draw on its General Account to help fund expanded longer-dated bond buybacks, following the prior week’s surprise increase in the size of those operations. These institutional moves helped ease some pressure on long-end yields later in the week but initially contributed to a cautious tone across risk assets.

By Wednesday, sector performance was mixed. Industrials led the gains, while Health Care lagged amid profit-taking in names that had run sharply higher in recent sessions. Technology was relatively resilient during the regular session but remained the clear focal point given the looming Nvidia report. Broader risk appetite stayed measured as Treasury yields ticked higher following the inflation update, which kept the possibility of further Federal Reserve tightening later in the year in play.

The clear highlight of the day—and the week—came after the closing bell. Nvidia delivered another blockbuster set of results that reinforced the durability of AI infrastructure demand. Revenue more than doubled year-over-year to $96.2 billion, comfortably ahead of expectations, while profit also doubled. Data-center revenue reached $89 billion. Management guided for approximately $108 billion in revenue for the current quarter and projected roughly 70% growth into fiscal 2028—well above consensus. CEO Jensen Huang described AI as having reached an “inflection point,” with demand continuing to accelerate. Shares rose more than 4% in after-hours trading after an initial dip, lifting related semiconductor and AI names and providing a strong counterpoint to the more cautious tone that had prevailed earlier in the week.

Oil prices extended recent declines, with Brent settling near $87.84 and WTI around $82.23. The softer tone reflected growing hopes of progress on the Strait of Hormuz, including reports of talks between Iran and Oman on a temporary joint maritime corridor and potential revenue-sharing arrangements. While traffic through the strait remains well below pre-conflict levels, the market is beginning to price a lower probability of prolonged full disruption. This has helped ease some of the geopolitical risk premium that had supported energy prices in recent weeks.

Overall, the session underscored a market that remains constructive on the structural AI theme but selective in the near term. Nvidia’s results provide important confirmation that hyperscaler and enterprise demand remains robust, even as valuation sensitivity, profit-taking, and institutional developments around yields and geopolitics continue to shape day-to-day price action across the semiconductor complex. Elevated long-term yields and residual geopolitical uncertainty around energy supply continue to act as moderating forces.

We maintain a constructive longer-term view on technology and AI-related equities, supported by strong fundamental demand for advanced semiconductors and data-center infrastructure. Concentration risk and elevated valuations warrant discipline. Our preference remains selective exposure to quality companies with clear visibility into AI-driven revenue growth, genuine pricing power, and strong free-cash-flow generation.

Nvidia’s results will set the tone for residual technology earnings in the days ahead. We will continue to monitor developments around the Strait of Hormuz, the path of bond yields and energy prices, and incoming inflation data, and will keep you updated as conditions evolve.
