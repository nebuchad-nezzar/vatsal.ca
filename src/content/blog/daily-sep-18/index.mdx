U.S. equities closed mixed on Friday as markets digested the Federal Reserve’s first rate increase in three years, a rebound in Treasury yields toward 5%, and ongoing volatility in energy prices. The S&P 500 finished roughly flat to modestly lower, the Dow Jones Industrial Average underperformed, and the Nasdaq Composite held relatively better supported by selective technology strength. The major averages remained on track for weekly declines, with the Dow facing its largest weekly percentage drop since March.

The 10-year Treasury yield moved back toward the 5.00% level after retreating below it on Thursday. The firming in yields reflected residual inflation concerns linked to elevated energy prices and rising odds of further policy tightening. Markets are currently pricing approximately a 50–58% probability of another 25-basis-point increase at the October FOMC meeting, up sharply from levels seen a month earlier. The Fed’s Wednesday decision to raise the funds rate to 3.75%–4.00%, accompanied by projections for additional tightening and Chair Warsh’s emphasis on persistent inflation, continues to anchor the front end of the curve.

Crude prices remained volatile. Brent traded near $104 after swinging earlier in the session, extending a three-day period of consolidation following the prior week’s surge above $108–110. Diesel prices stayed near record highs, underscoring refined-product tightness even as the prompt crude complex eased from peak levels. Geopolitical developments remained the dominant driver of the energy complex. Iranian forces were reported to have struck a Togo-flagged oil tanker attempting to transit the Strait of Hormuz. Houthi-Saudi exchanges continued along the border and Red Sea corridor, with Saudi airstrikes and Houthi drone/missile activity persisting. Reports indicated that China, at Saudi Arabia’s request, had urged Iran to restrain Houthi attacks on Saudi oil infrastructure — a diplomatic signal that contributed to the modest cooling in the oil risk premium. Shipping volumes through Hormuz remained severely constrained, and the East-West pipeline disruption continues to limit Saudi flexibility in redirecting crude away from the Gulf.

In equities, sector performance was uneven. Technology showed relative resilience after Thursday’s sharp recovery in semiconductors, though gains were more selective. Netflix declined approximately 5% following a Wells Fargo downgrade citing softening viewership and a less compelling content pipeline. Crypto-related names, including Coinbase, advanced as investors looked past a legislative setback on digital-asset clarity legislation. The broader semiconductor complex consolidated after the prior session’s strong rebound led by Intel, AMD, Nvidia, and Marvell.

Cross-asset moves reflected the dual influence of monetary policy and energy geopolitics. The dollar remained firm, consistent with the higher-for-longer rates narrative. Volatility moderated from mid-week peaks but stayed elevated relative to the summer range. The Bank of Japan raised its policy rate by 25 basis points to 1.25%, the highest level since 1995, citing energy-driven inflation and yen weakness — adding to the global tightening impulse.

The week leaves the market in a more constrained position. Policy has shifted into tightening mode, the 10-year yield is again testing the 5% threshold, housing affordability remains under pressure from elevated mortgage rates, and geopolitical risk around two critical energy chokepoints (Hormuz and the Red Sea/Bab el-Mandeb) has not been resolved. Selective exposure to companies with durable free-cash-flow generation, pricing power, and visible demand in semiconductors and AI infrastructure remains preferred. Concentration risk, elevated valuations, and a higher cost of capital continue to warrant disciplined positioning.
