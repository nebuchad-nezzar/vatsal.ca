March 10, 2026

Whatever the reasoning behind it, the markets were happy to hear that the war might end soon and that there might be an emergency release of strategic oil reserves. Asian equity markets were up across the board last night and let’s face it, when you are an energy importer, crude at $88 a barrel isn’t great, but it’s a lot better than $120 a barrel. Japanese GDP was also a bit better than expected and it looks like U.

S. tariffs have just shifted Chinese export destinations. Trade data released last night for the January–February period showed U. S. exports down 17% but China seems to be doing just fine with exports overall up nearly 40% YoY.

The year-to-date trade balance came in at $213bio versus the expected $176 billion. European bourses were all higher today as energy fears receded (at least for now). Reports say that there have been few Iranian missile launches and that attacks on Middle East neighbours have also been halted.

European natural gas prices remain elevated but have dropped about 13% from their highs, which has helped lift sentiment today. North American equity markets were mixed on Tuesday; the TSX logged a modest gain while U. S. markets were little changed.

Oil prices stabilized around $87 per barrel after briefly surging above $100 per barrel in yesterday’s session, before retreating following comments from U. S. President Trump indicating that the conflict in Iran could be nearing an end.

In fixed-income markets, Canadian government bond yields were lower; the 10-year GoC yield closed at 3.38% while the 10-year U. S. Treasury yield ticked higher to 4.15%.
