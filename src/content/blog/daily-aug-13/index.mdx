Global equities extended their advance, with the S&P 500 closing at a fresh record high as moderating U.S. inflation further reduced expectations of an imminent Federal Reserve rate hike. The S&P 500 rose 0.7% to 7,798.99, the Nasdaq Composite gained 0.8% to 26,803.03, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average edged up 0.1% to 53,839.99. European equities remained near record levels, while Asian markets rallied on Thursday, led by strong gains in technology and semiconductor names.

The principal catalyst was the July Producer Price Index, which was unchanged month-over-month against expectations of a 0.2% increase. Goods prices declined 0.7%, driven by a sharp drop in energy, while core PPI rose a modest 0.2%. Combined with Wednesday’s constructive CPI report, the data pushed market-implied odds of a September rate hike into the mid-30% range. The 10-year Treasury yield eased to approximately 4.65%. Crude prices retreated, with Brent falling roughly 1.5–2%, reflecting downward revisions to 2026 global demand forecasts by OPEC and the IEA, even as supply concerns linked to the Strait of Hormuz persist.

Shifting expectations for Fed policy in response to incoming data may contribute to periods of equity volatility, yet we continue to expect the global stock rally to extend. While we see further gains ahead for the S&P 500, we also identify attractive opportunities across European and Asian markets. Elevated concentration risk in U.S. mega-cap technology underscores the importance of diversified stock exposure; regional diversification offers an effective way to participate in a broadening rally.

In Europe, earnings, structural growth trends, and improving cyclical conditions remain supportive. Stoxx Europe 600 companies are on track for the strongest second-quarter profit growth since 2022. The region is benefiting from a more durable investment cycle, with rising spending on defense, infrastructure, AI, automation, electrification, and energy security supporting select industrial, technology, financial, and consumer companies. Although Europe remains more exposed than the U.S. to energy-market disruption, improving business activity, stronger order trends, and Germany’s fiscal impulse should help broaden the recovery. We favor banks, health care, industrials, consumer discretionary, Germany, and companies aligned with our “European Leaders” theme.

Japan’s solid fundamentals point to opportunities. Corporate earnings remain resilient, with second-quarter operating profit growth running above 20% year-over-year and positive earnings surprises supporting the outlook. We believe the market has likely established a cyclical bottom, and the recent valuation reset has created attractive entry points in high-quality companies with durable earnings growth. We favor balanced exposure to AI-related names, including semiconductor equipment, alongside cyclical recovery beneficiaries such as banks and machinery, as well as companies positioned to benefit from rising power demand linked to electrification, digitalization, and AI infrastructure.

Strong earnings underpin our constructive view on Asia ex-Japan. We forecast robust earnings growth this year, supported by the region’s AI hardware supply chain and a recovery in cyclical segments. In China, an improving risk-reward backdrop should support the internet sector, while semiconductor capital equipment continues to benefit from sustained AI investment. We also see opportunities in power and health care, and favor banks, insurers, select utilities, and consumer staples for defensive cash flow and income. Separately, India offers a compelling growth story beyond AI, while “value-up” reforms should continue to support select markets such as Singapore.

In aggregate, broad global earnings strength, an improving cyclical backdrop, and structural growth trends reinforce the case for a diversified equity portfolio. Such exposure should allow investors to participate in the broadening rally while navigating residual uncertainty and potential volatility. Near-term focus remains on residual earnings, further geopolitical developments around the Strait of Hormuz, and the durability of the recent disinflation impulse. We continue to manage risk with a controlled posture, emphasizing quality cash-flow generators and relative-value opportunities.
