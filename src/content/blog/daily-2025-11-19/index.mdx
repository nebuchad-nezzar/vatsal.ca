November 19, 2025

Asian equity markets were mixed overnight but overall moves were much more subdued than we have seen in recent sessions. The back and forth between China and Japan continues with the Chinese government once again banning the import of Japanese seafood (citing the need for monitoring of Fukushima nuclear plant’s treated water release) while maintaining a ban on Japanese beef as well. It will likely take a walk back of Japanese PM Takaichi’s recent comments on Taiwan to thaw relations once again.

China is the second largest export market for Japanese goods so I would guess Japan either looks for a reconciliation, or the size of that Japanese economic stimulus package will have to increase. It is a similar pattern in Europe with equity indices there mixed with much more subdued overall moves. UK and EU inflation numbers were pretty much as expected today, which means one remains quite high (UK of course) while the other is fairly benign.

Somehow, headline CPI at 3.6% and core at 3.4% are good enough for the market to price in a greater chance of a BoE rate cut next month. On a positive note (hopefully), Axios reports today that the Trump administration has been working with Russia to draft a new plan to end the war in Ukraine. Russian envoy Kirill Dmitriev claimed that it is the first time the Russian position is “really being heard”.

That might mean Ukraine would be expected to give up substantial territory to Russia? You have to remain optimistic but the plan has yet to be seen by the Europeans and by Ukraine and we have seen this story play out a number of times previously without much success in ending the conflict. After failing to sustain an early rally, North American stock markets fluctuated over the course of the trading session on Wednesday before eventually ending the day mostly higher.

The major averages all closed in positive territory after spending the day bouncing back and forth across the unchanged line. The early strength came as some traders looked to pick up stocks at relatively reduced levels following the significant weakness seen over the past few sessions. The major averages slumped to their lowest closing levels in a month on Tuesday amid lingering concerns about a potential AI bubble.

Buying interest waned over the course of the session, however, as traders looked ahead to the release of earnings news from market leader and AI darling Nvidia after the close of today’s trading. Ahead of the release of its third quarter results, Nvidia jumped by 2.9 percent after ending the previous session at its lowest closing level in almost a month. Stocks continued to show a lack of direction after the minutes of the Federal Reserve’s latest monetary policy meeting revealed officials had mixed views about the outlook for interest rates.

The minutes of the Fed’s October 28-29 meeting said participants expressed “strongly differing views” about what policy decision would most likely be appropriate at the central bank’s next meeting on December 9-10. While the Fed said most participants agreed that it would be appropriate to eventually lower rates, several indicated that they did not necessarily view another 25 basis point rate cut as likely to be appropriate at the December meeting. Others assessed that a further lowering of rates could be appropriate in December if the economy evolved about as they expected, although “many participants” suggested that it would likely be appropriate to keep rates unchanged for the rest of the year, the Fed said.

In the bond market, treasuries showed a lack of direction before eventually closing slightly lower. As a result, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, inched up by 1.0 basis point to 4.133 percent. Reaction to Nvidia’s earnings news is likely to drive trading on Thursday, although the September jobs report may also attract attention.
