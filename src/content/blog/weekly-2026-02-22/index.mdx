February 22, 2026

Uncertainty remained elevated amid a number of jarring geopolitical, trade and corporate developments this week. Escalating tensions surrounding Iran and looming US military action sent crude oil prices up more than 5%, and President Trump offered up a 10-15 day deadline after talks between the two sides made only limited progress. As more US military assets moved into the Gulf region, reports emerged that Trump might start with targeted strikes to push Tehran toward making a deal.

FOMC minutes affirmed growing division among policy makers heading towards the change in leadership. At a meeting where two dissented in favor of rate cuts, several policy makers indicated a preference for introducing a two-sided description on future rate decisions, reflecting the possibility that hikes could be appropriate if inflation remained above target. Separately, Fed dove Miran told an interviewer he now sees a less accommodative rate path due to employment holding up better than expected and to sticky goods inflation.

The Yen weakened mid-week as PM Takaichi certified her sweeping election victory, but the currency stabilized later in the week as she pledged not to pursue reckless fiscal policy that undermines market confidence. On Friday, President Trump came out swinging after the Supreme Court struck down his authority to use IEEPA to impose tariffs. Trump immediately imposed a new 10% global tariff and said he would use other authorities to keep existing tariffs in place, while any tariff refunds would take years of litigation to decide.

By the end of the week, Treasury yields were modestly higher, the greenback gained some ground, and stocks were back on track. For the holiday-shortened week, the S&P was up 1.1%, the DJIA added 0.3%, and the Nasdaq gained 1.5%. Corporate news continued to revolve around earnings reports and the AI saga. Deere shares broke out, trading up 12% after beating on top line sales growth and raising profitability forecasts. Walmart shares initially turned lower on conservative 2027 EPS guidance, but investors quickly looked past its 47x PE ratio, believing the company remains well positioned to thrive in a K-shaped economy.

Private credit names came under pressure after Blue Owl Capital Corp halted redemptions at a private retail debt fund alongside an announced sale of assets to raise liquidity. Nvidia and Meta both got a little boost as the two giants agreed to a multi-year infrastructure partnership. Meanwhile Google continued to raise its competitive profile across the AI sector as it introduced its latest Gemini Pro (3.1) model and is reportedly looking for ways to expand the market for its TPUs in an increasing threat to Nvidia.

The cybersecurity sector became the latest victim of AI replacement angst after Anthropic announced a preview release of Claude Code Security. In M&A chatter, reports emerged that JNJ is considering the sale of its $20B orthopedics unit, news that gave a lift to orthopedics sector names like Zimmer, Stryker and Medtronic.
