September 17, 2025

Equity indices were mixed in the Asian time zone, South Korea’s Kospi was the underperformer finally having a down day dropping about 1%. You have to put things in perspective though, the index is up about 50% from the bad old Liberation day lows of April. The Hang Seng outperformed climbing 1.8% helped by positive sentiment around the TikTok deal and by AI optimism (we’ve heard that before) with Baidu up about 14% overnight.

The AI optimism was likely fueled by reports that China’s largest chipmaker SMIC is testing a lithography machine which would be China’s first advanced tool for AI chipmaking. After the less than encouraging data dump out of China earlier this week, Chinese authorities outlined some new stimulus measures last night with a focus on boosting consumption in areas like culture and tourism. China’s Premier Li Qiang also pledged more support for increased R&D spending but I think overall the market was less than impressed by the stimulus news.

European bourses closed mixed today in cautious trade ahead of the Fed. Yields were a touch softer after both UK and EU inflation data came in more or less as expected, which is to say relatively benign in the EU but still too hot in the UK. North American stock markets turned in a lackluster performance throughout much of the session on Wednesday before seeing substantial volatility in afternoon trading following the Federal Reserve’s highly anticipated monetary policy announcement.

The late-day volatility came following the Federal Reserve’s widely expected announcement of its decision to lower interest rates by a quarter point. The Fed said it decided to lower the target range for the federal funds rate by 25 basis points to 4.0 percent to 4.25 percent, citing a shift in the balance of risks. The latest projections from Fed officials also suggest they expect the central bank to lower rates two more times this year, with rates forecast in a range of 3.50 percent to 3.75 percent by the end of 2025.

Traders may have been disappointed that Fed officials don’t appear eager to cut interest rates aggressively, with only newly sworn in Fed Governor Stephen Miran preferring to lower rates by half a point at the latest meeting. The latest forecasts also suggest Fed officials currently only expect one rate cut next year, although there were significant differences of opinion on the outlook. The central bank’s next monetary policy meeting is scheduled for October 28-29, with CME Group’s FedWatch tool currently indicating an 89.1 percent chance the Fed will lower rates by another quarter point.

In the bond market, treasuries came under pressure in the latter part of the session, closing notably lower. Subsequently, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, jumped 5.0 basis points to 4.076 percent. Trading on Thursday may continue to be impacted by reaction to the Fed announcement, while reports on initial jobless claims and leading economic indicators are also likely to attract attention.
