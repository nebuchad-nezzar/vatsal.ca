U.S. equities advanced on Thursday, led by a sharp rebound in technology stocks following Nvidia’s strong results and guidance the prior evening. The S&P 500 rose approximately 0.7–0.8%, the Nasdaq Composite gained around 1.4–1.5%, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average advanced 0.3–0.4%. Nvidia itself surged roughly 8–9%, delivering one of its strongest sessions in recent months and lifting the broader semiconductor and AI complex. Technology was the clear standout sector, while most other groups finished mixed to lower.

The positive tone in technology extended beyond Nvidia. Salesforce, CrowdStrike, and Okta—all of which reported strong results after the close on Wednesday—continued to see strong follow-through. On Thursday itself, Marvell Technology delivered a solid report, posting record quarterly revenue of $2.739 billion, up 37% year-over-year, with data-center revenue accelerating to 46% growth. Management guided higher for the current quarter. Workday also reported constructive results, with total revenue of $2.649 billion (up 12.8% year-over-year) and subscription revenue up 13.9%. The company raised its full-year subscription revenue and non-GAAP operating margin guidance, citing momentum in its AI-driven platform. The combination of Nvidia’s bullish longer-term outlook and supportive prints from Marvell, Workday, and the prior evening’s software and cybersecurity results helped reverse some of the caution that had weighed on the group earlier in the week.

Oil prices remained softer relative to recent peaks, reflecting both profit-taking and a modest improvement in the geopolitical backdrop. Iran and Oman advanced talks on an interim framework for a temporary joint maritime corridor through the Strait of Hormuz, including a joint mine-clearance project. Reports on Thursday indicated that the two sides had also reached a revenue-sharing arrangement related to the waterway. Qatar’s Prime Minister visited Tehran and voiced support for the framework while discussing de-escalation. Despite these diplomatic steps, actual shipping volumes through the strait remain well below pre-conflict levels, and isolated incidents continue to be reported. The market is beginning to price a lower probability of prolonged full disruption, which has contributed to the recent decline in crude prices, though residual risk remains.

Treasury yields were relatively stable as attention turned toward Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh’s remarks at the Jackson Hole Symposium on Friday. The week’s technology earnings have provided important confirmation that demand for AI infrastructure, enterprise software, and cybersecurity remains robust. Nvidia’s results in particular reinforced the durability of the structural growth story, while the strong showings from Marvell, Workday, Salesforce, CrowdStrike, and Okta added breadth to the positive fundamental picture. At the same time, elevated valuations, lingering yield sensitivity, and residual geopolitical uncertainty continue to favor a selective approach.

We maintain a constructive longer-term view on technology and AI-related equities, supported by resilient demand for advanced semiconductors, data-center infrastructure, and enterprise software platforms. Concentration risk and elevated valuations warrant discipline. Our preference remains selective exposure to quality companies with clear visibility into AI-driven revenue growth, genuine pricing power, and strong free-cash-flow generation.

Attention now turns to residual technology earnings and Federal Reserve Chair Warsh’s speech at Jackson Hole. We will continue to monitor developments around the Strait of Hormuz, the path of bond yields and energy prices, and incoming inflation data, and will keep you updated as conditions evolve.
