September 21, 2025

The Dow, S&P, NASDAQ and Russell 2000 all notched record high closes this past week after the U. S. Federal Reserve cut interest rates by 25 bps and signaled more cuts are likely to ensue.

The U. S. 10-year held above 4% and the 2-year reclaimed 3.5% while mortgage rates held near their lowest levels in 3-years. Initially rates moved lower as markets perhaps took solace in the fact the new temporary Governor Miran was one dissenter on the FOMC, quelling some of the rising anxiety over Fed independence.

Rates quickly steadied before drifting higher after Chairman Powell noted officials didn’t seriously debate a 50 bps cut this week. Powell acknowledged the difficulty he and his colleagues face in steering policy adeptly to satisfy both portions of the dual mandate. He labeled this current easing cycle as a risk management exercise to protect against growing downside risks to the labor market.

The Euro hit a 4-year high before the U. S. dollar broadly reversed higher and gold moved lower after topping $3,700 for the first time. Small caps led another leg up for stocks with the Russell 2K making a new all-time high for the first time since 2001.

For the week, the S&P gained 1.2%, and the Nasdaq rose 2.2%. European bourses were relatively flat on the week; the French CAC40 added 0.4%, and the German DAX slipped 0.3%. Asia Pacific stock markets were mixed; Japan advanced 0.6% while mainland China retreated 1.3%.

Your BSD Global Tech Hedge Fund rose 3.1% this past week. Economic data was largely constructive this week with signs of healthier growth and cooling inflation. Initial jobless claims moved back in-line with what has been seen throughout 2025, suggesting last week’s spike was more a data anomaly.

August retail sales and manufacturing production topped estimates sending the Atlanta Fed GDP Q3 model back into the mid 3% range. Also, Empire manufacturing and Philadelphia Fed readings were seen as encouraging both in the respective headline readings, and with the trends of the underlying components. Separately, President Trump traveled to England to enjoy the pomp and tradition of his second official state visit while announcing a series of tech partnerships in the U.

K. He also held a call with China’s President Xi touting progress towards a conclusion of the TikTok saga as well as advancement regarding their ongoing trade talks. On Capitol Hill, Republicans were able to pass their short term funding bill through the House but it failed to reach the 60 votes needed to overcome the filibuster rule in the Senate, leaving no clear path to avoid a government shutdown in early October.

Corporate events this week were highlighted by a number of interesting new alliances as well as a handful of notable earnings reports and guides. Nvidia announced a $5B investment in Intel, giving the AI hardware giant entrée into more PC products, while shoring up Intel’s finances. Like Intel, shares of Lyft got boosted by a surprise partnership with a company with deep pockets: Google’s Waymo announced that they will back Lyft in expanding a robo taxi service to Nashville starting next year.

In another case of rivals working more closely, Amazon said its multi-channel fulfillment will expand support for merchants on SHEIN, Shopify, and Walmart. Logistics giant Fedex registered decent quarterly results and guided positively for the quarter and year, saying its cautiously optimistic for winter holiday delivery season. Meanwhile Nucor indicated the U.

S. steel renaissance is not all it’s cracked up to be, guiding Q3 earnings substantially below consensus, citing lower volumes and margin compression. Live Nation also experienced some weakness as the FTC launched a long awaited suit over illegal ticket resale tactics. Next week is a busy one, with Flash PMI survey data providing a key focus for the markets in the coming week, though Friday’s release of the U.

S. core PCE price index will also be eagerly awaited, a measure widely seen as the Fed’s preferred inflation gauge. Other releases of note include revised US GDP numbers, consumer confidence data for the U. S. and Europe, plus US home sales, durable goods orders and inventories.

There will be plenty of Federal Reserve speakers on the docket this coming week including Chair Jerome Powell, newly appointed Governor Stephen Miran, and Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle Bowman. Looking at earnings, memory chipmaker Micron Technology will highlight the names reporting this week.
