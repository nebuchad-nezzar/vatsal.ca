December 4, 2025

Asian equity markets finished mostly in the green overnight led by a 2.3% jump in the Nikkei. Japanese stocks don’t seem bothered by the increased likelihood of a BoJ rate hike this month (reports say the Takaichi cabinet is “ok” with a move from the BoJ) or the fact that the 10 year JGB yield rose to its highest level since 2007 last night. Equities in mainland China finished the day relatively flat.

There is talk of more macro prudential management of property finance in China. On a positive note, U. S.

Treasury Secretary Bessent claims that the remainder of President Trump’s term will be good for China. European bourses closed modestly higher today, unbothered by the lack of progress in U. S.-Russia talks.

Besides threatening the rest of Europe, Putin also noted that Russia would take control of Donbas by military means or otherwise. A peace deal definitely remains elusive. Eurozone construction PMI data was better than expected but it was a relatively low bar to beat.

After moving higher over the two previous sessions, North American stock markets showed a lack of direction over the course of the trading day on Thursday. The major averages spent the day bouncing back and forth across the unchanged line before eventually closing narrowly mixed. The lackluster performance came as traders took a step back to assess the near-term outlook for the markets following the volatility seen earlier in the week.

Stocks pulled back on Monday after last week’s weak rally only to regain ground during relatively choppy trading on Tuesday and Wednesday. Optimism about another interest rate cut by the Federal Reserve next week helped the major averages more than offset Monday’s decline. Meanwhile, traders largely shrugged off a report from the Labor Department showing first-time claims for U.

S. unemployment benefits unexpectedly fell to a three-year low in the week ended November 29th. The report said initial jobless claims slid to 191,000, a decrease of 27,000 from the previous week’s revised level of 218,000. Economists had expected jobless claims to rise to 220,000 from the 216,000 originally reported for the previous week.

With the unexpected decline, jobless claims dropped to their lowest level since hitting 189,000 in the week ended September 24, 2022. While the data partly offset recent optimism about the Federal Reserve cutting interest rates next week, the central bank is still widely expected to lower rates by another quarter point. While most of the major sectors showed only modest moves on the day, computer hardware stocks saw a substantial rebound, with the NYSE Arca Computer Hardware Index surging by 3.0 percent after slumping by 1.7 percent on Wednesday.

In the bond market, treasuries gave back ground after moving higher over the two previous sessions. Subsequently, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, climbed 5.1 basis points to 4.108 percent. Trading on Friday may be impacted by reaction to closely watched readings on consumer price inflation in September, although the data could be seen as too backward looking to impact next week’s Fed decision.
