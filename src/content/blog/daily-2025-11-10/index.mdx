November 10, 2025

I think it is pretty clear that the main driver this morning is the news that eight Democratic senators broke ranks and sided with the Republicans in advancing a funding bill. The measure passed in a 60-40 vote and will be sent to the House later this week where it is expected to clear that hurdle before heading to President Trump’s desk for signature. Speedy passage means that the government could be up and running later this week.

That also means the BLS will also be up and running which in turn means the Fed can start getting its data, lift the statistical fog and cut rates in December. The trade news out of Asia was upbeat as well adding to positive sentiment. China suspended its ban on U.

S. bound shipments of dual use goods related to gallium and germanium for a year while the USTR confirmed its suspension of the Section 301 investigation targeting China’s maritime, logistics and shipbuilding sectors. China’s October CPI print did see its largest monthly increase since January (+.2%) but let’s keep things in perspective, year over year inflation is running at .2% and PPI came in at -2.1% YoY, no real inflation concern in China at the moment. European bourses are all in the green today despite some softer Eurozone investor confidence data.

No doubt markets have been cheered by thoughts of the U. S. Government reopening and more likely by the thought of another Fed cut in December.

We saw some more positive trade news for Europe as well with China exempting Nexperia chips for civilian use with reports that carmakers are now receiving chip deliveries following a recent supply halt. North American stock markets  moved sharply higher during trading on Monday, regaining ground following the considerable weakness seen in the previous week. The major averages all moved to the upside on the day, with the tech-heavy Nasdaq leading the charge.

The rally came after the Senate voted to advance legislation to end the government shutdown, which recently became the longest in U. S. history. The Senate voted 60-40 in favor of a temporary funding bill, which would also reverse some of the recent mass federal layoffs.

Several Democratic Senators broke with party leaders in favor of moving forward with the legislation, as it does call for a vote on the extension of enhanced Obamacare tax credits. Final approval of the bill could be delayed by any one Senator, and the legislation still needs to be approved by the Republican-controlled House of Representatives. While the markets have largely shrugged off concerns about the shutdown in recent weeks, the news still seems to have led some traders to pick up stocks at somewhat reduced levels following recent valuation worries.

The end of the shutdown would also lead to the resumption of the release of key U. S. economic data that has recently been withheld. Gold stocks moved sharply higher along with the price of the precious metal, with the NYSE Arca Gold Bugs Index spiking by 4.8 percent.

Computer hardware, semiconductor and software stocks also saw substantial strength on the day, contributing to the surge by the Nasdaq. Pharmaceutical, oil service and networking stocks also turned in strong performances, while airline stocks showed a notable move to the downside. In the bond market, treasuries saw modest weakness after ending lastFriday’s trading roughly flat. Subsequently, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, rose by 1.7 basis points to 4.110 percent.
