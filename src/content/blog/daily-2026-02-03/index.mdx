February 3, 2026

In Asia overnight stocks were up across the board. Let’s call it the Palantir effect with respect to stocks (at least partially). The company reported some pretty terrific earnings after the bell yesterday helping to boost sentiment early on last night. (The Rule of 40: “The Rule of 40 is a software-as-a-service (SaaS) benchmark stating that a company’s combined annual revenue growth rate and profit margin (usually EBITDA margin) should exceed 40%” – Palantir’s hit an apparently record breaking 127 last night).

After falling 5% yesterday, the Kospi finished up almost 7% to a new record high with chip names leading the way. The Nikkei jumped almost 4% as expectations grow for Takaichi’s LDP coalition to win a majority in the Lower House and kick start all that stimulus, India ended 2.5% higher helped by a US-India trade deal and even the ASX ended in the green despite a RBA rate hike and a more hawkish sounding central bank. All that positivity didn’t really spill over into Europe where markets are mixed.

France is outperforming helped by the passage (finally) of the 2026 budget as well as some very tame inflation data this morning. The FTSE is underperforming, perhaps spooked by fiscal concerns after the FT reported that PM Starmer plans to push forward with a delayed defence investment plan. North American stock markets moved notably lower during trading on Tuesday, more than offsetting the strength seen in the previous session.

The major averages all moved to the downside on the day, with the tech-heavy Nasdaq showing a particularly steep drop. The weakness on Wall Street was largely due to a rotation out of technology stocks, as reflected by the steep drop by the Nasdaq. Software stocks turned in some of the tech sector’s worst performances, dragging the Dow Jones U.S. Software Index down by 3.5 percent to its lowest closing level in over nine months. The weakness occurred despite a surge by shares of Palantir Technologies, which shot up by 6.9 percent after the AI-powered software provider reported better than expected fourth quarter results and provided upbeat guidance.

Substantial weakness was also visible among semiconductor stocks, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index tumbling by 2.1 percent. Shares of NXP Semiconductors plunged by 4.5 percent even though the Dutch chipmaker reported fourth quarter results that exceeded analyst estimates on the top and bottom lines. Meanwhile, some stocks benefited from the rotation out of tech, including retail giant Walmart, which surged by 2.9 percent and surpassed a $1 trillion market capitalization.

Gold stocks also saw considerable strength amid a significant rebound by the price of the precious metal, with the NYSE Arca Gold Bugs Index spiking by 4.4 percent. In the bond market, treasuries closed roughly flat after seeing modest weakness for much of the day. The yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, edged down by less than a basis point to 4.274 percent after reaching a high of 4.30 percent. Trading on Wednesday may be impacted by reaction to reports on private sector employment and service sector activity.
