December 11, 2025

It didn’t take long before the Fed meeting was all but a distant memory as Oracle earnings came in giving the market a bit of a reality check after Oracle reported earnings that reignited all those concerns we have been having about the aggressive spending on data centers. In the earnings call, ORCL noted that the jump in spending on AI centers and other equipment is taking longer to translate into cloud revenue than investors may want, sending shares down 11% on early trading. The concerns rippled through to Asia with Nikkei leading the losses overnight down 0.90%.

Losses were not contained to tech with Hang Seng falling 0.5% marking a third straight decline to a two week low pressured by the worries of AI paired with risks to Chinese growth forecasts. Softer producer and consumer prices yesterday highlighted the weak demand and the risks to the property sector aren’t helping ease concerns. The North American stock markets moved in starkly opposite directions in early trading on Thursday, with the Dow moving sharply higher and the Nasdaq showing a significant pullback.

The surge by the Dow partly reflected a sharp increase by shares of Visa, with the payment card services company spiking by 6.1 percent after Bank of America upgraded its rating on the stock to Buy from Neutral. Strong gains by Nike, UnitedHealth and American Express also contributed to the jump by the blue chip index. On the other hand, a steep drop by shares of Oracle weighed on the Nasdaq, as the software giant plunged by 10.8 percent.

The slump by Oracle came after the company reported fiscal second quarter earnings that exceeded analyst estimates but weaker than expected revenues. Other AI-related stocks like Nvidia also moved to the downside, potentially reflecting renewed valuation concerns. On the U.S. economic front, the Labor Department released a report showing first-time claims for U.S. unemployment benefits rebounded by more than expected in the week ended December 6th. The report said initial jobless claims rose to 236,000, an increase of 44,000 from the previous week’s revised level of 192,000.

Economists had expected jobless claims to climb to 220,000 from the 191,000 originally reported for the previous week. In the bond market, treasuries extended the upward move seen over the course of the previous session. Subsequently, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, fell 2.3 basis points to 4.141 percent.
