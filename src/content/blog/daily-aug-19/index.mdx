U.S. equities managed a modest rebound on Wednesday, snapping a three-day losing streak as a pullback in longer-term Treasury yields provided some relief to risk assets. The S&P 500 rose 0.2% to 7,707.98, the Dow Jones Industrial Average advanced 0.2% to 53,463.05, and the Nasdaq Composite edged 0.2% higher to 26,331.09. While the gains were measured, they reflected a constructive shift in bond-market dynamics after recent upward pressure on yields had weighed on valuations, particularly in growth-oriented segments of the market.

Oil prices remained elevated, with Brent settling near $91.60 and WTI around $85.80, as restricted shipping through the Strait of Hormuz and the absence of any diplomatic progress continued to support a geopolitical risk premium. The 60-day memorandum of understanding between the United States and Iran has expired without renewal, and both sides remain entrenched. Shipping volumes through the strait stayed well below normal levels, reinforcing the energy-market overhang that continues to complicate the broader inflation outlook.

The Federal Reserve's July meeting minutes, released Wednesday, revealed that inflation concerns had deepened among policymakers. Several participants favored a rate increase at the time, and many indicated that further policy tightening would likely be required if inflation failed to decline toward the 2% target. The minutes underscored a more divided Committee than markets had fully priced, even as recent softer data have reduced near-term odds of a September hike.

On the corporate front, retail earnings provided a mixed but generally resilient picture of the consumer. Target reported better-than-expected second-quarter results, with sales up 5.3% and comparable sales rising 3.8%. TJX Companies delivered a solid beat on both the top and bottom lines, with comparable sales up 4%, and raised its full-year profit guidance. However, management noted weaker-than-expected performance at its largest U.S. apparel banners, which it described as largely self-inflicted and within its control. The results suggest value-oriented retail continues to attract budget-conscious shoppers, even as some discretionary categories show signs of moderation.

The session highlighted the ongoing tension between constructive corporate fundamentals and persistent macro and geopolitical crosscurrents. Earnings resilience, particularly among companies with genuine pricing power and efficient cost structures, remains a key support for equities. At the same time, elevated energy prices, the backup in long-end yields earlier in the week, and the unresolved Hormuz situation continue to inject uncertainty into both the growth and inflation outlooks.

We maintain our constructive longer-term view on global equities, underpinned by broad earnings strength, structural investment themes in AI infrastructure, defense, electrification, and energy security, and improving cyclical conditions in several regions outside the United States. Concentration risk in U.S. mega-cap technology remains elevated, reinforcing the importance of diversified exposure across Europe, Japan, and Asia ex-Japan. Our preference continues to favor quality cash-flow generators and selective relative-value opportunities over aggressive directional positioning.

Walmart reports tomorrow and will provide an additional read on the consumer. We will continue to monitor residual earnings, developments around the Strait of Hormuz, the path of bond yields, and incoming inflation data, and will keep you updated as conditions evolve.
