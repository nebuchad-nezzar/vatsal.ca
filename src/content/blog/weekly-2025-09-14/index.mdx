September 14, 2025

Global stock markets notched fresh all-time highs yet again this week, driven by a confluence of another surge in AI momentum, increased certainty the Fed will re-engage in a rate cutting cycle this coming week, and robust capital markets activity. Nevertheless, questions remained on what ongoing political turmoil overseas could mean for U. S. framework trade agreements following the resignation of Japan PM Ishiba, the ouster of French PM Bayrou, and UK PM Starmer’s unenviable situation.

Separately, Mexico announced plans to impose 50% tariffs on China car imports in a move seen as intended to bolster ties with the U. S. Crude prices initially rose after Israel attempted to take out a Hamas negotiating team in Doha, resulting in broad condemnation from the Muslim world and Europe, as well as ire from the White House.

Oil prices largely remained capped on growing concerns of a potential supply glut and softening U. S. demand. The ECB left rates unchanged and hinted their rate cutting cycle may have come to end.

The U. S. 10-year yield moved down to test 4% for the first time since April following the release of August inflation readings, allowing the S&P to push up towards 6600 for the first time. Notably, that is still ~22x FY26 estimates reflecting continued optimism about earnings growth, predominantly driven by the AI technology sector.

Gold prices also continued to notch fresh all-time highs as central banks and investors alike continued to search for ballast. For the week, the S&P gained 1.6%, and the Nasdaq rose 2.0%. European bourses rallied to varying degrees; the French CAC40 advanced 2.0%, and the German DAX rose 0.4%.

Asia Pacific stock markets participated in the enthusiastic sentiment with Japan surging 4.1% and mainland China gaining 1.5%. Your BSD Global Tech Hedge Fund rose 3.5% this past week. August PPI was welcomingly cool, including the first negative m/m print since April.

The shortfall in the headline index mainly reflected surprisingly soft results for energy, food and trade services with the narrow core index (excluding those three components) rising as expected. CPI data ran hotter but mostly in line with expectations while remaining stuck above the Fed’s target. A jump in weekly initial jobless claims to the highest level since 2021 came in the wake of disappointing BLS revisions showing the U.

S. added close to 76K fewer jobs per month over the last year. It was the latest data to underscore the risks to the employment portion of the Fed’s dual mandate. Futures markets saw expectations for a 25 basis point cut at all three of the remaining 2025 FOMC meetings solidify while calls for a 50bps cut this week grew louder.

Undoubtedly, expectations have been goosed heading into the Wednesday Fed meeting which includes a dot plot update, where lingering stagflation concerns could keep the Fed from delivering an overwhelmingly dovish message. Outside of the mostly constructive commentary from firms at a wide array of financial conferences, the corporate front saw a number of high profile deals, product launches, and key earnings. Oracle’s quarterly results were about in line with expectations but analysts were blown away by its RPO contract backlog, up 359% to $455B, largely predicated on OpenAi remaining on track for a massive build out of its service infrastructure in the years ahead.

Echostar saw its shares gain a higher orbit as it signed a massive spectrum sale to SpaceX as the latter tries to beef up Starlink’s ability to compete in space based broadband services. Apple’s iPhone 17 launch was well received, including the roll out of a new ultrathin iPhone Air model alongside the usual camera and processing speed upgrades. As expected, Apple did fold in a 10% price bump for its Pro model under the guise of providing more storage in the base model.

Bill.com was in the news again as a report emerged that Elliott Management has built a stake on the heels of Starboard taking an activist position that was revealed last week. Warner Brothers Discovery shares shot higher on a report that the Ellison family’s Skydance Paramount is looking to roll up WBD, though other bidders could still emerge for the premium content provider. Investors’ focus this coming week will be almost entirely on the Federal Reserve.

The central bank’s monetary policy committee will deliver its seventh interest rate decision of the year on Wednesday, but unlike the previous six, this resolution will be one of the most anticipated in a while. The Fed has stubbornly held interest rates steady since ending 2024 with a series of cuts, but now with the labor market showing continued signs of cooling and inflation remaining sticky, traders are all but certain that the central bank will restart its policy easing process. While the Fed will be in focus, this week’s economic calendar will be highlighted by the latest retail sales data.

Turning to the earnings season, global economic bellwether FedEx is among notable names set to report this coming week. Elsewhere in the world, the Bank of Canada is also expected to cut rates by 25 bps on Wednesday, while the Bank of England and the Bank of Japan are not expected to cut rates. China will publish its August retail sales, industrial output, and fixed-asset investment data on Monday.
