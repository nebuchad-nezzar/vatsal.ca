November 17, 2025

Asian markets were mixed overnight with the Kospi outperforming on the back of reports that both Samsung and Hynix are planning on investing billions (Samsung $300bio/Hynix $85bio) in chip production over the next three to five years (spending is a huge boost – as long as it is related to anything AI). The Hang Seng/stocks in mainland China underperformed despite U. S.

Treasury Secretary Bessent saying that the U. S. aims to complete its rare earths trade agreement with China by Thanksgiving stating he expects the U. S. military to also get access to material.

European bourses were lower across the board today. I don’t think there is/was any single catalyst but European Commission forecasts of pretty tepid economic growth (1.4%) for the next two years against a backdrop of “persistent uncertainty” sure didn’t compel anyone to race out and buy stocks. UK house price data also showed the biggest drop since the start of the year adding to the gloomy picture.

After showing a lack of direction early in the session, North American stock markets moved sharply lower over the course of the trading day on Monday. The major averages all showed notable moves to the downside, falling to their lowest closing levels in a month. The weakness came as valuation concerns resurfaced ahead of the release of quarterly results from Nvidia.

The strength of Nvidia’s results and its guidance could have a significant impact on the markets amid recent concerns about an AI bubble. Nvidia, which is due to release its third quarter results after the close of trading on Wednesday, tumbled by 1.8 percent on the day. Traders were also looking ahead to the release of U.

S. economic data that was delayed due to the recently ended government shutdown. The Commerce Department kicked off the release of its delayed data this morning with the release of a report unexpectedly showing a modest increase by construction spending in the month of August. Reports on factory orders and the U.

S. trade deficit in August are also due to be released in the coming days along with the monthly jobs report for September. While the reports will be more backward looking than traders would prefer, the data could still impact the outlook for interest rates ahead of the Federal Reserve’s next monetary policy meeting in December. CME Group’s FedWatch Tool is currently indicating a 55.1 percent chance the Fed will leave rates unchanged next month and a 44.9 percent chance of another quarter point rate cut.

In the bond market, treasuries regained ground after moving lower over the two previous sessions. As a result, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, dipped 1.5 basis points to 4.133 percent.
