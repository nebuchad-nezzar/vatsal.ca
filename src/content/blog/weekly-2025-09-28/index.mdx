September 28, 2025

Nvidia’s splashy agreement to invest $100B into OpenAI initially sent the AI hype train further into overdrive. But that news along with Oracle’s $18B investment grade bond offering resulted in renewed scrutiny on just how sustainable the AI financing ecosystem will ultimately prove to be. U.

S. Treasury yields also continued drifting up with rates testing the highest levels since the FOMC rate cut. Fed officials were out in force staking claims to the disparate views outlined in the latest SEP projections.

Chair Powell underscored again that the balance of risks has shifted toward employment downside, as the demand for jobs cools; but since labor supply is also falling, while inflation remains elevated, forecasting remains exceedingly difficult. He also opined a view that equity markets are currently fairly highly valued. Stocks rolled off their most recent all-time highs following his comments with relative weakness, and a rotation away from technology shares.

Gold remained thoroughly bid, testing $3,800 for the first time. The U. S. dollar gained ground, touching a 3-week high after a slew of economic readings were mostly robust.

Weekly jobless claims fell to the lowest levels since mid-July, GDP was revised higher on the back of notably strong personal consumption while August durable goods orders were strong even excluding transportation. Existing home sales were also solid while importantly on the inflation front prices held flat. S&P preliminary PMI respondents also noted the number of companies able to hike selling prices to pass along rising costs to customers has fallen.

Conversely, Richmond Fed data was softer than expected including new orders. Friday’s PCE print was basically on the screws with expectations stuck ~1% above the Fed’s 2% target but not worryingly accelerating. Tensions between the West and Russia over Ukraine rose coming out of the UN general assembly.

President Trump rhetoric turned demonstrably more acute towards Russia in particular its more recent provocations and the western members promised additional measures to curb Russia from financing its war. Trump also announced another batch of tariffs to take effect on October 1st including on branded drugs by producers not showing good faith in moving production to the U. S., as well as additional levies on furniture and heavy trucks.

Also, the administration is reportedly planning to ask chipmakers to produce domestically as many semiconductors as their customers import. Separately, Argentina ADRs rose along with the Peso after U. S.

Treasury Secretary Bessent indicated the U. S. stands ready to support the Milei government. For the week, the S&P slipped 0.3%, and the Nasdaq lost 0.7%.

European bourses modestly rose; the French CAC40 advanced 0.2%, and the German DAX rose 0.4%. Asia Pacific stock markets were bifurcated with Japan gaining 0.7% while Hong Kong fell 1.6%. Your BSD Global Tech Hedge Fund retreated 2.3% this past week.

Apple shares opened the week on strong footing as all signs continued to point to a strong iPhone 17 launch this past weekend. Oracle announced a CEO transition and confirmed they are part of President Trump’s TikTok deal. Nvidia’s announcement that they are investing $100B into OpenAI through a strategic partnership to deploy 10 Gigawatts of NVIDIA Systems initially threw gasoline on the AI hype fire.

But analysts and investors alike were fairly quick to point out the intertwined relationship between the two companies could be seen in a similar light as the vendor financing deals that dotted the landscape ahead of the bursting of the internet bubble nearly at the turn of the century. Also on Friday, Jabil and Accenture both reported strong top line growth, but profitability of their incremental AI related revenue was somewhat underwhelming. Micron reported strong results and raised forecasts for their data center demand.

Freeport cut their gold and copper sales forecasts following a tragic incident at Indonesia’s Grasburg mine. Carmax shares got slammed following an extremely disappointing Q2 earnings report marred by higher depreciation from a pull forward in demand by tariffs. Costco had another strong quarter and noted the will continuing investing aggressively into 2026.

Boeing climbed on Friday following a report the FAA is set to sign off on an increase in 737 Max production. Investors will gear up for jobs week, with market participants set to receive a host of indicators at a time when the Federal Reserve has cut interest rates, citing increased downside risks to the labor market. On Tuesday, August job openings data will arrive, followed by private employment figures on Wednesday.

The main highlight of the week will be the U. S. September nonfarm payrolls report on Friday.

Traders will be closely watching out for further signs of weakness in the labor market. Aside from the economic calendar, investors will also have some earnings to look forward to this week, including the latest quarterly report from the world’s largest shoe company, Nike. Golden Week begins this week in China (commemorating the 75th anniversary of the founding of the People’s Republic of China) plus some major Jewish holidays are celebrated this week and next week (Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Sukkot and Simchat Torah); these events will diminish market liquidity.
