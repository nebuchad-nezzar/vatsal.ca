January 26, 2026

Overnight, Asian equity markets were mixed with the Nikkei the clear underperformer (-1.8%) on the back of a much stronger Yen. Reports say the Fed conducted a rare “rate check” on USD/JPY implying that the Fed may be considering coordinated action with the BoJ to support the Yen. This has brought up thoughts about a carry trade unwind and the potential impact on stocks.

I remind you of the move in USD/JPY the last time the BoJ reportedly intervened in July 2024. Elsewhere, there was a purge in China with President Xi removing China’s top Military Commander along with the Chief of the Joint Staff. Reports note that no one with military experience remains on the Central Military Commission that runs China’s armed forces.

European bourses were mixed today. Ukrainian President Zelenskyy mentioned trilateral talks with the U.S. and Russia were constructive, but we’ve heard that one before.

Reports say further meetings might take place next week. Positives: France’s PM Lecornu managed to pass at least part of the 2026 budget after surviving a couple of no confidence votes and the EU will reportedly sign a trade deal with India tomorrow with a headline cut in tariffs on EU made cars to 40% from 110%. The negative: German business sentiment data was softer than expected this morning.

Following the mixed performance seen during last Friday’s session, North American stock markets moved mostly higher during trading on Monday. With the upward move, the major averages further offset the steep drop seen last Tuesday. The strength came amid strong gains by Apple, Meta Platforms and Microsoft, which are moving higher ahead of the release of their quarterly results later this week.

Shares of Apple surged by 3.0 percent, shares of Meta jumped by 2.1 percent and shares of Microsoft advanced by 0.9 percent. However, traders seemed reluctant to make more significant moves ahead of the Federal Reserve’s monetary policy announcement on Wednesday. While the Fed is widely expected to leave interest rates unchanged, traders will closely watch the accompanying statement for clues about the outlook for rates.

Traders also kept an eye on the latest geopolitical developments, with President Donald Trump threatening to impose a 100 percent tariff on goods from Canada over a potential free trade deal with China. Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney responded by saying his country has no intention of pursuing such a deal. The U.S. government is also facing the possibility of another shutdown, as several Democratic senators have threatened to oppose a spending bill if it includes appropriations for the Department of Homeland Security. The impasse over DHS funding comes after federal immigration agents shot and killed another U.S. citizen in Minneapolis over the weekend.

In U.S. economic news, a report released by the Commerce Department showed new orders for U.S. manufactured durable goods surged by much more than expected in the month of November.

Networking stocks turned in some of the market’s best performances on the day, with the NYSE Arca Networking Index jumping by 2.0 percent. Notable strength was also visible among software stocks, as reflected by the 1.1 percent gain posted by the Dow Jones U.S.

Software Index. In the bond market, treasuries saw further upside after ending last Friday’s session slightly higher. As a result, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, fell 2.6 basis points to 4.213 percent.
