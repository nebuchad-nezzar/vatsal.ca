U.S. equities rebounded strongly on Thursday, reversing much of the post-Fed selling as oil prices and bond yields both eased. The **S&P 500** rose 1.1% to 7,637.76, the **Dow Jones Industrial Average** gained 0.6% to 51,778.04, and the **Nasdaq Composite** advanced 1.7% to 26,418.30. The move reflected a reassessment of the previous day’s hawkish Federal Reserve decision and a pullback in energy prices that reduced near-term inflation concerns.

### Yields and Oil Cool
The **10-year Treasury yield** slipped back below 5% after briefly trading above 5.01% on Wednesday. The retreat in yields, combined with lower crude prices, supported rate-sensitive and growth-oriented sectors. **Brent crude** fell further, trading below $105 and at one point near $103–104, as markets weighed reports that Saudi Arabia was offering alternative cargoes and that the impact of the East-West pipeline disruption might prove less severe than initially feared.

### Geopolitical Risks Persist
Geopolitical risks, however, showed no meaningful de-escalation. Saudi warplanes continued airstrikes across Yemen, including in Hajjah province near the border and around Taiz, while Houthi forces launched fresh drone and missile attacks into Saudi territory. Saudi civil defence reported the first confirmed fatality linked to the recent cross-border exchanges — a Yemeni resident killed by debris from an intercepted drone over Taif. 

The Houthis’ rapid advance has given them control of Yemen’s entire Red Sea coastline and strategic positions near the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, expanding the conflict’s reach and complicating Saudi oil export routes. More than 100,000 Yemenis have been displaced in the latest fighting. The United States tightened travel restrictions for government personnel near the Yemen border. Despite these developments, oil prices eased on the day as traders focused on alternative supply arrangements and the possibility that pipeline repair timelines may be shorter than first assessed. Shipping volumes through the Strait of Hormuz remained heavily constrained.

### Tech Leads the Recovery
Technology led the equity recovery. After several sessions of heavy selling triggered by industry warnings about the pace of AI development, selective buying returned with force:
*   **Intel** rose sharply, at one point advancing close to 10%, supported by ongoing speculation around potential foundry and memory collaborations. 
*   **AMD**, **Nvidia**, **Marvell**, and **Super Micro Computer** also posted solid gains. 
*   Hardware and server-related names, including **Dell** and **Hewlett Packard Enterprise**, continued to attract interest on the back of durable demand for data-center capacity. 

Broader semiconductor and AI-infrastructure names participated, with the **PHLX Semiconductor Index** advancing more than 3%. The rebound suggested investors are increasingly differentiating between companies with visible near-term revenue visibility and those more exposed to longer-duration valuation risk.

### Broad Sector Performance
Sector performance was broadly constructive. Technology and communication services outperformed, while energy lagged as crude declined. The dollar eased after six consecutive sessions of strength, providing additional relief to commodities and multinational earnings. Volatility also moderated.

### The Bottom Line
The session left the market in a more constructive short-term posture following the Fed’s first rate increase in three years. The combination of lower oil prices and a modest retreat in long-term yields reduced some of the immediate pressure that had weighed on equities. At the same time, the fundamental backdrop remains challenging: policy is now in tightening mode, the 10-year yield is still near multi-year highs, housing affordability stays constrained, and geopolitical risk around energy supply has not been resolved.

Selective exposure to companies with durable free-cash-flow generation, pricing power, and clear exposure to structural demand in semiconductors and AI infrastructure remains preferred. Concentration risk, elevated valuations, and a higher cost of capital continue to warrant discipline.
