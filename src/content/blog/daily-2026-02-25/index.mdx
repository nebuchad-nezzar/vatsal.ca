February 25, 2026

As we have become used to by now, the Kospi finished the session higher after the Bank of Korea held rates steady (as expected) with BOK Governor Rhee noting that MSCI inclusion could boost foreign equity inflows. The Nikkei also ended in the green although it closed well off its earlier highs with PM Takaichi noting authorities were watching FX moves with the usual “high sense of urgency”. The Hang Seng was the underperformer of the session falling about 1.5% while stocks in mainland China also faced some pressure despite USTR Greer stating that the U.S. didn’t have plans to escalate tariffs on China. Of note, the PBOC set the Yuan reference rate at 6.9228, the strongest fix since May 2023 (maybe helping to maintain pressure on the U.S.

Dollar more broadly?). European bourses were all higher today despite weaker than expected EU confidence data. Maybe the market has been encouraged by ongoing U.S.-Iran nuclear talks. Iran’s Foreign Ministry spokesperson stated that his country has a “reasonable amount of flexibility,” while Oman’s foreign Minister who is mediating said both the U.S. and Iranian negotiators are showing unprecedented openness in the discussions.

After moving sharply higher over the two previous sessions, North American stock markets gave back some ground during trading on Thursday. The tech-heavy Nasdaq showed a significant move to the downside, although the Dow managed to end the day slightly higher. The pullback came amid a negative reaction to earnings news from Nvidia, with the artificial intelligence chipmaker tumbled by 5.5 percent.

Nvidia’s shares pulled back from their best closing level in over three months even though the company reported better-than-expected fiscal fourth-quarter results and provided upbeat guidance. Nvidia helped to lead the semiconductor sector lower, as reflected by the 3.2 percent plunge by the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index. The index ended the previous session at a record closing high.

The uptick by the Dow partly reflected a sharp increase by shares of Salesforce, with the customer service software maker spiking by 4.0 percent after reporting better than expected fourth quarter results. On the U.S. economic front, a report released by the Labor Department showed a modest increase in first-time claims for U.S. unemployment benefits in the week ended February 21st. The Labor Department said initial jobless claims rose to 212,000, an increase of 4,000 from the previous week’s revised level of 208,000. Economists had expected jobless claims to climb to 215,000 from the 206,000 originally reported for the previous week.

In the bond market, treasuries moved higher, more than offsetting the weakness seen in the previous session. Subsequently, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, fell 3.1 basis points to a nearly three-month closing low of 4.017 percent. Trading on Friday may be impacted by reaction to the Labor Department’s report on producer price inflation in the month of January.
