January 12, 2025

Despite questions swirling around the Fed’s independence and a shaky geopolitical backdrop to say the least things got off on the right foot overnight in Asia with equity indices up across the board led by a 1.4% jump in the Hang Seng. Japan was closed for a holiday but managed to grab the headlines with reports over the weekend that PM Takaichi may take advantage of her high approval ratings to call for a snap election as early as February. Elsewhere, Chinese press reports are speculating on near term rate moves with talk of a potential RRR cut of 50bps ahead of the NPC annual session (March 5th).

With PPI still negative and CPI almost non-existent there seems to be plenty of room to lower rates to help spur growth in China. Meanwhile the trend continues for the currency with the PBOC setting the strongest Yuan fix in fifteen months. Sentiment shifted lower as Europe walked in with equity markets there mixed to start the week although moves have been pretty limited.

The news of Powell being investigated obviously has dampened sentiment (the ECB’s Muller stated that Trump’s pressure on Powell was purely political) and of course, concerns over Greenland remain front and center. The latest reports note that the UK and Germany are leading a group of countries in discussions for a military presence in Greenland in hopes of appeasing Trump and cooling off threats to take Greenland by force. A meeting is scheduled with U.S. Secretary of State Rubio on Wednesday. North American stock markets moved to the downside at the start of trading on Monday but showed a notable recovery over the course of the session.

The major averages climbed well off their lows of the session and into positive territory, with the Dow and the S&P 500 reaching new record closing highs. The initial pullback partly reflected concerns about the Federal Reserve’s independence after Fed Chair Jerome Powell revealed that the U.S. central bank has been served subpoenas by the Department of Justice that threaten criminal charges.

U.S. media reports said Federal prosecutors have launched a criminal investigation of Powell over his June testimony to Congress regarding the $2.5 billion renovation project for three buildings, including the Eccles building that serves as the headquarters of the Fed. Powell termed this action “unprecedented” and ascribed it to President Donald Trump’s ongoing threats and pressure on the Fed to lower interest rates.

The top official expressed his deep respect for the rule of law and for accountability in our democracy, adding that the Fed chair was not above the law. Selling pressure waned over the course of the session, however, as traders remain optimistic about the outlook for interest rates. While the Fed is widely expected to leave interest rates unchanged at its next meeting later this month, the central bank is still seen as likely to cut rates by at least another quarter point in the coming months.

Computer hardware stocks turned in some of the market’s best performances on the day, with the NYSE Arca Computer Hardware Index spiking by 5.0 percent. A sharp increase by the price of gold also contributed to substantial strength among gold stocks, as reflected by the 3.5 percent surge by the NYSE Arca Gold Bugs Index. In the bond market, treasuries regained some ground after an early slump but still ended the day in negative territory. Subsequently, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, increased by 1.6 basis points to 4.187 percent.
