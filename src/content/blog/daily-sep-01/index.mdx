September 1, 2026

U.S. equities opened September on a softer note, declining as rising Treasury yields, a sharp jump in oil prices, and renewed geopolitical tensions weighed on risk appetite. The S&P 500 fell 0.71% to 7,631.47, the Dow Jones Industrial Average declined 0.79% to approximately 52,767, and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 1.03% to around 26,100. The session marked a shaky start to a month that has historically been challenging for equities, extending recent pressure after August’s gains.

Sector performance highlighted the prevailing macro forces. Energy led the advance, supported by the strong move higher in crude prices. Defensive areas such as Utilities and Health Care showed relative resilience. Technology, Consumer Discretionary, and Industrials lagged, with the tech-heavy Nasdaq under particular pressure as higher yields weighed on growth-oriented valuations. Semiconductors and select software names faced selling, reflecting both the rate-sensitive backdrop and residual profit-taking after last week’s strong earnings-driven rally.

The dominant drivers were the continued climb in bond yields and a sharp rise in energy prices. The global bond sell-off deepened, with the U.S. 10-year Treasury yield moving higher and longer-dated yields remaining elevated by recent standards. In a notable development, Japan’s 10-year bond yield briefly touched 3% — its highest level in about 30 years — underscoring the breadth of the global rise in long-term rates. The Japanese yen hovered near the key 160-per-dollar level, with some firming after U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent indicated he expected Japanese authorities and the Bank of Japan to take steps that would support a stronger yen. Concerns around sticky inflation, fiscal dynamics, and the possibility of further Federal Reserve tightening—reinforced by Chair Warsh’s Jackson Hole remarks last week—kept rate-hike expectations elevated. At the same time, oil prices surged, with Brent settling up more than 4% near $94.65 and WTI advancing over 5% to around $90.22, the highest levels in several weeks. The move reflected renewed military exchanges between the United States and Iran, including fresh U.S. strikes on Iranian targets and continued risks to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. Traffic through the waterway remains well below pre-conflict norms, sustaining a geopolitical risk premium in energy markets.

On the data front, the JOLTS report showed fewer job openings than expected in July, offering a modest counterpoint to concerns about an overheating labor market, though it did little to alter the broader narrative around inflation and policy. Corporate news included the announced leadership transition at Apple, with John Ternus set to succeed Tim Cook as CEO—an orderly succession that drew attention but had limited immediate market impact.

Overall, the session underscored the ongoing tension between resilient corporate fundamentals—particularly in the technology and AI complex—and a more challenging macro environment defined by elevated yields, higher energy prices, and geopolitical uncertainty. Last week’s strong results from Nvidia, Salesforce, CrowdStrike, Marvell, and others provided important confirmation of durable AI-related demand, yet valuation sensitivity and policy risks continue to influence near-term price action.

Technology and AI-related equities supported by resilient demand for advanced semiconductors, data-center infrastructure, and enterprise software platforms. Concentration risk, elevated valuations, residual geopolitical uncertainty around energy supply, and the possibility of further monetary tightening warrant discipline. Our preference remains selective exposure to quality companies with clear visibility into AI-driven revenue growth, genuine pricing power, and strong free-cash-flow generation.

Looking ahead, the week brings key labor market data, ISM surveys, residual technology earnings (including Broadcom and others), and continued focus on developments around the Strait of Hormuz and the path of bond yields and energy prices. We will monitor these factors closely and keep you updated as conditions evolve.
