February 18, 2026

China, Hong Kong, South Korea and Taiwan among others all remain out for holiday so to say Asia was quieter than normal might be a bit of an understatement. The main news came out of New Zealand where the RBNZ held rates steady as expected but leaned a bit more dovish which in turn has led to the Kiwi underperforming this morning. Elsewhere, Japan’s trade balance came in much better than expected with exports jumping 16.8% YoY.

Shipments to the EU and China more than made up for a drop in exports to the U. S. but maybe the U. S. side will improve a bit now that Japan has kickstarted its U.

S. investment program with three projects in Texas, Georgia and Ohio worth $36 billion. European bourses were all higher today as fears of an AI led decimation of various sectors of the economy recede, at least for now. We saw pretty much inline inflation data out of the UK and very soft CPI numbers out of France.

We also heard rumours that ECB head Lagarde will be leaving her post early although that report had little overall impact. In the meantime, the latest Russia-Ukraine negotiations ended after two hours—not a great sign. We await to see if diplomacy wins out over force in Iran.

North American stock markets moved strongly upward in early trading on Wednesday before giving back some ground during the session. The major averages pulled back well off their session highsbut managed to end the day firmly in positive territory. The early strength on Wall Street came as Nvidia shares surged after the AI chipmaker announced a multi-year, multi-generational strategic partnership with Meta, Facebook’s parent company, spanning on-premises, cloud and AI infrastructure.

The company said the partnership will enable the large-scale deployment of Nvidia CPUs and millions of Nvidia Blackwell and Rubin GPUs. After jumping by as much as 2.9 percent, Nvidia pulled back well off its best levels of the day but still closed up by 1.6 percent. Shares of Micron also spiked by 5.3 percent on news David Tepper’s Appaloosa Management has increased its holdings in the chipmaker by 200 percent.

Positive sentiment may also have been generated in reaction to some upbeat U. S. economic data, including a Federal Reserve report showing industrial production increased by more than expected in the month of January. However, stocks pulled back off their highs following the release of the minutes of the Fed’s latest monetary policy meeting, which revealed officials remain divided about the interest rate outlook.

The minutes of the Fed’s January 27-28 meeting said several participants felt further rate cuts would likely be appropriate if inflation declined in line with their expectations. However, others believed it would likely be appropriate to leave rates unchanged for “some time” as the Fed carefully assesses incoming data. A number of these participants judged that additional policy easing may not be warranted until there was clear indication that disinflation progress was firmly back on track, the Fed said.

The Fed noted several participants even supported a two-sided description of the rate outlook, reflecting the possibility that rate increases could be appropriate if inflation remains above target levels. With the price of crude oil skyrocketing, oil service stocks turned in some of the market’s best performances on the day, resulting in a 2.7 percent surge by the Philadelphia Oil Service Index. Gold stocks also saw significant strength amid a sharp increase by the price of the precious metal, driving the NYSE Arca Gold Bugs Index up by 2.5 percent.

In the bond market, treasuries moved lower after ending the previous session nearly unchanged. Subsequently, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, climbed 2.7 basis points to 4.079 percent. Trading on Thursday may be impacted by reactions to a slew of U. S. economic data, including reports on weekly jobless claims, the trade deficit and pending home sales.
