Markets absorbed a constructive U.S. inflation report against a backdrop of mixed regional performance and persistent geopolitical uncertainty. U.S. equities closed modestly higher on balance, with the S&P 500 advancing 0.3% to 7,748.50 and the Nasdaq Composite rising 0.5% to 26,588.49. The Dow Jones Industrial Average finished essentially unchanged, down less than 0.1% at 53,770.27. The session reflected measured relief that inflation data aligned with expectations, tempered by the ongoing overhang from energy-market disruptions.

Asian equity markets delivered a mixed outcome. Strength in Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix propelled the Kospi higher by nearly 4%, while technology names also supported the Nikkei following the prior holiday. The ASX, by contrast, closed lower after Commonwealth Bank of Australia highlighted slower domestic growth. Late in the session, headlines citing Pakistani sources suggested the United States and Iran had agreed to extend their ceasefire ahead of a 60-day deadline. Iranian officials quickly pushed back, stating to Reuters that no such discussions were underway. The conflicting signals reinforce the fragile and fluid nature of any potential de-escalation.

European markets finished modestly lower. German inflation printed in line with expectations at 2.8% year-over-year—still elevated relative to the ECB's objective, particularly should Strait of Hormuz disruptions prove prolonged. Offsetting this macro caution, the European earnings season has been notably robust, with year-over-year profit growth approaching 22%, the strongest in recent years.

In the United States, leadership remained concentrated in AI-related names. Stronger-than-expected results and guidance from companies such as Super Micro Computer, alongside continued strength in Nvidia, underscored sustained corporate investment in AI infrastructure. The July CPI report showed headline prices rising 0.1% month-over-month, lowering the annual rate to 3.4% from 3.5%. Core CPI increased 0.2% and stood at 2.5% year-over-year. A 1.5% monthly decline in energy prices helped contain the overall print, even as energy remains sharply higher on an annual basis.

Treasury yields eased in response, with the 10-year yield moving to 4.68% from 4.70%. Market-implied probability of a September Federal Reserve rate hike declined to approximately 40% from near even odds the prior day. While the softer inflation data provides the Fed greater flexibility to remain on hold, the 10-year yield remains meaningfully above its pre-conflict level of 3.97%, reflecting the lasting imprint of elevated energy prices.

Geopolitical developments continue to anchor the energy complex. Limited progress toward restoring normal flows through the Strait of Hormuz, combined with reports of shipping incidents, has kept crude prices firm—WTI in the low-to-mid $83s and Brent near the high $80s. Sustained higher oil prices introduce upside risks to the disinflation trajectory in the months ahead.

The current environment continues to favor discipline and selectivity. The benign CPI print reduces near-term policy pressure and supports measured exposure to quality growth and AI infrastructure themes that retain fundamental support. Equity valuations, however, leave limited margin for error should growth disappoint or energy-driven inflation reaccelerate. We maintain a preference for companies with durable free-cash-flow generation and genuine pricing power, while remaining cautious toward pure duration and highly leveraged balance sheets. Relative-value opportunities and a readiness to add capital on material weakness remain preferred over aggressive directional positioning.

Near-term focus centers on further clarity regarding Hormuz negotiations, the upcoming PPI release, and residual earnings. Until greater resolution emerges on both the geopolitical and inflation fronts, we continue to manage risk with a controlled posture.
