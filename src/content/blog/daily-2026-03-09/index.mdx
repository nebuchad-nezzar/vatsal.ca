March 9, 2026

WTI surged just shy of $120 per barrel early on overnight and if you guessed that dampened sentiment and put downward pressure on equities, you guessed right. Asian indices were lower across the board with the Kospi (-6%) and the Nikkei (-5.2%) leading the decline. Mainland Chinese investors snapped up a record HK$ 37.2B of stocks today as markets plumbed the lows but that only cushioned the blow for the Hang Seng and stocks in mainland China, they were “only” down -.7 to -1.35%.

On another note, CPI data out of China is finally showing signs of life coming in at a much stronger than expected 1.3% YoY. Meanwhile, the PBOC set the Yuan reference rate at 6.9158, the strongest fix since late April 2023. I wouldn’t call it a rebound, it’s more like clawing back losses but stocks have fared better in Europe after reports that the G7 might band together to release strategic oil reserves.

Apparently, G7 Finance Ministers will coordinate an emergency meeting today to “discuss the possible release of IEA strategic oil reserves”, there are a couple of disclaimers in there but where there is smoke there is fire and let’s face it, crude at $120 has stagflationary written all over it so no doubt a release is coming. To put things in perspective, reports note that the G7 and IEA are weighing an emergency oil release of 300-400 million barrels – roughly double the volume released during 2022 at the start of the Ukraine war. Of course, those barrels must be bought back someday.

North American stock markets moved sharply lower in early trading on Monday but showed a substantial turnaround during the session. The major averages climbed well off their worst levels and into positive territory, with the tech-heavy Nasdaq leading the recovery. Early in the session, the Dow tumbled by as much as 1.9 percent and the Nasdaq and the S&P 500 both slumped by as much as 1.5 percent, hitting their worst intraday levels in over three months.

The late-day rally on Wall Street came after President Donald Trump purportedly told a CBS News reporter the U.S. war with Iran could be over soon. In a post on X, CBS News’ Senior White House Correspondent Weijia Jiang said Trump told her, “I think the war is very complete, pretty much.

They have no navy, no communications, they’ve got no Air Force.” Trump also said that the U.S. is “very far” ahead of his initial four-to-five-week estimated timeframe, according to Jiang. In a separate post, Jiang said Trump told her that he is thinking about taking over the Strait of Hormuz, resulting in a nosedive by the price of crude oil.

An extended surge by the price of crude oil contributed to early weakness on Wall Street, with crude spiking above $100 a barrel for the first time since 2022 and reaching a high of nearly $120 a barrel. The continued increase followed reports that major oil producers Iraq, Kuwait and the United Arab Emirates are cutting production. With the Strait of Hormuz effectively closed due to Iranian threats against tankers, the countries are reportedly running out of storage space.

Semiconductor stocks helped lead the turnaround on Wall Street, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index spiked 3.9 percent after tumbling as much as 2 percent to a two-month intraday low. In the bond market, Treasuries recovered from early weakness to end the day roughly flat. As a result, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, crept up by less than a basis point to 4.136 percent after reaching a high of 4.177 percent.
