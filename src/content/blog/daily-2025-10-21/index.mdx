October 21, 2025

President Trump has said that potential tariffs of 155% on China will come on November 1st if there is no trade deal. Clearly the market isn’t too concerned with the Hang Seng and stocks in Shanghai up .65% and 1.36% respectively overnight. The President did make further comments that he expects to make a fair deal with President Xi and I suppose the market feels there will either be an agreement or some kind of TACO extension/delay in tariff implementation.

Meanwhile the Chinese press reports that future cuts to China’s Loan Prime Rates are on the table and the PBOC continues to fix the Yuan stronger with a reference rate of 7.0930 last night, the best level for the yuan in more than a year. Keep in mind, China’s Fourth Plenum wraps up on Thursday, there is the typical talk about spurring growth and boosting consumption but as usual details will probably be light. Equity indices were up across the board in the Asian session with Australia posting strong gains after yesterday’s U.

S. critical minerals deal. The Nikkei traded higher once again as there were two firsts in Japan with Takaichi officially becoming Japan’s first female PM and Satsuki Katayama becoming the country’s first female Finance Minister (she thinks the “real” value for USDJPY is somewhere around 120-130 FYI). European bourses are all in the green today shrugging off news reports from CNN that President Trump’s push for a quick meeting with Russian President Putin may be delayed.

Apparently the White House sees no indication that Putin is prepared to offer meaningful concessions so there is little point to the two leaders getting together. After moving notably higher over the two previous sessions, North American stock markets turned in a relatively lackluster performance during trading on Tuesday. The Nasdaq and the S&P 500 spent the day bouncing back and forth across the unchanged line, although the narrower Dow showed a more significant increase to reach a new record closing high.

The advance by the Dow partly reflected a sharp increase by shares of 3M, with the multinational conglomerate spiking by 7.7 percent after reporting better than expected third quarter results. Beverage giant Coca-Cola also surged by 4.1 percent after reporting third quarter results that exceeded analyst estimates on both the top and bottom lines. Dow components Salesforce, Amazon and Sherwin-Williams also posted significant gains on the day.

Meanwhile, the lack of direction shown by the broader markets came as traders took a breather following the recent upward move. While the corporate earnings season is off to a positive start, traders may have been reluctant to continue buying stocks amid lingering concerns about U. S.-China trade tensions and the ongoing government shutdown.

A lack of major U. S. economic data due in part to the shutdown may also have kept some traders on the sidelines ahead of the release of closely watched consumer price inflation data on Friday. With most other economic reports indefinitely delayed, the inflation data could impact the outlook for interest rates ahead of the Federal Reserve’s monetary policy meeting next week.

Most of the major sectors ended the day showing only modest moves, although oil service stocks moved sharply higher, driving the Philadelphia Oil Service Index up by 2.3 percent. Halliburton helped to lead the sector higher, soaring by 11.6 percent after reporting better than expected third quarter results. Retail stocks also saw notable strength, while gold stocks plummeted along with the price of the precious metal, dragging the NYSE Arca Gold Bugs Index down by 10.0 percent.

In the bond market, treasuries extended the upward move seen over the course of the previous session. Subsequently, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, fell by 2.3 basis points to a one-year closing low of 3.963 percent. Trading on Wednesday may be impacted by reaction to the latest earnings news, with Netflix, Capital One and Mattel among the companies releasing their quarterly results after the close of today’s trading. AT&T, Hilton and Winnebago are also among the companies due to report their quarterly results before the start of trading on Wednesday.
