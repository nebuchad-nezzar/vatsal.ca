June 5, 2026

The overnight session did not start off on a positive note with equity markets down across the board in Asia led by a 5.5% drop in the Kospi. Yes, by now you’ve heard all the jokes about the market literally taking some “chips” off the table. The Broadcom results from earlier this week are still causing some concern and of course investors are also cashing in to get ready to invest in the upcoming SpaceX IPO as well.

The Kospi is still up 93% YTD, why not take some profit and help make Elon a trillionaire. The Korean Won was also under pressure falling to its lowest level since the GFC days back in 2009. So much tech, such a weak currency.

South Korea’s Finance Minister warned that authorities will respond if FX volatility increases – as if it’s not bad enough already. Speaking of weak currencies, USDJPY continues to hover around 160 despite better than expected earnings data last night. It is the first time in decades that the country has seen wage growth greater than 3% in three consecutive months and the print just reinforced a BoJ rate hike later this month.

European bourses retreated from the open following underwhelming data out of the Eurozone. EU Q1 GDP was revised notably lower to -.2% QoQ moving into contraction for the first time since 2021. With the ongoing conflict in Iran and still high energy prices the odds have to be improving unfortunately.

An ECB rate hike next week won’t help. North American stock markets ended the week on a sour note as investors digested a stronger-than-expected May jobs report, sending stocks lower, pressuring bitcoin below $60,000, and dragging down shares of Broadcom despite better-than-expected quarterly results. The U.

S. labor market showed unexpected strength in May as nonfarm payrolls increased by 172,000—more than double economists’ forecasts—while the unemployment rate held steady at 4.3%. Bitcoin extended its recent slide, falling below the $60,000 level on Friday for the first time since September 2024 as risk-off sentiment intensified across global markets and fueled broad selling pressure in digital assets. Shares of Broadcom fell sharply this week as investors reacted to Friday’s broader market selloff despite the company reporting fiscal second-quarter results that topped expectations, including adjusted earnings of $2.44 per share and revenue of $22.19B. For the week, the tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite fell -4.68%, and the benchmark S&P lost -2.59%, while the blue-chip Dow lost -0.32%.
