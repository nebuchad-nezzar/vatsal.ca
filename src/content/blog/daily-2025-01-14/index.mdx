January 14, 2025

Overnight, Asian equity indices ex-mainland China finished the session higher led by another 1.5% gain in the Nikkei. Of course, thoughts of an upcoming election, a stronger, more stable Takaichi led government and more stimulus in the pipeline are lending a helping hand. A weaker currency is probably helping as well but keep an eye on that one, the verbal intervention ramped up in Japan last night and maybe 160 is a line in the sand for Japanese officials.

Similarly, officials in South Korea are making noise about too much currency weakness as well as the won has suffered ten straight days of losses. Weaker currencies help make exports cheaper and that probably isn’t making Treasury Secretary Bessent too happy either. Chinese trade data released last night saw a new record monthly surplus of $114 billion with exports rising by a greater than expected 6.6% YoY.

It should be noted that exports to the U. S. remain significantly lower though as it seems everyone else is soaking up all the cheap Chinese goods. Chinese equities gave up their earlier gains after the Beijing and Shenzhen stock exchanges simultaneously announced they would raise their margin ratios (from 80% to 100%) for the first time since 2015.

The war in Ukraine rages on, France is in yet another political deadlock and President Trump is still talking about taking Greenland yet somehow, European bourses were only modestly lower today. Resource stocks are leading gains as commodity prices remain buoyant. Geopolitics were front and center later today as the foreign ministers of Greenland and Denmark met with Vice President JD Vance  and Secretary of State Marco Rubio to discuss the future of Greenland and the role the U.

S. will play in it. Greenland’s PM Nielsen ruled out joining the U. S. and has said the territory prefers to be in a union with Denmark.

Following the modest pullback seen during Tuesday’s session, North American stock markets saw further downside during the trading day on Wednesday. The weakness may partly have reflected growing concerns about rising geopolitical tensions around the world. President Donald Trump’s threats to take control of Greenland have made headlines recently, while traders are also keeping an eye on political unrest in Iran and the ongoing Russia-Ukraine war.

A slump in Wells Fargo shares also weighed on the markets, as the financial services giant plunged by 4.6 percent. Wells Fargo came under pressure after the company reported fourth-quarter earnings that were better than expected, but revenues were weaker than expected. Shares of Bank of America also tumbled by 3.8 percent even though the company reported fourth quarter results that exceeded analyst estimates.

Citigroup also moved significantly downward, even though the company reported better than expected fourth quarter results. On the U. S. economic front, the Commerce Department released a report showing retail sales in the U.

S. increased by more than expected in the month of November. The Commerce Department said retail sales climbed by 0.6 percent in November after edging down by a revised 0.1 percent in October. Economists had expected retail sales to rise by 0.4 percent compared to the unchanged reading originally reported for the previous month.

Excluding sales by motor vehicle and parts dealers, retail sales grew by 0.5 percent in November after inching up by 0.2 percent in October. Ex-auto sales were expected to increase by 0.4 percent. A separate report released by the Labor Department showed a modest increase by U.

S. producer prices in the month of November. Software stocks showed a substantial move to the downside on the day, dragging the Dow Jones U. S.

Software Index down by 2.4 percent to its lowest closing level in eight months. Considerable weakness was also visible among networking stocks, as reflected by the 1.6 percent loss posted by the NYSE Arca Networking Index. In the bond market, treasuries saw further upside after ending the previous session modestly higher. As a result, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, fell 3.1 basis points to 4.140 percent.
