February 4, 2026

AMD reported after the bell last night. The results fell short of expectations and as we typically see after less-than-stellar earnings, the stock is being punished, currently down about 15% today. AMD hasn’t really affected the broader market though with equities mostly higher overnight.

The Asian session saw the Kospi hit another record high with the index up 27% YTD. The Hang Seng and stocks in mainland China were higher helped by better than expected Chinese services (35 month of expansion) and composite PMI data. Of note we saw the strongest yuan fix (6.9533) from the PBOC since May 2023.

The Nikkei underperformed despite reports that PM Takaichi will win a healthy majority in the Lower House in the upcoming election. European bourses were mostly higher today, excluding Germany, as the DAX was weighed down by software names like SAP. Eurozone PMI data was a touch softer than expected but remains expansionary while EU core CPI data was a bit lower than expected (2.2%).

After weighing on the markets during Tuesday’s session, technology stocks moved significantly lower again during trading on Wednesday. The tech-heavy Nasdaq slumped to its lowest closing level in over a month after flirting with its record highs just a week ago. The Dow advanced amid a sharp increase in Amgen shares, as the biotech company spiked by 8.2 percent after reporting better than expected fourth quarter results. 3M and Nike also surged by more than 5 percent, contributing to the blue-chip index’s rebound.

Meanwhile, traders continued to rotate out of the tech sector, and semiconductor stocks showed a substantial move to the downside. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index plunged by 4.4 percent, pulling back further off the record closing high set last Thursday. Advanced Micro Devices helped lead the sector lower, plummeting by 17.3 percent after reporting better than expected fourth quarter results but providing first quarter guidance that disappointed some analysts.

On the other hand, pharmaceutical stocks saw significant strength on the day, driving the NYSE Arca Pharmaceutical Index up by 3.1 percent. In U.S. economic news, payroll processor ADP released a report showing private sector employment in the U.S. increased by much less than expected in the month of January. ADP said private sector employment rose by 22,000 jobs in January after climbing by a downwardly revised 37,000 jobs in December. Economists had expected private sector employment to grow by 45,000 jobs compared to the 41,000 jobs originally reported for the previous month.

A separate report released by the Institute for Supply Management showed its reading on U.S. manufacturing activity. Service sector activity remained unchanged in January.

The ISM said its services PMI came in at 53.8 in January, unchanged from the revised reading in December. A reading above 50 indicates growth in the sector. Economists had expected the services PMI to edge down to 53.5 from the 54.4 originally reported for the previous month.

In the bond market, treasuries showed a lack of direction before closing roughly flat for the second straight day. The yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, crept up by less than a basis point to 4.275 percent. Trading on Thursday may be impacted by reaction to earnings news from Alphabet, as the Google parent is among the companies releasing their quarterly results after today’s close.

Traders are also likely to keep an eye on the latest U.S. economic data, including reports on job openings and weekly jobless claims.
