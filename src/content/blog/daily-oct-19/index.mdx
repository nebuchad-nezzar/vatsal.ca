The Nasdaq Composite gained 1.11% to 26,430.89 and the S&P 500 rose 0.41% to 7,690.73, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average slipped 0.07% to 51,966.03. It was a clean technology rebound after last week’s worst stretch since June, but the arithmetic keeps it in perspective: the Nasdaq regained only about a fifth of its decline from the October 6 record, and the S&P 500 about the same share. The Nasdaq remains roughly 4% below its peak and the S&P 500 about 1.6% below. The VIX closed last week near 20 after jumping more than 21% on Friday, and the Nasdaq recorded 263 new 52-week lows to 49 highs, so the market is bouncing from a position of stress, not strength.

**What Led, and What Didn’t**

The leadership tells you this is a relief rally. SanDisk rose more than 5.5% after falling over 22% in the prior month, and semiconductor and cloud-infrastructure names led the gains. Amazon climbed about 5% to $262.45 after Morgan Stanley named it a top pick with a $350 price target, a sell-side call and not a fundamental development, ahead of its October 29 earnings. Meta and Apple, two of the largest stocks, finished slightly lower, and the Dow was flat, so the advance was confined to the part of the market that had fallen most. Beaten-down groups rebound first, but sustained recoveries need a reason, and nothing about AI financing was resolved today. CoreWeave, whose shares fell more than 25% last week over debt concerns, remains the stress test for that issue.

**Three Regimes in Five Sessions**

The relationship between stocks and bonds has flipped several times in a week, which we think is the most useful analytical point of the session. On Wednesday, a hot CPI sent yields and equities in opposite directions, with equities falling on higher rates. On Thursday and Friday, stocks fell while Treasuries rallied, as investors sought safety from AI risk: the 10-year yield dropped 14.6 basis points last week, its biggest weekly decline in seven months, despite hot CPI and PPI readings. Today both moved together in the risk-on direction, with stocks rising and the 10-year yield up 4.2 basis points to about 5.14%. Three different correlation regimes within five sessions means the market is trading whatever its dominant fear is on a given day, whether inflation, financing or growth. In that environment, assuming bonds will hedge equities, or the reverse, is the mistake to avoid.

**Oil: Read the Contract, Not the Headline**

Crude’s headline moves were misleading. U.S. benchmark West Texas Intermediate settled up 2.2% at $103.66, the highest settlement since September 15, while Brent edged down 0.3% to $109.55 after falling more than $3 earlier in the session. But WTI’s front-month contract expires tomorrow and traded thinly, and the more liquid December contract actually fell 0.5% to $98.54. The compressed Brent-WTI gap on the front-month screen therefore overstates what happened. On like-for-like December pricing, the spread is still roughly $11.

The physical picture is more important than the screen. Houthi forces claimed attacks on Saudi energy, airport and military targets, including an Aramco facility at Yanbu and the Shaybah oilfield, and Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said a tanker exploded after striking a naval mine in the southern Strait of Hormuz. Kpler estimates crude flows through the region averaged about 8.5 million barrels per day last week, down from more than 20 million the week before, a drop of roughly 57%, with at least ten confirmed incidents involving commercial vessels. Yanbu matters because it is the Red Sea terminus of Saudi Arabia’s East-West pipeline, the main bypass around Hormuz, so the Houthi targeting hits the workaround as well as the chokepoint. Prices fell early after President Trump waived specified sanctions and tariffs on Russia, which raised expectations of additional Russian fuel exports, but those declines faded by the close. As with last week’s diesel arrangement, supply headlines move oil intraday while security headlines set the closing level. One analyst noted that Iran could escalate attacks ahead of the November 3 midterm elections, and Egypt’s parliament has delayed until October 26 a vote on authorizing support for Saudi Arabia.

**One Supportive Data Point**

China reported third-quarter GDP growth of 4.5%, above the 4.4% forecast and an improvement from 4.3% in the second quarter. It is a modest signal that global demand is holding up, which matters for the argument that AI skepticism is about financing and not about the broader economy.

Today as a technical bounce, not a trend change. The drivers that sold technology last week, including AI financing costs, hot inflation and Fed hike risk, remain in place, and a market that gained on rising yields and no new financing news is relying on the earnings calendar to supply the answer. Our positioning is unchanged: we favor companies that fund growth from cash flow and hold pricing power, we are cautious on leveraged AI infrastructure and on component suppliers whose valuations assume uninterrupted demand, and we regard energy as a reasonable diversifier while Gulf flows are impaired. Given three correlation regimes in a week, we would size positions with the assumption that neither bonds nor gold will reliably offset equity drawdowns. We would become more constructive if earnings this week show AI spending converting into revenue without margin erosion and credit spreads on AI borrowers tighten. We would become more defensive if guidance disappoints or if Gulf flows deteriorate further.

The week brings a heavy stretch of earnings, with Tesla, Meta and Netflix among those expected, and Amazon on October 29th. The Federal Reserve meets October 27 and 28 and is expected to hold, and Chair Warsh is scheduled to speak beforehand. WTI’s front-month expiry tomorrow may distort oil prices, so we will watch the December contract. We are also tracking Houthi activity around Yanbu and Hormuz transit volumes, the details and durability of the Russia sanctions waiver, and credit spreads for CoreWeave and Oracle.
