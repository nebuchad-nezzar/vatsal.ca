February 2, 2026

To say the overnight session got off to a shaky start in Asia would be an understatement with stocks finishing lower across the board. The Kospi led the way falling about 5% with tech names under pressure. Sentiment took a hit on the back of two stories.

First, news that Oracle plans to raise somewhere in the neighbourhood of $50 billion in calendar year 2026 to build more cloud infrastructure capacity. Normally, that might be seen as good news but the company plans to raise about half of its funding through equity-linked and common equity issuances so there is concern about potential earnings dilution. The second story was likely more impactful with reports that Nvidia’s plan to invest $100 billion in OpenAI was “on ice”.

According to the WSJ, Nvidia’s plan to invest $100B in OpenAI to help it run its AI models has stalled after some at NVDA have expressed doubts about the deal. The companies unveiled the giant agreement last September and at the time, OpenAI expected the negotiations to be completed within weeks but the talks haven’t progressed beyond the early stages. The report says that Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has privately emphasized that the original $100bio agreement was nonbinding and not finalized.

He also criticized what he described as a lack of discipline in OpenAI’s business approach and expressed concern over competition. There was nowhere to hide in Asia with the Nikkei down about 1.25% and the Hang Seng and stocks in mainland China down well over 2%. Chinese government PMI data was less than encouraging with both the manufacturing and non-manufacturing numbers weaker than expected, slipping back into contractionary territory.

Despite the poor start in Asia, European bourses managed to have a winning day. Maybe sentiment has been boosted by more cordial Iran-U.S. relations with President Trump saying he’s hopeful that “we’ll make a deal”.

Certainly, oil markets are pricing in less of a chance of military action today with crude down about 5%. Sentiment may also have improved due to a steadying in the precious metals market, better-than-expected German retail sales data and broadly better than expected PMI data out of Europe this morning. Following mixed performance last week, North American stock markets moved mostly higher during trading on Monday.

All major averages moved upward for the day, with the Dow posting a standout gain. The strength followed the release of a report from the Institute for Supply Management showing manufacturing activity in the U.S. unexpectedly expanded for the first time in 12 months in January.

The ISM said its manufacturing PMI jumped to 52.6 in January from 47.9 in December, with a reading above 50 indicating growth. Economists had expected the index to inch up to 48.5. The upbeat economic data helped traders shrug off lingering trade tensions and renewed uncertainty over U.S. monetary policy. Stocks also benefitted from signs of de-escalating tensions between the U.S. and Iran amid reports Iran is prepared to negotiate with the U.S. over its nuclear program. The markets saw continued strength after President Donald Trump announced in a post on Truth Social that he has reached a trade deal with India. Following a conversation with Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi, Trump said the U.S. would reduced its reciprocal tariffs on India to 18 percent from 25 percent, while India purportedly agreed to reduce their tariffs and non-tariff barriers against the U.S. to zero. However, traders seemed somewhat reluctant to make more significant moves ahead of the Labor Department’s closely watched monthly jobs report on Friday.

The report, which is expected to show employment climbed by 70,000 jobs in January after rising by 50,000 jobs in December, could impact the outlook for interest rates. In the bond market, treasuries came under pressure during the session after seeing initial strength. As a result, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, climbed 3.4 basis points to 4.275 percent. Trading on Tuesday may be impacted by reaction to the Labor Department’s report on December job openings.
