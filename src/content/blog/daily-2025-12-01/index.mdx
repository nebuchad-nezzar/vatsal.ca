December 1, 2025

Equity indices were mixed in Asia with the Nikkei leading the way lower dropping almost 2% after BoJ Governor Ueda sounded more hawkish over the weekend and hinted at a rate hike as early as this month. PMI data out of China was less than inspiring with the official government manufacturing print remaining in contraction for the eighth month in a row while the private RatingDog PMI number was weaker than expected sliding into contractionary territory for the first time in three months. Despite the sub-par Chinese data, the Hang Seng and stocks in mainland China managed to finish the session in the green.

I suppose one bright spot: the private PMI data showed that with China and the U.S. agreeing to a trade truce in October, new export orders expanded at the quickest pace in eight months. European bourses were lower across the board today despite U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio noting U.S. and Ukrainian negotiators had “very productive” discussions over the weekend.

As we have come to realize “productive” = pretty standard terminology and the real test comes later today/tomorrow with U.S. envoy Witkoff off to Russia to meet with Putin. If Putin says “nyet” all the U.S.-Ukraine talks don’t matter all that much. Eurozone PMI data was more or less inline this morning and that means manufacturing data for the EU as a whole remains in contractionary territory. After coming under pressure early in the session, North American stock markets staged a recovery attempt in mid-day trading on Monday but moved back to the downside as the day progressed.

The major averages all finished the session firmly in negative territory. The weakness comes as some traders looked to cash in on last week’s rally, which saw the major averages stage a significant recovery from the sharp pullback seen earlier in November. The major averages had closed higher for five consecutive sessions, clawing their way back toward their record highs.

Stocks have recently benefited from renewed optimism about the outlook for interest rates following dovish comments from leading Federal Reserve officials. CME Group’s FedWatch Tool is currently indicating an 87.6 percent chance the Fed will lower interest rates by another quarter point at its monetary policy meeting next week. However, the release of key U.S. economic data in the coming days could impact Fed officials’ thinking, potentially keeping traders on edge. The Institute for Supply Management released a report this morning unexpectedly showing a modest decrease by its reading on U.S. manufacturing activity in the month of November.

The ISM said its manufacturing PMI slipped to 48.2 in November from 48.7 in October, with a reading below 50 indicating contraction. Economists had expected the index to inch up to 49.0. In the bond market, treasuries moved notably lower, extending the pullback seen during last Friday’s session. Subsequently, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, jumped 7.9 basis points to 4.096 percent.
