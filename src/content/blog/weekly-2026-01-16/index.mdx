January 16, 2026

It was another eventful week for investors, and once again many of headlines emanated from Washington DC and the White House. Concerns surrounding Federal Reserve independence resurfaced after Fed Chairman Powell responded to last Friday’s announced DoJ criminal investigation into building renovation overruns. Initially long dated yields moved up and stocks were heavy but quickly President Trump distanced himself from the DoJ decision, while it was repeatedly rebuked by Democrats as well as some Republicans on Capitol Hill.

The President stepped-up rhetoric against the Iranian government for its heavy handed response to protests, threatening military action which drove up global oil prices early in the week. Crude price rolled over after Trump seemed to walk back some of his threats back on reports that Iranian leadership had cancelled scheduled hangings. The President also continued to express his desire to commandeer Greenland for national security purposes, butting heads with NATO officials and threatening tariffs on nations that oppose his designs.

Regarding domestic policy, the White House’s latest populist lean gained momentum, seemly turning further against Corporate America in an attempt to champion affordability ahead of the mid-term elections. Credit card stocks got slammed after Trump mused about a 10% rate cap. Homebuilders were volatile after FHA Director Pulte demanded the industry halt share buybacks and threatened to withhold GSE financing access unless they did more to lower housing costs.

On Friday, the Administration introduced an initiative around power grid operator PJM, intended to cap short term power prices for consumers by shifting more of the burden onto hyperscalers through longer term power agreements. Also on Friday, Trump may have tipped his hand regarding the Fed Chair pick, when betting odds for the Fed’s Warsh jumped, following his latest off the cuff comments regarding NEC Director Hassett at the White House. At the same event, Trump formally announced his grand healthcare plan that he promised would greatly reduce prescription costs, increase transparency in the system, and cut out the middle men.

Next week investors should expect more of the same when the President and much of his top brass descend upon the globalists at Davos. U.S. economic readings were notably constructive for growth this week, and key inflation readings signaled some progress towards the Fed’s target.

Nevertheless Fed expectations shifted marginally in a hawkish direction with futures projecting the Fed will remain on hold through Q1. December core CPI undershot expectations by 0.1% in an encouraging signal that prices were headed back towards their 2% target. November PPI was mixed but rising goods — food in particular — drove-up the annualized number above expectations.

November retail sales topped expectations, though October was revised lower. January Empire manufacturing and Philadelphia Fed business outlook data easily topped expectations including improvement in new orders. Overseas, China unveiled a slew of monetary stimulus measures Thursday morning, including a reduction to its main relending rate.

The Yen remained notably volatile, prone to reacting to spats of verbal intervention by officials and various sourced Japanese press reports. For the week, crude prices finished up ~1% after being up as much as 5% on Iran fears. Silver added another 11% to trade above $90/oz for the first time, and gold tacked on 2%.

Bitcoin climbed nearly 4% to test 95K. The U.S. 10-year yield broke out to highest level since September, climbing above resistance at 4.2%.

Small caps and the S&P equal weight continued to outperform highlighting the broadening in equity markets that has gained momentum in early 2026. For the week, the S&P slipped 0.4%, the DJIA fell 0.3%, and the Nasdaq lost 0.7%. European bourses were mixed with the German DAX edging 0.14% higher while the French CAC40 retreated 1.23%.

Asia Pacific stock markets were once again bifurcated, with mainland China losing 0.45% while Japan surged 3.84%. Your BSD Global Tech Hedge Fund slipped 0.2% this past week. Bank stocks were in focus as the new earnings season began, and for the most part the numbers were good following the three recent Fed rate cuts.

Goldman Sachs reported strong earnings and raised its dividend, and though revenue was saddled with a markdown from giving up the Apple credit card business, investors took heart from the M&A advisory business going gangbusters with the promise of even bigger deals in 2026. Morgan Stanley posted record quarterly results, with equity trading fees up 10% in Q4, and 28% for the fiscal year. JP Morgan also had outstanding results, but the stock took some lumps from President Trump’s push to lower credit card interest rates.

Bank of America’s shares also trended lower on the week despite modestly beating earnings expectations. Elsewhere, Taiwan Semi reported blow out numbers again, keeping the AI/semiconductor narrative alive and strong, despite the White House talking about imposing broader tariffs on chip imports. Micron comments also drew some attention as the company said that memory shortages won’t improve before 2028.

A press report also suggested that rising AI powerhouses OpenAI and Anthropic have taken further steps toward IPOs. In M&A this week, medical device maker Penumbra struck a deal to be acquired by Boston Scientific in a $14.5B deal. The holiday-shortened week is set to begin relatively quietly, with U.S. markets shut on January 19 for Martin Luther King, Jr. Day. The economic calendar for Tuesday and Wednesday is muted; most key data releases are scheduled for the latter part of the week.

The Q/Q GDP data, initial jobless claims and the PCE price index for November are due on Thursday. On Friday, S&P Global PMI data for January and Michigan consumer sentiment data are scheduled for release. The World Economic Forum kicks off its annual event in Davos, with President Trump expected to appear amid a standoff with EU nations over Greenland.

Notable executives on the Davos program include Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella, Salesforce CEO Marc Benioff, PepsiCo CEO Ramon Laguarta, JPMorgan Chase CEO Jamie Dimon, and Goldman Sachs CEO David Solomon. Earnings seasons continues to ramp up, with Netflix, 3M, J&J, Kinder Morgan, Intel, Visa, GE Aerospace and P&G, releasing their quarterly financial results.
