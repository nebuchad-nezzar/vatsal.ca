Equities extended their advance for a second straight session on Tuesday as the AI supply chain received another massive capital injection from Wall Street and diplomatic activity in the Middle East took a modest edge off crude prices. The S&P 500 rose 0.60% (+45.83 points) to 7,722.60, the Nasdaq 100 climbed 1.27% to 29,645.23 (with the Nasdaq Composite up 1.18% to 26,477.81), and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index surged 3.60%. The Russell 2000 rebounded 1.16% to 2,818.87 as Brent crude’s pullback allowed the 10-year Treasury yield to slip half a basis point to 4.995%, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average finished essentially flat at 52,377.57 (+0.03%, or +15.99 points) only because a 10.7% post-earnings drop in Boeing masked strength across technology and financials. Volatility continued to compress ahead of tomorrow’s Federal Reserve decision, with the VIX falling 4.73% to 17.52.

### Wall Street’s $63 Billion Answer to Anthropic’s S-1

Yesterday I wrote that Anthropic’s Form S-1—which disclosed more than $600 billion in non-cancellable multi-year compute commitments alongside $16.3 billion in first-half revenue—proved that internal cash flows alone can no longer finance the AI infrastructure buildout. It took less than 24 hours for Wall Street and private credit to show how that commitment table will be funded.

Today, a coalition of money-center banks (including Bank of America, Citigroup, and Morgan Stanley) alongside Blackstone organized a $63 billion debt and equipment-financing vehicle structured specifically to fund AI computing infrastructure leased by Anthropic. Crucially, the facility is anchored around custom AI accelerators (XPUs) and high-speed networking hardware supplied by Broadcom (AVGO), sending Broadcom shares up 4.41% to $343.68 and igniting a 3.60% rally across the semiconductor index.

In last week’s commentary, I noted that the next phase of the AI cycle would move off clean corporate balance sheets and into structured credit, project finance, and public equity syndication. That transition is now happening in real time: private credit and Wall Street syndicates are underwriting tens of billions of dollars in special-purpose compute vehicles, and the cash flows immediately pass through to the silicon toll-collectors.

### Finance, FinTech, and Aerospace & Defense Scorecards

Alongside the $63 billion AI credit facility and a stronger-than-expected October Consumer Confidence print of 91.7, today’s earnings across global payments, financial data infrastructure, and defense aerospace highlighted strong institutional cash generation and record defense backlogs—contrasted against commercial aerospace execution drag.

### [V] Visa Inc. (Q4 FY2026)

Global payment volumes and cross-border flows remained resilient through fiscal year-end, though shares edged lower after hours on conservative low-double-digit FY2027 guidance.

* Q4 Net Revenue: $12.1B (+13% YoY nominal and constant-dollar), matching consensus; full-year FY2026 net revenue reached a record $46.0B (+15% YoY)
* Q4 Earnings Per Share: Non-GAAP EPS of $3.48 (+17% YoY), beating the $3.44 consensus (GAAP EPS of $3.19, +22% YoY); full-year Non-GAAP EPS reached $13.27 (+16% YoY)
* Network Volume Metrics (Constant-Dollar YoY):
  * Total Payments Volume: $4.73T (+9% YoY)
  * Cross-Border Volume: +14% YoY (both total and excluding intra-Europe), accelerating on international travel and e-commerce
  * Processed Transactions: +10% YoY
* Capital Return: Raised quarterly cash dividend by 16% to $0.780/share and returned $5.2B via buybacks and dividends in Q4.

### [SPGI] S&P Global Inc. (Q3 2026)

Index and ratings outperformance drove a Q3 beat and full-year guidance upgrade alongside a major strategic review to potentially separate Capital IQ.

* Q3 Revenue & Margin: $3.678B (+7% YoY vs. ~$3.64B consensus), with adjusted operating margin expanding 60 bps YoY to 53.5%
* Q3 Earnings Per Share: Adjusted diluted EPS of $4.58 (+9% YoY), topping the $4.42–$4.45 consensus (GAAP diluted EPS of $4.45, +26% YoY)
* Segment Revenue Breakdown:
  * S&P Dow Jones Indices: $554M (+20% YoY)
  * S&P Global Ratings: $1.271B (+2% YoY)
  * S&P Global Market Intelligence: $1.263B (+7% YoY)
  * S&P Global Energy: $645M (+6% YoY)
* Strategic Portfolio Action & Capital Return: Initiated a formal strategic review—including a potential separation—for Capital IQ and associated Kensho Data & Platforms (while retaining RatingsDirect, RatingsXpress, and Enterprise Solutions); executed $2.6B in Q3 share repurchases and raised FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance to $17.70–$17.95 (from $17.50–$17.75).

### [PYPL] PayPal Holdings, Inc. (Q3 2026)

Aggressive share repurchases and strict operating expense discipline delivered an 8% bottom-line beat and a full-year EPS upgrade despite softer top-line international growth.

* Q3 Net Revenue: $8.58B (+2% reported / +1% FX-neutral), slightly below consensus estimates of ~$8.71B
* Q3 Earnings Per Share: Non-GAAP EPS of $1.45 (+8% YoY), beating the $1.31 consensus by $0.14 (GAAP EPS of $1.25)
* Profitability & Cash Flow Drivers:
  * Transaction Margin Dollars: $4.0B (+2% YoY), supported by double-digit growth across Venmo and Braintree and just 1% growth in non-transaction operating expenses
  * Capital Allocation: Repurchased $1.5B of common stock during Q3
* Full-Year 2026 Outlook Raised: Upgraded full-year Non-GAAP EPS guidance to ~$5.60 (up from ~$5.38), with transaction margin dollars guided to ~$15.7B and adjusted free cash flow of at least $6.5B.

### [BA] The Boeing Company (Q3 2026)

Defense, Space & Security revenue jumped 15% YoY to a record $101B backlog, but commercial 737 production delays into early 2027 sent shares down 10.7% to $168.18 and capped the Dow.

* Consolidated Q3 Results: Revenue of $24.19B (+4% YoY); non-GAAP core loss of $(0.34)/share (narrowed from $(7.47) in Q3 2025, though wider than consensus; GAAP net loss of $116M or $(0.26)/share); positive free cash flow of $464M ($1.5B operating cash flow)
* Defense, Space & Security (BDS) Outperformance:
  * BDS Revenue & Margin: $7.94B (+15% YoY from $6.9B in Q3 2025), generating $492M in segment operating earnings (6.2% operating margin)
  * Record Defense Backlog: Reached a record $101B (23% international), prior to incorporating the U.S. Navy’s selection of Boeing for the next-generation F/A-XX carrier fighter program
* Commercial Airplanes & 737 Timeline: Delivered 161 commercial aircraft and secured FAA type certification for both the 737-7 and 737-10, but CEO Kelly Ortberg pushed the target 737 production rate of 47 jets/month out to early 2027.

### Oil, the 10-Year Yield, and Day One of the FOMC

In energy markets, December Brent crude fell 1.67% ($1.93) to settle at $113.88 per barrel, while WTI traded near $100–$101. The second consecutive day of crude relief followed diplomatic visits to Tehran by officials from Saudi Arabia, Turkey, and Pakistan alongside conditional de-escalation rhetoric from Iranian leadership. Gold held steady at $4,026.00 (-0.12%), consolidating just above the $4,000 psychological floor.

That $3.00 two-day pullback in Brent from Friday’s $116.66 settlement gave rate-sensitive equities room to breathe today, lifting the Russell 2000 by 1.16% and nudging the 10-year Treasury yield down to 4.995%. However, as the Federal Open Market Committee convened today for the start of its two-day policy meeting, the macro picture remains governed by the same “Grind” boundaries: $113.88 Brent is still an inflationary tax on the global economy, Consumer Confidence just surprised to the upside at 91.7, and payments volume at Visa is growing 9% year-over-year. With a rate hold tomorrow at 2:00 PM ET fully priced in, the market’s focus will rest entirely on Chair Powell’s 2:30 PM ET press conference and how the Committee weighs energy-driven headline inflation against expectations for the December meeting.
