One theme connected the two biggest stories on the tape today—from frontier AI clusters to consumer FinTech: in a 5% interest-rate regime, the winners are the platforms that originate assets and syndicate the balance-sheet risk to private credit and institutional capital. The S&P 500 rose 0.60% (+45.83 points) to 7,722.60, the Nasdaq 100 climbed 1.27% to 29,645.23 (with the Nasdaq Composite up 1.18% to 26,477.81), and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index surged 3.60%. The Russell 2000 rebounded 1.16% to 2,818.87 as Brent crude’s pullback to $113.88 allowed the 10-year Treasury yield to tick down half a basis point to 4.995%. Meanwhile, the Dow Jones Industrial Average finished essentially flat at 52,377.57 (+0.03%, or +15.99 points) only because a 10.7% drop in Boeing masked strong gains across semiconductors and financials. Ahead of tomorrow’s Federal Reserve decision, the VIX fell another 4.73% to 17.52.

### Wall Street’s $63 Billion Answer to Anthropic’s S-1

Yesterday I wrote that Anthropic’s Form S-1—which disclosed more than $600 billion in non-cancellable multi-year compute commitments alongside $16.3 billion in first-half revenue—proved that internal cash flows can no longer carry the AI buildout alone. It took less than 24 hours for Wall Street and private credit to show how that commitment schedule will actually be financed.

Today, a syndicate of major banks (including Bank of America, Citigroup, and Morgan Stanley) alongside Blackstone organized a $63 billion debt and equipment-financing vehicle structured specifically to fund AI computing infrastructure leased by Anthropic. Crucially, the facility is built around custom AI accelerators (XPUs) and high-speed networking hardware supplied by Broadcom (AVGO), sending Broadcom shares up 4.41% to $343.68 and fueling a 3.60% breakout across the semiconductor index.

Look at the financial plumbing of that deal: instead of forcing a single corporate balance sheet to absorb $63 billion of depreciating silicon, Wall Street and private credit are wrapping Anthropic’s lease obligations into an off-balance-sheet project finance vehicle—and wiring the proceeds straight to Broadcom. The capital-intensive builders take on the structured lease obligations; the custom-silicon and networking suppliers collect the cash upfront.

### FinTech’s Generational Split: SoFi’s Origination Engine vs. PayPal’s Buyback Math

Today’s financial earnings delivered a textbook case study in organic operating leverage versus financial engineering. While Visa confirmed that baseline consumer and cross-border payment rails remain healthy (supported by an October Consumer Confidence beat at 91.7), the real story in FinTech was the divergence between SoFi Technologies (SOFI) and PayPal (PYPL).

SoFi is thriving in a 5% rate environment for two structural reasons: a $51.3 billion deposit base that gives it a massive funding-cost advantage, and a booming capital-light Loan Platform Business that originated $3.8 billion of loans in Q3 on behalf of third-party institutional buyers—generating $153 million in high-margin fee revenue without holding the credit risk on its own balance sheet. Contrast that with PayPal, where top-line revenue grew just 1% currency-neutral ($8.58B, missing consensus) and an 8% non-GAAP EPS beat ($1.45) had to be manufactured through $1.5 billion in quarterly share buybacks and holding non-transaction operating expense growth to 1%.

### [SOFI] SoFi Technologies, Inc. (Q3 2026)

Record 44% top-line growth, 70% EBITDA expansion, and $16.9B in quarterly loan originations paired with raised 2026 guidance and a self-funded $0.90 EPS target for 2027.

* Adjusted Net Revenue: Record $1.40B (+44% YoY; GAAP net revenue of ~$1.39B), crushing consensus estimates of $1.26B–$1.37B
* Profitability & Operating Leverage:
  * Adjusted EBITDA: Record $470M (+70% YoY), representing a 34% EBITDA margin
  * Earnings Per Share: Adjusted EPS of $0.18 (+64% YoY and +50% QoQ vs. $0.17 consensus); adjusted net income reached $243M
* Originations, Deposits & Member Scale:
  * Total Loan Originations: Record $16.9B (+71% YoY), led by $12.6B in personal loans, $2.9B in student loans, and $1.4B in home loans
  * Capital-Light Loan Platform Business: Originated $3.8B on behalf of third-party investors, generating ~$153M in fee revenue
  * Member & Deposit Base: Added a record 1.2M new members to reach 17.1M (+35% YoY) and 2.2M new products to reach 26.7M (+44% YoY), while total deposits climbed to $51.3B
* Guidance Raised & 2027 Roadmap:
  * Full-Year 2026 Upgrade: Raised adjusted net revenue to $5.04B (from $4.75B–$4.85B), adjusted EBITDA to ~$1.66B, and adjusted EPS to $0.61
  * Preliminary 2027 Outlook: Guided 2027 adjusted net revenue to $6.4B (~27% growth), adjusted EBITDA to $2.24B (~35% margin), and adjusted EPS to $0.90—fully funded via internal earnings and excess capital with no equity raise required.

### [PYPL] PayPal Holdings, Inc. (Q3 2026)

Aggressive share repurchases and strict cost discipline delivered an 8% bottom-line beat and a full-year EPS upgrade despite sluggish 1% FX-neutral top-line growth.

* Q3 Net Revenue: $8.58B (+2% reported / +1% FX-neutral), missing consensus estimates of ~$8.71B amid international headwinds
* Q3 Earnings Per Share: Non-GAAP EPS of $1.45 (+8% YoY), beating the $1.31 consensus by $0.14 (GAAP EPS of $1.25)
* Margin & Capital Return Drivers:
  * Transaction Margin Dollars: $4.0B (+2% YoY), supported by Venmo and Braintree volume alongside just 1% growth in non-transaction operating expenses
  * Share Cannibalization: Repurchased $1.5B of common stock during Q3
* Full-Year 2026 Outlook: Lifted full-year Non-GAAP EPS guidance to ~$5.60 (from ~$5.38), with transaction margin dollars guided to ~$15.7B and adjusted free cash flow of at least $6.5B.

### [V] Visa Inc. (Q4 FY2026)

Global payment volumes hit $4.73T and cross-border flows accelerated 14% YoY, though shares dipped slightly after hours on conservative low-double-digit FY2027 guidance.

* Q4 Net Revenue: $12.1B (+13% YoY nominal and constant-dollar), matching consensus; full-year FY2026 net revenue reached a record $46.0B (+15% YoY)
* Q4 Earnings Per Share: Non-GAAP EPS of $3.48 (+17% YoY), beating the $3.44 consensus (GAAP EPS of $3.19, +22% YoY); full-year Non-GAAP EPS reached $13.27 (+16% YoY)
* Network Volume Metrics (Constant-Dollar YoY):
  * Total Payments Volume: $4.73T (+9% YoY)
  * Cross-Border Volume: +14% YoY (both total and excluding intra-Europe), accelerating on international travel and cross-border e-commerce
  * Processed Transactions: +10% YoY
* Capital Return: Raised quarterly cash dividend by 16% to $0.780/share and returned $5.2B via buybacks and dividends in Q4.

### Aerospace & Defense: Boeing’s $101 Billion Defense Backlog vs. 737 Reality

In aerospace and defense, Boeing (BA) illustrated the exact tension between a generational global rearmament cycle and commercial manufacturing bottlenecks. Inside Boeing’s Defense, Space & Security (BDS) division, revenue surged 15% year-over-year to $7.94 billion and segment backlog hit an all-time record of $101 billion—and that figure does not yet include the U.S. Navy’s recent selection of Boeing for the next-generation F/A-XX carrier fighter program. Yet the equity market punished the stock by 10.7% (to $168.18) because CEO Kelly Ortberg pushed the commercial 737 production milestone of 47 jets per month out to early 2027.

### [BA] The Boeing Company (Q3 2026)

Defense revenue jumped 15% YoY to a record $101B backlog ahead of the F/A-XX fighter contract book, but pushing the 47-jet/month 737 target into early 2027 sent shares down 10.7%.

* Consolidated Q3 Results: Revenue of $24.19B (+4% YoY); non-GAAP core loss of $(0.34)/share (narrowed from $(7.47) in Q3 2025, though wider than Street expectations; GAAP net loss of $116M or $(0.26)/share); positive free cash flow of $464M ($1.5B operating cash flow)
* Defense, Space & Security (BDS) Strength:
  * BDS Revenue & Operating Profit: $7.94B (+15% YoY from $6.9B in Q3 2025), generating $492M in segment operating earnings (6.2% operating margin)
  * Record Defense Backlog: Reached $101B (23% international), prior to incorporating the U.S. Navy’s F/A-XX sixth-generation fighter award
* Commercial Airplanes & 737 Timeline: Delivered 161 commercial aircraft and secured FAA type certification for both the 737-7 and 737-10 in October, but management delayed stabilizing 737 output at 47 jets/month until early 2027.

### Oil, the 10-Year Yield, and Day One of the FOMC

In energy markets, December Brent crude fell 1.67% ($1.93) to settle at $113.88 per barrel, while WTI traded near $100–$101. The second consecutive day of crude relief followed diplomatic visits to Tehran by officials from Saudi Arabia, Turkey, and Pakistan alongside conditional de-escalation rhetoric from Iranian leadership. Gold held steady at $4,026.00 (-0.12%), consolidating just above the $4,000 psychological floor.

That $3.00 two-day pullback in Brent from Friday’s $116.66 settlement gave rate-sensitive equities room to breathe today, lifting the Russell 2000 by 1.16% and nudging the 10-year Treasury yield down to 4.995%. However, as the Federal Open Market Committee convened today for the start of its two-day policy meeting, the macro picture remains governed by the same “Grind” boundaries: $113.88 Brent is still an inflationary tax on the global economy, Consumer Confidence just surprised to the upside at 91.7, SoFi just originated $16.9 billion in quarterly consumer loans, and Visa’s payment volume is growing 9% year-over-year. With a rate hold tomorrow at 2:00 PM ET fully priced in, the market’s focus will rest entirely on Chair Kevin Warsh’s 2:30 PM ET press conference and how the Committee weighs energy-driven headline inflation against expectations for the December meeting.
