Equities extended their advance for a second straight session on Tuesday as the AI supply chain received another massive capital injection and diplomatic activity in the Middle East took a modest edge off crude prices. The S&P 500 rose 0.60% (+45.83 points) to 7,722.60, the Nasdaq 100 climbed 1.27% to 29,645.23 (with the Nasdaq Composite up 1.18% to 26,477.81), and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index surged 3.60%. The Russell 2000 rebounded 1.16% to 2,818.87 as Brent crude’s pullback allowed the 10-year Treasury yield to slip half a basis point to 4.995%, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average finished essentially flat at 52,377.57 (+0.03%, or +15.99 points) only because a 10.7% post-earnings drop in Boeing masked broad industrial and consumer strength. Volatility continued to compress ahead of tomorrow’s Federal Reserve decision, with the VIX falling 4.73% to 17.52.

### Wall Street’s $63 Billion Answer to Anthropic’s S-1

Yesterday I wrote that Anthropic’s Form S-1—which disclosed more than $600 billion in non-cancellable multi-year compute commitments alongside $16.3 billion in first-half revenue—proved that internal cash flows alone can no longer finance the AI infrastructure buildout. It took less than 24 hours for Wall Street and private credit to show how that commitment table will be funded.

Today, a coalition of money-center banks (including Bank of America, Citigroup, and Morgan Stanley) alongside Blackstone organized a $63 billion debt and equipment-financing vehicle structured specifically to fund AI computing infrastructure leased by Anthropic. Crucially, the facility is anchored around custom AI accelerators (XPUs) and high-speed networking hardware supplied by Broadcom (AVGO), sending Broadcom shares up 4.41% to $343.68 and igniting a 3.60% rally across the semiconductor index.

In last week’s commentary, I noted that the next phase of the AI cycle would move off clean corporate balance sheets and into structured credit, project finance, and public equity syndication. That transition is now happening in real time: private credit and Wall Street syndicates are underwriting tens of billions of dollars in special-purpose compute vehicles, and the cash flows immediately pass through to the silicon and optical toll-collectors.

### Physical AI and Real-Economy Scorecards

Yesterday I highlighted four earnings reports—Corning, UPS, Visa, and HCA Healthcare—as our cleanest pre-Fed read on physical data-center demand and the real economy, while Boeing provided a critical test of industrial execution. Paired with a stronger-than-expected October Consumer Confidence print of 91.7, today’s scorecards drew a sharp line between companies exercising pricing power and those still battling supply-chain friction.

### [GLW] Corning Incorporated (Q3 2026)

Optical fiber interconnect demand inside generative AI data-center clusters drove a 44% surge in Optical Communications revenue and an across-the-board Q3 beat and Q4 raise.

* Core Revenue: $5.08B (+19% YoY), beating consensus estimates of ~$5.03B (GAAP revenue of $4.86B)
* Core EPS: $0.91, topping the $0.88 Wall Street consensus (GAAP diluted EPS of $0.78)
* Segment Performance:
  * Optical Communications Revenue: $2.38B (+44% YoY), propelled by hyperscale gen-AI fiber deployments
* Q4 2026 Outlook: Guided core sales to $5.2B–$5.3B and core EPS to $0.96–$1.00.

### [UPS] United Parcel Service (Q3 2026)

Shares rallied 5.75% as disciplined yield management, B2B mix shift, and network cost reductions delivered a solid Q3 beat and a full-year guidance upgrade.

* Consolidated Revenue: $22.9B (+6.7% YoY), beating consensus expectations of ~$22.25B
* Adjusted Diluted EPS: $1.69 (vs. $1.62–$1.63 consensus; GAAP diluted EPS of $1.37)
* Unit Economics & Mix: U.S. domestic revenue per piece climbed 6.1% YoY as management intentionally shed lower-margin volume in favor of higher-yielding B2B and international shipments
* Guidance Raised:
  * Q4 2026 Targets: Consolidated revenue of ~$25.7B and adjusted operating profit of ~$3.3B
  * Full-Year 2026 Upgrade: Raised full-year revenue outlook to ~$92.6B (from ~$91.2B), adjusted operating profit to ~$8.75B, and adjusted EPS to ~$7.28 (from $7.22).

### [V] Visa Inc. (Q4 FY2026)

Global consumer spending and cross-border travel remained resilient through fiscal year-end, though shares edged lower after hours on conservative low-double-digit FY2027 guidance.

* Q4 Net Revenue: $12.1B (+13% YoY nominal and constant-dollar), matching consensus; full-year FY2026 net revenue reached a record $46.0B (+15% YoY)
* Q4 Earnings Per Share: Non-GAAP EPS of $3.48 (+17% YoY), beating the $3.44 consensus (GAAP EPS of $3.19, +22% YoY); full-year Non-GAAP EPS reached $13.27 (+16% YoY)
* Network Volume Metrics (Constant-Dollar YoY):
  * Total Payments Volume: $4.73T (+9% YoY)
  * Cross-Border Volume: +14% YoY (both total and excluding intra-Europe), accelerating on international travel and e-commerce
  * Processed Transactions: +10% YoY
* Capital Return: Raised quarterly cash dividend by 16% to $0.780/share and returned $5.2B via buybacks and dividends in Q4.

### [BA] The Boeing Company (Q3 2026)

Shares tumbled 10.7% to $168.18—single-handedly capping the Dow’s advance—after management pushed its key 737 production rate milestone into early 2027 despite positive free cash flow and FAA certifications.

* Q3 Revenue: $24.19B (+4% YoY), supported by 161 commercial aircraft deliveries
* Profitability & Cash Flow:
  * Core Loss Per Share: $(0.34) (narrowed from $(7.47) in Q3 2025, but wider than Street expectations; GAAP net loss of $116M or $(0.26)/share)
  * Free Cash Flow: Positive $464M ($1.5B operating cash flow)
* Production & Certification Update: While the FAA granted amended type certification for the 737-7 and official type certification for the 737-10 in October, CEO Kelly Ortberg disclosed that stabilizing 737 output at the targeted 47 jets per month is taking longer than planned and is now expected in early 2027.

Elsewhere on the tape, defensive and consumer service names confirmed that underlying U.S. demand remains intact: HCA Healthcare (HCA) beat estimates with $19.8 billion in revenue and $7.22 in adjusted EPS on strong inpatient admissions, Royal Caribbean (RCL) posted $6.57 in adjusted EPS and raised its full-year outlook to $17.85–$17.95 on close-in cruise demand, and PayPal (PYPL) beat on the bottom line ($1.45 non-GAAP EPS vs. $1.31 consensus, with FY26 EPS guidance lifted to ~$5.60) via $1.5 billion in Q3 buybacks and strict opex discipline even as top-line revenue ($8.58B vs. $8.71B expected) reflected international headwinds.

### Oil, the 10-Year Yield, and Day One of the FOMC

In energy markets, December Brent crude fell 1.67% ($1.93) to settle at $113.88 per barrel, while WTI traded near $100–$101. The second consecutive day of crude relief followed diplomatic visits to Tehran by officials from Saudi Arabia, Turkey, and Pakistan alongside conditional de-escalation rhetoric from Iranian leadership. Gold held steady at $4,026.00 (-0.12%), consolidating just above the $4,000 psychological floor.

That $3.00 two-day pullback in Brent from Friday’s $116.66 settlement gave rate-sensitive equities room to breathe today, lifting the Russell 2000 by 1.16% and nudging the 10-year Treasury yield down to 4.995%. However, as the Federal Open Market Committee convened today for the start of its two-day policy meeting, the macro picture remains governed by the same “Grind” boundaries: $113.88 Brent is still an inflationary tax on the global economy, Consumer Confidence just surprised to the upside at 91.7, and payments volume at Visa is growing 9% year-over-year. With a rate hold tomorrow at 2:00 PM ET fully priced in, the market’s focus will rest entirely on Chair Powell’s 2:30 PM ET press conference and how the Committee weighs energy-driven headline inflation against expectations for the December meeting.
