Markets took a measured step back today after a strong run, with U.S. equities easing off their record highs as investors digested a soft consumer report and fresh Middle East headlines. The S&P 500 slipped 0.2% to 7,785.76, the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.3% to 26,729.16, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average declined 0.2% to 53,732.41. Even so, both the S&P 500 and Nasdaq managed to finish the week higher — a quiet signal that the broader trend remains intact.

July retail sales landed softer than expected, falling 0.6% month-over-month after a 0.2% gain in June. Retail sales excluding vehicles dropped 0.3%, and the core measure excluding vehicles and gasoline declined 0.2%. The numbers came in below consensus and reinforced the view that the consumer is showing some cracks. Ironically, the same month that produced this soft data also saw a surge in small-business optimism — a reminder that the economic picture remains mixed. Investors largely interpreted the report as reason for the Federal Reserve to think twice before hiking rates next month. Treasury yields moved lower early on the data before firming later in the session; the 10-year finished near 4.65–4.69%, while the 2-year hovered around 4.13% as the yield curve continued to steepen. The VIX stayed near 14, close to 2026 lows, and the dollar index traded around 99.6.

Oil prices recovered during the day, with WTI near $81 and Brent around $87–88. The bounce came after two more ships were attacked while trying to transit the Strait of Hormuz, slowing what little traffic remained to a crawl. President Trump appears to be leaning toward economic pressure rather than immediate military response. Secretary of War Pete Hegseth said the United States will unveil measures to isolate Iran economically “like we’ve never seen before.” Iran, for its part, advanced a plan that would ban transit of U.S., Israeli, and other “hostile” countries’ assets through the Strait — a clear attempt to raise the cost of doing business for Washington’s partners. Previous rumors of advancing peace talks have faded, and the status quo of constrained commodity flows through Hormuz looks likely to persist for months. Gulf states are working on longer-term bypass options, but those take time.

Asia delivered a mixed session, though South Korea’s Kospi stood out with a gain of more than 2%, powered by another strong day in semiconductors. Japan’s Nikkei advanced modestly, while Hong Kong and Australia finished lower. European markets closed mixed to slightly softer near recent highs.

What caught our eye during the session was a series of sharp, telling moves. Reddit rocketed nearly 13% after being named a new addition to the S&P 500, effective next week, a reminder that index inclusion still moves the needle even on a low-volume Friday. Broadcom dropped roughly 6% after analysts raised questions about the scale and structure of potential AI-related financing vehicles, showing that the market is no longer just debating how much companies are spending on AI but starting to ask how they’re paying for it. Applied Materials sold off despite a solid earnings beat and upbeat guidance — a classic case of “good numbers, high bar,” as expectations in the semiconductor equipment space leave little room for anything short of perfection. Energy stocks led as oil swung higher on the latest Hormuz developments, underscoring how quickly geopolitical risk can reassert itself. Meanwhile, grain and oilseed prices rallied overnight on unconfirmed chatter that Ukraine had struck the Russian Baltic port complex at Ust-Luga. If confirmed, it would highlight Ukraine’s growing ability to hit Russian export infrastructure deep inside the country. Both the Russia–Ukraine and Middle East conflicts have effectively become wars on commodity logistics, and that dynamic shows little sign of easing.

Looking ahead, shifting expectations around Fed policy may create pockets of volatility. Still, we continue to believe the global equity rally has further room to run. While we expect additional gains in the S&P 500, we also see compelling opportunities across Europe and Asia. Concentration risk in U.S. mega-cap technology remains elevated, which is precisely why diversified regional exposure continues to make sense.

In Europe, robust earnings, structural investment themes, and an improving cyclical backdrop remain supportive. We favor banks, health care, industrials, consumer discretionary names, Germany, and companies aligned with our “European Leaders” theme. Japan’s resilient corporate profits and recent valuation reset offer attractive entry points in high-quality names linked to AI, semiconductors, and rising power demand. Across Asia ex-Japan, we stay constructive on the AI hardware supply chain, select Chinese internet and semiconductor equipment stocks, and the longer-term growth story in India.

The path forward will not be linear. Soft consumer data, elevated energy prices, and unresolved geopolitical flashpoints will keep volatility in the mix. Yet the combination of resilient corporate earnings, an improving cyclical backdrop in key regions, and powerful structural themes continues to support a selective, diversified approach. We remain focused on quality cash-flow generators with genuine pricing power and are prepared to add on material weakness rather than chase strength.
