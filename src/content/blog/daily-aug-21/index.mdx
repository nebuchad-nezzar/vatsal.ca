U.S. equities rebounded on Friday in a low-volume session, partially recovering from Thursday’s sharp decline, though the major averages still finished lower for the week. The S&P 500 rose 0.4% to 7,674.37, the Dow Jones Industrial Average advanced 1.0% to 53,277.01, and the Nasdaq Composite gained 0.4% to 26,180.46. The weekly losses snapped three-week winning streaks for the S&P 500 and Nasdaq, with the S&P declining approximately 1.4% over the five sessions.

Sector performance was mixed but skewed positively on the day. Materials led with a gain of roughly 2.1%, followed by Health Care up about 1.4% and Consumer Discretionary up around 1.1%. Financials advanced approximately 1.0%, Communication Services rose nearly 1%, and Consumer Staples finished modestly higher. Industrials and Information Technology posted more limited gains. Real Estate was little changed to slightly lower, Energy edged lower by about 0.2%, and Utilities lagged significantly, declining more than 2%.

Within technology, performance was mixed. Crypto-related names stood out, with Strategy rising more than 7% and Coinbase also advancing as Bitcoin climbed above $77,000, marking a strong weekly recovery for digital assets. Tesla gained more than 4%. Select AI and software names such as Palantir moved higher. However, parts of the semiconductor complex faced pressure, with Marvell Technology declining more than 6% and Arm Holdings and Intel also finishing lower. The divergence highlighted ongoing rotation and profit-taking in certain high-beta AI and infrastructure-related names even as broader risk appetite improved modestly on the day.

The semiconductor sector continues to reflect both the strength of the structural AI infrastructure cycle and growing selectivity after a powerful multi-month advance. Demand from hyperscalers remains robust, with custom silicon, advanced packaging, and data-center GPUs still supporting strong fundamental growth. Nvidia’s upcoming earnings remain a key near-term catalyst. At the same time, elevated valuations and heavy positioning have made the group more sensitive to any signs of slowing momentum or shifts in risk appetite. Recent sessions have shown clear bifurcation, with certain memory, storage, and platform names holding up better while high-beta AI infrastructure and some custom silicon names experienced sharper swings and profit-taking. The sector’s longer-term outlook remains constructive, but near-term price action is likely to stay rotational and more dependent on clear evidence of sustained demand rather than pure narrative momentum.

Oil prices remained elevated, with Brent trading near $94 and WTI in the mid-to-high $80s, extending a strong weekly advance of more than 5–6%. The unresolved standoff over the Strait of Hormuz continued to support a geopolitical risk premium. Shipping volumes through the strait remain far below pre-conflict norms. The 60-day memorandum of understanding between the United States and Iran has expired without renewal, and both sides remain entrenched. President Trump has reiterated a hard line, including threats of intensified economic measures against Iran and its trading partners, while Iran has maintained that the waterway will stay closed until its conditions are met. The United Arab Emirates’ decision to suspend trade and financial transactions with Iran has further elevated regional tensions. These developments continue to keep a risk premium embedded in energy markets and complicate the broader inflation and growth outlook.

Treasury yields remained a key focus after the prior day’s reversal higher. While the market found some footing on Friday, the backup in longer-term yields earlier in the week, alongside elevated energy prices, has reinforced caution around valuations and the policy path. Corporate earnings remain broadly resilient, but the market continues to show limited tolerance for signs of slowing growth or margin pressure against a backdrop of still-elevated input costs and geopolitical uncertainty.

We continue to view the longer-term outlook for global equities as constructive, supported by resilient earnings, structural investment themes in AI infrastructure, defense, electrification, and energy security, and improving cyclical conditions in several regions outside the United States. Concentration risk in U.S. mega-cap technology remains elevated, reinforcing the case for diversified exposure across Europe, Japan, and Asia ex-Japan. Our preference remains for quality cash-flow generators with genuine pricing power and selective relative-value opportunities rather than aggressive directional positioning.

We will continue to monitor residual earnings, developments around the Strait of Hormuz, the path of bond yields and energy prices, and incoming inflation data, and will keep you updated as conditions evolve.
