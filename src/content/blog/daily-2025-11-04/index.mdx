November 4, 2025

Risk sentiment/equities staged a recovery early on in the overnight session. Following the rebound in North America yesterday tech shares rallied with Asian equity markets finishing in the green across the board. U.S.-China trade relations continue to improve with China agricultural purchases picking up. The first sorghum shipment in a long while has been sent from the U.S. to China while traders note China bought around 120Kt of U.S. wheat for December shipment as well. China’s Ministry of Commerce also officially issued its first batch of rare earth control adjustments under the recent U.S.–China trade framework lifting restrictions on 31 U.S. firms for one year. I suppose the most interesting China related news came from Nvidia’s CEO Jensen Huang who told the FT China will win the AI race with the U.S. due to lower regulation and cheap electricity.

Huang was probably talking about his book a bit and walked the comments back a bit later noting that “China is nanoseconds behind America in AI. It’s vital that America wins by racing ahead”. European bourses were decidedly less upbeat with indices all trading lower.

The Norges Bank and the BoE held rates steady this morning while overall the EU economic data was less than inspiring. We saw weaker than expected German industrial production data and soft construction PMI numbers across the board. On a positive note, China said that Beijing and Brussels have a huge space for cooperation adding that it’s willing to explore the possibility of a broader EU-China free trade pact.

After coming under pressure early in the session, North American stock markets saw continued weakness throughout much of the trading day on Thursday. The major averages more than offset the gains posted during Wednesday’s session, falling to their lowest closing levels in two weeks. The sharp pullback came amid renewed weakness among artificial intelligence-related stocks, which led to the sell-off seen on Tuesday.

Shares of Advanced Micro Devices plunged by 7.3 percent after showing a strong move to the upside over the course of the previous session. Major AI players Palantir Technologies, Oracle and Nvidia also showed significant moves to the downside. Chipmaker Qualcomm also tumbled by 3.6 percent despite reporting better than expected fiscal fourth quarter results and providing upbeat guidance for the current quarter.

Concerns about an AI bubble and the possibility of a near-term correction have recently weighed on investors’ minds. Negative sentiment may also have been generated in reaction to a report from global outplacement firm Challenger, Gray & Christmas showing a sharp increase in layoff announcements in the month of October. Challenger, Gray & Christmas said U.S.-based employers announced 153,074 job cuts in October, up 183 percent from the 54,064 job cuts announced in September and up 175 percent from the 55,597 cuts announced in the same month a year ago. Challenger, Gray & Christmas said employers have announced 1,099,500 job cuts through the first ten months of the year, marking the highest level of year-to-date job cuts since 2020. Semiconductor stocks showed a substantial move back to the downside following yesterday’s rebound, with the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index tumbling by 2.4 percent.

Significant weakness was also visible among software stocks, as reflected by the 2.2 percent slump by the Dow Jones U.S. Software Index.

In the bond market, treasuries showed a notable rebound following the weakness seen in the previous session. Subsequently, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, slumped 6.4 basis points to 4.093 percent. A preliminary reading on U.S. consumer sentiment in November may attract attention on Friday, as the government shutdown delays the release of the Labor Department’s closely watched monthly jobs report.
