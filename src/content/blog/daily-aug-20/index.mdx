U.S. equities closed lower on Thursday as rising bond yields, higher oil prices, and a sharp reaction to Walmart’s results weighed on sentiment. The S&P 500 declined 0.9% to 7,641.16, the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 1.3% to 52,759.21, and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 1.0% to 26,067.17. The session marked the market’s weakest performance in three weeks and left the major averages lower for the week.

Sector performance was broadly negative. Energy was the standout, finishing higher by approximately 0.4% as oil prices extended their recent advance. Real Estate also managed a modest gain. Materials were roughly flat to slightly lower. Information Technology held up relatively better than the broader market, declining about 0.4%, while Communication Services, Utilities, and Financials posted more moderate losses. Industrials, Consumer Discretionary, Health Care, and Consumer Staples led the downside, with Consumer Staples under the most pressure, falling roughly 2.0–2.7% as Walmart’s results weighed heavily on the group.

Walmart reported second-quarter results that exceeded consensus on both revenue and adjusted earnings, with global revenue rising 5.9% to $187.9 billion and adjusted EPS of $0.81. Global e-commerce sales grew 23%, and the company raised its full-year outlook. However, U.S. comparable sales growth slowed to 2.6% — the softest pace in six years — as management noted that customers were making trade-offs amid elevated gasoline prices. Shares fell more than 9%, exerting significant downward pressure on the broader consumer complex and underscoring market sensitivity to any signs of consumer fatigue.

Several other notable moves stood out during the session. Deere advanced roughly 7–8% after delivering better-than-expected earnings and an improved full-year outlook, offering a constructive signal for the agricultural equipment cycle. Moderna declined sharply, falling around 20–24%, as investors took profits following the previous day’s extraordinary surge on positive late-stage cancer vaccine data with Merck. Bitcoin climbed above $70,000 for the first time since early June, supported by constructive policy commentary, while related digital-asset names also advanced. These divergent moves highlighted the selective and sometimes sharp nature of price action even on a broadly weaker day.

Oil prices extended their recent advance, with Brent moving above $93 and WTI rising toward the mid-$80s, as the unresolved standoff over the Strait of Hormuz continued to support a geopolitical risk premium. Shipping volumes through the strait remain far below pre-conflict norms. The 60-day memorandum of understanding between the United States and Iran has expired without renewal, and both sides remain entrenched. President Trump has reiterated that no talks are ongoing or scheduled and that the U.S. naval blockade of Iranian ports remains in force, while Iran maintains that the waterway will stay closed until its conditions are met. The United Arab Emirates has suspended trade, commercial exchanges, and financial transactions with Iran following recent missile activity, further elevating regional tensions. These developments continue to keep a risk premium embedded in energy markets and complicate the broader inflation and growth outlook.

Treasury yields also reversed higher after Wednesday’s brief relief, with the 10-year yield climbing back near 4.70% and the 30-year yield firming, reflecting ongoing concerns about fiscal dynamics and the inflationary implications of elevated energy costs.

The combination of firmer yields, higher oil prices, and selective disappointment in a high-profile consumer name created a more risk-off tone. While corporate earnings, particularly among companies with scale and operational flexibility, remain broadly supportive, the market is showing limited tolerance for any signs of slowing growth or margin pressure against a backdrop of still-elevated input costs and policy uncertainty.

We continue to view the longer-term outlook for global equities as constructive, supported by resilient earnings, structural investment themes in AI infrastructure, defense, electrification, and energy security, and improving cyclical conditions in several regions outside the United States. Concentration risk in U.S. mega-cap technology remains elevated, reinforcing the case for diversified exposure across Europe, Japan, and Asia ex-Japan. Our preference remains for quality cash-flow generators with genuine pricing power and selective relative-value opportunities rather than aggressive directional positioning.

We will continue to monitor residual earnings, developments around the Strait of Hormuz, the path of bond yields and energy prices, and incoming inflation data, and will keep you updated as conditions evolve.
