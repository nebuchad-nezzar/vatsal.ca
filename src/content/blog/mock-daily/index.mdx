Risk assets spent the session digesting elevated oil prices, limited progress toward a near-term resolution in the Strait of Hormuz, and positioning ahead of the U.S. CPI release. U.S. equities closed modestly lower, with the S&P 500 down 0.3%, the Dow Jones Industrial Average lower by a similar amount, and the Nasdaq Composite declining 0.6%. The move reflected the persistence of geopolitical risk premia and renewed attention to inflation dynamics.

Japan was closed for “Mountain Day” holiday so volumes were lighter in Asian trading, and results were mixed as well. The ASX and Kospi finished higher while the Hang Seng and stocks in mainland China underperformed. Australia’s ASX advanced modestly after the Reserve Bank of Australia held the cash rate at 4.35%. The Board characterized policy as restrictive, noted that the economy is slowing in line with expectations, and left open the possibility of further tightening should upside inflation risks materialize. Hong Kong and Shanghai declined (Hang Seng approximately –1.1%, Shanghai Composite –0.8%), while Korea’s Kospi showed relative resilience, supported by ongoing interest in the semiconductor complex. The regional backdrop continues to reflect the overhang from elevated energy prices, particularly for energy-importing economies, even as selective technology strength persists.

Europe was comparatively resilient. The Stoxx Europe 600 finished essentially flat and the DAX advanced modestly, both remaining near recent highs. Energy equities provided support as crude prices stayed firm, and ongoing earnings results continued to underpin the broader market. European equities have absorbed geopolitical noise more effectively than might have been anticipated given the region’s energy exposure.

In the United States, early stability gave way to a gradual decline as oil held its gains and technology and communication services—led by Alphabet—acted as a drag. Energy and healthcare led sector performance, consistent with firm crude prices and a degree of defensive positioning. Market breadth was soft and volume was moderate. The Russell 2000 advanced modestly, indicating that not all segments traded purely as macro proxies.

The primary driver remained developments in the Middle East. No meaningful progress emerged on a U.S.–Iran framework that would restore more normal flows through the Strait of Hormuz. Mutual demands for compensation and other conditions continued to limit the prospect of near-term de-escalation. Crude prices responded accordingly, with WTI moving into the mid-$83s and Brent trading in the high $80s to low $90s. Treasury yields were relatively contained, with the 10-year near 4.70%, suggesting the bond market continues to afford the Federal Reserve some benefit of the doubt ahead of inflation data. However, the direction of energy prices remains unhelpful for the disinflation narrative.

From a portfolio construction perspective, the current environment rewards discipline. Elevated oil prices sustain inflation tail risks and complicate the Federal Reserve’s policy path. Equity valuations leave limited room for error in the event of disappointing growth or inflation outcomes. Emphasis remains on selective exposure to quality cash-flow generators and companies with genuine pricing power, while maintaining caution toward pure duration and highly leveraged balance sheets. The AI infrastructure theme retains support, as evidenced by recent financing activity and selective strength in related names, though concentration risk warrants ongoing attention.

The U.S. CPI report represents the next clear catalyst. A benign outcome would likely be constructive for risk assets given the energy backdrop; a firmer core reading would reinforce the higher-for-longer rates discussion and place additional pressure on multiples. Until greater clarity emerges on both the Hormuz situation and the inflation trajectory, risk remains controlled, with a preference for relative-value opportunities over outright directional exposure and a readiness to add on material weakness rather than chase strength.
