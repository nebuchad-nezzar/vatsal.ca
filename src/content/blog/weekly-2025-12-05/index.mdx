December 5, 2025

Global stock markets steadied this week at higher levels which coincided with continued stabilization in bitcoin. Following a particularly weak November U.S.

ADP employment report, market expectations around a 25 basis point cut at next week’s FOMC meeting solidified. Global interest rates tracked higher nevertheless. BoJ Governor Ueda reiterated rates need to move up in Japan as the economic outlook is realized while reports circulated that bondholders have voiced concerns that presumptive Fed Chair nominee Hassett could roil markets by cutting rates too quickly to appease President Trump.

Oil prices rose after OPEC+ confirmed expectations to keep production steady in Q1, while peace in Ukraine remained elusive. U.S. natural gas prices surged above $5 nearing a 3-year high amid a cold snap and record flows to LNG export facilities.

For the past week, the S&P rose 0.3%, and the Nasdaq gained 0.9%. European bourses finished the week higher, with Germany leading the charge adding 0.8%. Asia Pacific stock markets also felt the positive sentiment; Japan gained 0.5% and the Hong Kong Hang Seng rose 0.9%.

Your BSD Global Tech Hedge Fund advanced 0.5% this past week. November ADP jobs were well into negative territory, falling to the lowest levels since 2020, dragged down by small business losses. ISM Services was solid and included a welcomed pull back in prices paid.

Initial jobless claims fell this week but did little to offset the narrative the Fed will still be cutting next week. A strong Canadian employment report sent the Loonie and those rates up significantly. U.S. Treasury yields continued to track higher after September PCE didn’t really alter the inflation narrative while University of Michigan inflation expectations saw a welcome decline. AI headlines continued to lead corporate news, with Microsoft volatile as it refuted a report that it had lowered AI software sales quotas as customers hold off on uptake of new products.

Heading into the key holiday quarter, many retailers were already showing strong quarterly results, including Macy’s and American Eagle Outfitters, which both handily beat expectations even though tariffs are still admittedly causing a drag. Ulta Beauty also rewarded shareholders as strong store traffic led to stellar same store sales and a big earnings beat. Among industrial names, Boeing had a great week after it said “recovery is in full force” and gave assurances that deliveries for 737 and 787 aircraft will be higher in 2026.

Synopsis was the latest tech firm to be blessed by Nvidia, garnering a $2B investment from the chip giant for a design and engineering partnership and expanding Nvidia’s CUDA ecosystem. On the M&A front, Warner Brothers clinched a richly valued deal with Netflix at approximately $83B that will face significant scrutiny from U.S. regulators.

On Friday, shares of digital infrastructure investor Digitalbridge surged 40% on a report that Softbank is mulling a takeover approach. Investor’s focus this coming week will be on the Federal Reserve’s final interest rate decision of the year. It will also be the penultimate full trading week of the year, and markets will hope to ride recent momentum into a strong year-end Santa Claus rally.

Traders widely expect the Fed on Wednesday to cut its key rate by another 25 basis points for a third straight monetary policy committee meeting. December rate cut odds have been on a bit of a roller-coaster ride since last month, but recent economic data has reinforced the chances. The Fed will get one final major update on the labor market in the form of the delayed October U.S. JOLTS report on Tuesday. The earnings calendar is also active this week, headlined by Broadcom, Costco, Adobe, and GameStop.

For investors still reeling from Netflix’s blockbuster Warner Bros. deal, this week could bring more M&A history as Electronic Arts shareholders are set to vote on Wednesday on a $55B acquisition offer from Saudi Arabia’s sovereign wealth fund. If approved, it would be the largest leveraged buyout in history.
