U.S. equities closed modestly lower on Friday as a stronger-than-expected August jobs report reignited expectations for a potential Federal Reserve rate hike later this month. The S&P 500 fell 0.4% to 7,718.60, the Dow Jones Industrial Average declined 0.5% to 53,414.25, and the Nasdaq Composite slipped 0.3% to 26,506.99. Despite the soft finish, the major averages managed to hold onto small weekly gains, reflecting a market that continues to balance resilient corporate fundamentals against shifting macro signals.

The standout economic release was the employment report. Nonfarm payrolls rose by 162,000 in August—nearly three times the consensus forecast of around 56,000—while the unemployment rate held steady at 4.1%. Revisions to prior months added further strength. The data painted a picture of a still-resilient labor market and quickly lifted the odds of a September rate increase, sending the 2-year Treasury yield higher and pressuring rate-sensitive areas of the market. What had been a more constructive tone earlier in the week, aided by comments from Fed Governor Christopher Waller, gave way to renewed caution.

Interesting individual stock moves added color to the session. Lululemon Athletica plunged more than 17%, the largest decliner in the S&P 500, after reporting weaker-than-expected revenue and cutting its full-year outlook. In sharp contrast, memory and semiconductor names showed notable strength: Sandisk jumped nearly 12%, Micron gained over 6%, and Advanced Micro Devices advanced nearly 5%. Nvidia also finished higher, supported by ongoing AI infrastructure demand and residual positive sentiment around its strategic moves. The divergence underscored how selective the current environment remains—quality AI-related names continue to attract capital even as consumer discretionary faces scrutiny.

Oil prices held near recent elevated levels, with Brent and WTI consolidating after posting strong weekly gains driven by renewed U.S.-Iran hostilities and ongoing constraints on shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. Traffic through the waterway remains well below pre-conflict norms, keeping a geopolitical risk premium embedded in energy markets. This dynamic continues to complicate the inflation outlook and the Federal Reserve’s path.

Overall, Friday’s session captured the push-and-pull that has defined recent trading: solid economic data that supports growth but also reinforces the higher-for-longer rates narrative, selective strength in technology and AI infrastructure, and persistent geopolitical influence on energy prices. The labor market’s resilience is a double-edged sword for equities—encouraging for the real economy, yet a potential headwind for valuations if it locks in further policy tightening.

We maintain a constructive longer-term view on technology and AI-related equities, supported by resilient demand for advanced semiconductors, data-center infrastructure, and enterprise software platforms. Concentration risk, elevated valuations, residual geopolitical uncertainty around energy supply, and the evolving monetary policy outlook warrant discipline. Our preference remains selective exposure to quality companies with clear visibility into AI-driven revenue growth, genuine pricing power, and strong free-cash-flow generation.

Looking ahead, next week brings key inflation data (CPI and PPI) that will further shape expectations for the Federal Reserve’s September meeting, alongside residual technology earnings and continued monitoring of developments around the Strait of Hormuz, bond yields, and energy prices. We will keep you updated as conditions evolve.
