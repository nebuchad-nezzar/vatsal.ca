January 15, 2025

The Asian session saw stocks in Australia trading higher and the Kospi hitting a new record after the Bank of Korea held rates steady (as expected). China provided some stimulus after the PBOC moved forward with some “targeted easing” lowering rates on structural monetary tools and relending rates with a 25bp cut (1yr rate lowered to 1.25%) with additional measures to support credit to priority sectors (including private bond financing and green transition projects). There was also a cut in the minimum down payment for commercial property loans to 30%.

All of that didn’t help stocks though with the Hang Seng and equities in mainland China finishing the session lower. I would say the most positive news out of Asia: TSMC earnings. A record quarter and bullish forward guidance including up to $56 billion in capex this year.

The AI fueled tech frenzy remains alive and well it seems. European bourses were mostly higher today. The positives: the French government survived two no confidence motions and in general we saw better than expected economic data out of Europe including stronger UK GDP and EU industrial production data that topped expectations.

The negative of course remains Greenland but I suppose the fact that Europe and the U.S. have agreed to keep talking has to be seen as somewhat of a plus, at least no military intervention threats. After turning in a strong performance for much of the session, North American stock markets gave back some ground in the latter part of the trading day on Thursday but managed to remain mostly higher.

The early strength partly reflected a positive reaction to earnings news from Taiwan Semiconductor, with the chipmaker surging by 4.4 percent. Taiwan Semiconductor jumped after reporting a sharp increase in fourth quarter profits and announced larger-than-expected capital spending plans, contributing to renewed confidence in the artificial intelligence trade. Positive sentiment may also have been generated in reaction to a Labor Department report showing first-time claims for U.S. unemployment benefits unexpectedly dipped in the week ended January 10th. The Labor Department said initial jobless claims fell to 198,000, a decrease of 9,000 from the previous week’s revised level of 207,000. Economists had expected jobless claims to rise to 215,000 from the 208,000 originally reported for the previous week.

In the bond market, treasuries gave back ground after moving higher over the two previous sessions. Subsequently, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, rose 2.0 basis points to 4.160 percent.
