October 19, 2025

U. S. stock markets recouped about half of last Friday’s sell off to open trade this past week. Hopes had resurfaced that cooler heads will prevail in the U.

S.-China trade talks. Treasury Secretary Bessent noted he had been in contact with Chinese trade officials over the weekend and the Xi-Trump meeting remained on track. U.

S.-China tensions would remain a volatility driver throughout the week, with a trickle of additional China measures being leaked against ramped up rhetoric from the Trump Administration. By Friday, the talking points coming from both sides were more conciliatory with Bessent set to speak with his Chinese counterpart by phone, ahead of a face to face meeting in Malaysia this coming week. Gold prices continued their surge, crossing above $4300/oz before reversing hard on Friday along with other precious metals.

WTI crude prices also slumped further below the $60/mark. U. S. economic readings were sparse given the U.

S. government shutdown, but October Empire manufacturing data was notably stronger than anticipated and included a continued rise in prices. The Fed’s latest Beige Book remained tepid, showing little change in overall economic activity while pricing pressures remained modest. The U.

S. 10-year Treasury yield tested below 4% for the first time since March. French stock and bond markets lead in Europe after PM Lecornu survived two key confidence votes tasking him to shepherd in a budget agreement. For the week, the S&P gained 1.7%, and the Nasdaq rose 2%.

European bourses were quite bifurcated; the French CAC40 advanced 3.24% while the German DAX retreated 1.69%. Asia Pacific stock markets also struggled with Japan falling 1.05% and the Hong Kong Hang Seng fell 3.97%. Your BSD Global Tech Hedge Fund gained 0.3% this past week.

Earnings season once again was kicked-off by a deluge of bank results. Large money center banks generally reported robust results buoyed by a profitable yield curve structure, a rejuvenated investment banking environment, and a resilient consumer. A very different narrative around the regional banks developed after mid-week loan loss disclosures from Western Alliance and Zions Bank.

The headlines dovetailed with cautionary commentary about “cockroaches” potential lurking on corporate balance sheets from JP Morgan’s CEO Dimon on Monday. Private credit names and a host of regional banks experienced a significant selloff which induced handwringing around potential contagion and systemic risks. The VIX jumped to levels not seen since Trump’s ‘Liberation Day’ tariff announcement in April.

Separately, overnight repo rates jumped in what some cautioned could be viewed as another sign of stress for lending markets. By Friday, some of these banking/lending fears seemed to abate after a slew of additional regional bank reports didn’t offer up any fresh canaries regarding credit risk. Chairman Powell’s somewhat dovish commentary on Wednesday also served as backstop.

He seemed to endorse the market view that we will get two more 25 bps rate cuts this year regardless of the lack of visibility caused by the ongoing government shutdown. Perhaps more importantly he also indicated that the balance sheet runoff (QT) is likely to come to end before the end of this year in order to keep reserves somewhat above the level seen as “ample”. In corporate news this week, outside of the previously noted banking sector reports, Oracle and OpenAI kept the excitement alive in the AI trade.

At its multi-day investor event, Oracle raised long term revenue targets dramatically and presented strong gross margin projections, though numbers for FY26 were taken only modestly higher. It was also reported that Oracle would deploy some 50K advanced AMD AI chips in its buildout plans for FY26, keeping AMD shares bid near 52-week highs. Meanwhile, Broadcom and Walmart shares both got a boost as the companies were tapped as OpenAI’s latest strategic partners, even as reports circulated that CEO Sam Altman has formulated a 5-year business plan to fund his $1T of spending plans for AI infrastructure.

Reports that OpenAI is looking to boost revenue by allowing more ‘adult’ content via ChatGPT drew some immediate backlash from politicians and technology commentators. Elsewhere, the slowdown in the electric car market after the expiration of U. S. rebates became more apparent as GM announced $1.6B in charges for EV realignment charges and said they would slow EV expansion.

Investors will gear up for a busy week, with the third quarter earnings season picking up steam. Market participants deprived of economic data due to the ongoing government shutdown will also have something to look forward to in the consumer inflation report (CPI) for September, to be announced on Friday, October 24th, 2025. Streaming giant Netflix is set to report results on Tuesday and electric vehicle maker Tesla on Wednesday.

The two companies headline a packed week, which also includes other major names such as IBM, Coca-Cola, General Motors, and 3M. Investors will also be keeping a close eye on trade developments between the U. S. and China, as the world’s two largest economies spar over the Asian nation’s strict rare earth export controls.

China is facing a pivotal week that will set its economic direction for the next five years, even as new data is expected to confirm a recent slowdown. From Monday to Wednesday, the Fourth Plenum meeting will be held, with the main goal of discussing China’s important Five-Year Plan for the years 2026 to 2030.
