October 12, 2025

Animal spirits generally remained bountiful opening this past week, more than offsetting worries arising from the lengthening U.S. government shutdown in DC. Elevated M&A activity continued to garner attention heading into bank earnings this coming week.

Another sizable AI compute deal between AMD and OpenAI sent AMD shares soaring, and drove continued investor fervor across the AI stack. An intensifying debate over AI remained front and center amid another deluge of articles and headlines highlighting bubble risks from stretched valuations, extended positioning, circular transactions, muted productivity gains and ROI doubts. Nvidia CEO took to CNBC mid-week to reiterate his overwhelmingly bullish thesis, lamenting on recent deals within the ecosystem which he wished he was either part of or took more aggressive positioning in.

French bond yields initially rose and the CAC lagged after French PM Lecornu’s surprise resignation opened the door to new questions surrounding President Macron’s leadership. Concerns abated a bit into the week’s end as Macron seemed poised to announce a palatable, more right leaning PM replacement before landing on reappointing Lecornu. The Japanese Yen remained under significant pressure as PM-elect Takaichi offered up clues as to how she intends to lead the LDP party while reportedly losing support from key junior, coalition party member the Komeito party.

Separately, rhetoric clearly picked on both sides of the U.S./China trade front with each leader looking for leverage ahead of the Trump Xi summit later this month. President Trump upped in the ante during what was a relatively sleepy trading session on Friday.

In a Truth Social post he threatened to pull out of that Xi meeting and bring back “massive” tariffs on China due to their increased hostility, particularly around rare earth export controls. The VIX jumped to a 1-mohth high and the S&P fell more ~200 points back below 6600 following the post. Initially oil prices rose after OPEC+ producers showed some restraint in the output increase announcement.

WTI crude prices tested below $60/barrel for the first time since early June late in the week following news of President Trump’s brokered deal on a Gaza ceasefire and hostage release and the resurfacing China trade war. Gold and silver broke above key levels of $4K/oz and $50/oz respectively. Multiple factors continued to power the rally, including geopolitical risks, robust central bank buying, exchange-traded fund inflows, expectations for U.S. rate cuts, and trade-related uncertainties. The U.S. government remained shut with no talks scheduled between GOP and Democratic leadership heading into the weekend resulting in a dearth of economic readings.

Canada’s September employment report was notably stronger than expected resulting in a bump for the Loonie after the U.S. dollar posted a strong week across the board. U.S. stocks held at or near all-time highs led by the NASDAQ before the selling pressure on Friday. Those same risk off flows pushed Treasury yields lower sending the 2-year back towards 3.5% and the 10-year within a few bps of 4%. For the week, the S&P fell 2.4%, and the Nasdaq declined 2.5%.

European bourses also retreated; the French CAC40 lost 2.0% and the German DAX slipped 0.6%. Asia Pacific stock markets were bifurcated with Japan gaining 5.1% while the Hong Kong Hang Seng fell 3.1%. Your BSD Global Tech Hedge Fund gained 0.1% this past week; Thanks to Friday’s surge in volatility and the value of our put options.

In corporate news, Dell significantly raised long term expectations predicated on the explosive growth in AI. IBM and Anthropic made waves announcing an enterprise focused tie following the AMD/OpenAI announcement Monday. Taiwan Semi saw September sales accelerate for the first time in 4-months.

Delta reported solid top and bottom-line results buoyed by resurgent domestic demand. PEP met expectations despite softness in their North American food unit. Comerica announced a deal to be acquired by Fifth Third in a $10.9B all-cash transaction which some argued could be the start of much needed regional bank consolidation under the Trump administration.

Tesla introduced their long awaited cheaper model Y option to little fanfare. Ford shares dipped on reports it was being forced to shut down some production following a September fire at one of its key aluminum suppliers. Verizon named former PayPal CEO Dan Schulman as its new CEO.

BMW had one its worst session in years after cutting their 2025 outlook citing tariffs and weak China sales. Heading a key investor day next week Oracle shares came into focus following a report Oracle has lost money renting Nvidia Blackwell chips in its most recent quarter. With the U.S. government shutdown showing no signs of ending, thus delaying key economic data, and renewed trade war concerns, market participants will get a welcome new catalyst this week in the form of the kickoff of the third quarter earnings season. Major U.S. banks will be in the spotlight, with the largest one, JPMorgan, set to announce its results on Tuesday.

Joining it will be Goldman Sachs, Wells Fargo, and Citibank. On Wednesday, Bank of America and Morgan Stanley will report. Investors will also get financial updates from Johnson & Johnson and American Express.

Traders will also be cautious after Friday’s tariff shock. Sentiment took a heavy beating after President Donald Trump accused China of “hostile” export controls on rare earths and said the U.S. would impose an additional 100% tariff on products from the Asian nation.

Any further dialogue or back-and-forth between Trump and China will be closely watched. The U.S. calendar is stacked with high-profile speeches from Fed Chair Powell, Bowman, Waller, and Barr.

Elsewhere in the world, a relatively quiet week will still see the publication of trade balance data for the eurozone (August) and China (September). Also in China, the September CPI report will be available.
