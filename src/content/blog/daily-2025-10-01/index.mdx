October 1, 2025

China is out for Golden Week holiday until October 8th so no surprise price action was a bit more subdued in Asia overnight. Equity markets that were open finished the session mixed. Maybe the biggest headline out of Asia was news that Taiwan has rejected the U.S. request to make 50% of its chips locally. Vice Premier Cheng Li-chiun spelled it out pretty clearly saying Taipei never agreed to such terms and “will not agree” to them. This comes on top on Japan LDP leadership hopeful Sanae Takaichi stating she would review the recent Japan-U.S. trade agreement if she became leader and reports that cite Seoul’s presidential adviser saying that South Korea is unable to pay the $350 billion upfront in investment in the U.S. as the Trump administration had suggested under their recent tariff deal. It feels like a lot of bold statements get made but then there isn’t a lot of fact checking down the road.

European bourses were in a more upbeat mood today and are in the green across the board. Pharma stocks are leading the way higher today as Trump’s efforts to lower prices in the U.S. are seen as nowhere near a worst case scenario for drug companies.

Outside of that, the news isn’t all that great with mixed European PMI data and EU-Russia tensions at the forefront again. Russian spokesperson Dmitry Peskov warned European leaders that Russia would chase any individual or country which stole its money. North American stock markets came under pressure early in the session on Wednesday but staged a significant turnaround over the course of the trading day.

The major averages climbed well off their lows of the session and into positive territory. Due to the turnaround on the day, the major averages closed higher for the fourth straight session, with the Dow and the S&P 500 reaching new record closing highs. The initial pullback came after the U.S. government officially shut down early this morning after lawmakers failed to pass a temporary spending bill. Democrats have demanded that any stop-gap funding bill include an extension of enhanced Obamacare tax credits, while Republicans have argued the issue should be debated after a funding bill is passed. However, the early selling pressure may have been offset by optimism about the outlook for interest rates following the release of private sector employment data.

While the release of the Labor Department’s closely watched monthly jobs on Friday is likely to be delayed due to the shutdown, payroll processor ADP released a report this morning showing an unexpected decrease by private sector employment in the month of September. ADP said private sector employment fell by 32,000 jobs in September after dipping by a revised 3,000 jobs in August. Economists had expected private sector employment to climb by 50,000 jobs compared to the addition of 54,000 jobs originally reported for the previous month.

The subsequent turnaround also came as analysts have pointed out that the markets have historically not been materially impacted by government shutdowns. Pharmaceuticals extended the rally seen over the course of the previous session, driving the NYSE Arca Pharmaceutical Index up by 5.4 percent to its best closing level in almost seven months. Considerable strength also emerged among computer hardware stocks, with the NYSE Arca Computer Hardware Index spiking by 3.9 percent to a record closing high.

In the bond market, treasuries moved higher in reaction to government shutdown and the private sector jobs data. Subsequently, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, slid 4.2 basis points to 4.106 percent. Reports on weekly jobless claims and factory orders were scheduled to be released on Thursday, but the data is likely to be delayed due to the government shutdown.
