The new quarter opened with the market finally getting the relief it had been waiting weeks for, and fittingly, it came from the bond market easing off rather than any single piece of good news. Major indices clawed back from a sharp intraday decline to finish modestly higher, with the S&P 500 adding roughly 0.2% to close near 7,667–7,669, the Dow essentially flat at around 50,930, and the Nasdaq up a fraction to 26,871. Small caps were the real story of the day — the Russell 2000 outperformed with a 0.35% gain, a meaningful signal after a brutal September in which it dropped more than 5%, bringing its quarterly decline to roughly 7.5%. The closing numbers mask real volatility underneath, but starting October and the fourth quarter in the green, after the month we just had, counts for something.

The bond market did the heavy lifting again today, just in the opposite direction from the past few weeks. Treasury yields retreated from their highest levels in more than two decades, giving equities room to breathe for the first time in a while. That's the headline worth sitting with: after a month where the 10-year repeatedly tested post-financial-crisis highs, even a modest pullback in yields was enough to flip sentiment meaningfully. It's a reminder of just how much of this entire market's mood has been a function of the rates story rather than anything happening at the company level.

Tech delivered the single biggest story of the day, and it came after the bell: Micron reported a genuinely blowout quarter, with revenue of roughly $41.5 billion against a consensus near $35 billion, and earnings per share of $25.11 versus the $20.28 expected. The company also touted roughly $22 billion in new customer deals and issued a next-quarter guide well above expectations. Shares jumped over 12% in after-hours trading, and the read-through rippled across the entire AI supply chain — Nvidia and Broadcom both got a sympathy lift given how directly their platforms depend on Micron's high-bandwidth memory. This is exactly the kind of print that resets sentiment on a whole sector: after two days of chip selling into the report, Micron flipped the narrative from "is the AI memory trade over" back to "the memory supercycle is alive and well."

Layered on top of that, Broadcom confirmed it will lend Anthropic up to $42 billion to lease chips — another example of the AI infrastructure buildout increasingly being financed through creative, large-scale debt and leasing arrangements rather than straight capex. This is worth watching closely rather than just celebrating: it's the same dynamic we flagged with Oracle's data center troubles a week ago — more of this ecosystem's growth is being underwritten by leverage, and the bigger these numbers get, the more exposed the sector becomes to any shift in the rate environment. Elsewhere in tech-adjacent names, Nike posted an earnings beat, a reminder that strength isn't purely confined to the AI complex today.

On rare earths, nothing new to flag — the U.S.-China truce extension continues to hold, and a quiet rare earth market remains a modest tailwind rather than a headline.

One thing worth noting beneath the surface: despite the index-level gains, market breadth stayed weak today — this was very much a narrow, mega-cap and AI-driven rally rather than a broad risk-on move, even with small caps technically outperforming on a percentage basis. That's worth remembering before reading today's green close as a true all-clear signal.

Today was a genuine, fundamentals-driven positive — a real earnings beat from Micron, not just sentiment or a Fed-speak headline, combined with yields finally giving equities some room. That's a meaningfully better setup than most of what we saw in September. But I'd caution against treating one strong print and one day of yield relief as confirmation that the higher-for-longer regime is behind us — the Broadcom-Anthropic financing deal is a reminder that leverage is quietly building up underneath this entire AI trade, and that's a vulnerability, not a strength, if rates move against it again. My positioning bias remains the same: favor AI names with proven monetization and real demand validation (Micron just earned its place in that category) over those still absorbing infrastructure and financing risk.

Watch for read-through from Micron's report into Nvidia, Broadcom, and the broader chip complex over the next several sessions — this kind of beat usually has legs. Keep an eye on whether Treasury yields continue to retreat or whether today was just a pause within the broader uptrend — that's the single biggest swing factor for risk assets right now. And given the scale of the Broadcom-Anthropic financing arrangement, expect more scrutiny on how much of the AI buildout is being funded through debt and leasing structures rather than free cash flow — that's a theme likely to keep surfacing through Q4 earnings season.
