U.S. equities finished mixed on Tuesday as the market consolidated Monday’s sharp rebound. The S&P 500 was essentially unchanged at 7,764.64, remaining approximately 0.4% below its record close. The Dow Jones Industrial Average declined 0.4% to 51,863.69, while the Nasdaq Composite advanced 0.5% to 27,244.28, setting a new record high. Technology continued to provide relative support, offsetting softness in more cyclical and rate-sensitive areas.

Crude prices extended their decline for a fifth consecutive session. Brent settled near $99.25 after briefly trading below $98 earlier in the day, while WTI also moved lower. The sustained pullback has reduced the near-term inflation impulse that had driven the 10-year Treasury yield above 5% last week. The benchmark yield held in the mid-4.90% area. The oil move was driven by a combination of supply-side signals and diplomatic developments. Reports indicated that Saudi Arabia had restarted operations on the East-West pipeline, potentially restoring an important alternative export route that bypasses the Strait of Hormuz. Separately, Iranian officials were cited as offering to reopen the Strait within approximately one week if the United States eases military pressure and its blockade. These claims have not been independently verified and remain contingent on broader negotiations.

Diplomatic activity intensified around the United Nations General Assembly. President Trump, in his address, stated that a deal with Iran was possible after the November midterm elections and disclosed that U.S. and Iranian officials had held a “very good” meeting on the sidelines. Oil prices initially extended losses on the diplomatic signals before partially retracing during and after the speech. The reduction in the energy risk premium continues to support risk assets and ease pressure on long-term yields, though the conflict remains unresolved and shipping volumes through Hormuz are still constrained relative to pre-war levels.

Technology again provided leadership. Semiconductor names extended gains, with Intel among the stronger performers. The Nasdaq’s new record reflected continued demand for AI-related exposure following Monday’s surge in AMD (which briefly reached a $1 trillion market capitalization) and Meta Platforms. Meta consolidated after its sharp advance, while broader chip and infrastructure names held up. The relative strength in technology stands in contrast to the more cautious tone in energy and parts of the industrial complex.

Cross-asset moves were consistent with a market that is cautiously constructive but still constrained by the higher cost of capital. The Federal Reserve’s first rate increase in three years remains a key backdrop, with markets continuing to assign a meaningful probability of further tightening later this year. Attention is now focused on the Trump-Xi meeting scheduled for Thursday, which is expected to address trade and artificial-intelligence issues, alongside any further signals on the Iran file.

The session leaves equities near recent highs but with limited breadth outside technology. Lower oil prices and the modest retreat in yields have improved the near-term risk backdrop. However, policy remains in tightening mode, the 10-year yield is still elevated by recent standards, housing affordability continues to be challenged by higher mortgage rates, and geopolitical risk around energy supply has only partially de-escalated. Selective exposure to companies with durable free-cash-flow generation, pricing power, and clear exposure to structural demand in semiconductors and AI infrastructure remains preferred. Concentration risk and a higher cost of capital continue to warrant disciplined positioning.

Attention turns to the Trump-Xi meeting, residual data, and further developments in the Middle East and the path of yields.
