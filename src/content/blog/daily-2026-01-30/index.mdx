January 30, 2026

Asian equities finished lower with Indonesian equities closing  just over 1% higher thanks to the efforts to stabilize the market following the MSCI’s call on transparency of the market that surged the worst selloff in decades. The CEO resigned and while worries still exist, it seems the authorities have been able to stop the bleeding for now. We received a slew of data last night and will start with Japan, which came in quite soft across the board.

Tokyo CPI came in a bit softer than expected. Moving over to Europe, sentiment picked up slightly, thanks to some positive data and geopolitics seeming to simmer down. On the data front, eurozone GDP came in surprising the market with at +0.3% bump for Q4 eeking it out amidst the geopolitical tensions.

North American stock markets fluctuated during Friday’s trading session, maintaining a negative bias throughout the day before eventually closing mostly lower. Meanwhile, the major averages showed a mixed performance for the week. While the S&P 500 rose by 0.3 percent, the Nasdaq dipped by 0.2 percent and the Dow decreased by 0.4 percent.

The weakness may partly reflect renewed concerns about inflation after the Labor Department released a report showing producer prices increased much more than expected in the month of December. The Labor Department said its producer price index for final demand climbed by 0.5 percent in December after rising by 0.2 percent in November. Economists had expected producer prices to rise by another 0.2 percent.

The report also said producer prices in December were up by 3.0 percent compared to the same month a year ago, unchanged from November. The annual rate of growth was expected to slow to 2.7 percent. New tariff threats from President Donald Trump may have also have contributed to the negative sentiment, with the president threatening Canada with a 50 percent tariff on all aircraft sold in the U.

S. over its refusal to certify certain Gulfstream jets. Trump also signed an executive order that would impose tariffs on any goods from countries that sell or provide oil to Cuba. Meanwhile, traders were also reacting to news that Trump announced his intent to nominate former Federal Reserve Governor Kevin Warsh to succeed Fed Chair Jerome Powell.

Gold stocks posted some of the market’s worst performances on the day amid a nosedive in the precious metal’s price, with the NYSE Arca Gold Bugs Index plummeting by 12.6 percent. Semiconductor and computer hardware stocks also saw substantial weakness, contributing to the slump by the tech-heavy Nasdaq. In the bond market, treasuries gave back ground following the rebound seen on Thursday.

As a result, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, rose 1.4 basis points to 4.241 percent. The Labor Department’s monthly jobs report is likely to be in the spotlight next week. Reports on manufacturing and service sector activity, job openings and consumer sentiment may also attract attention along with the latest geopolitical developments.
