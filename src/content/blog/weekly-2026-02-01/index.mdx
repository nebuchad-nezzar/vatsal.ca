February 1, 2026

Investors navigated another newsworthy week through varying bouts of volatility. Nowhere was that more evident than in the action in precious metals markets. President Trump’s off the cuff remarks regarding the dollar sparked another brief round of de-dollarization concerns.

Rate check speculation continued to weigh on the USD/JPY, affording the Greenback to hit a 4-year low while gold simultaneously spiked above $5500/oz for the first time. Treasury Secretary Bessent assured markets that this government remains squarely behind a strong dollar policy, while strength in the Yen abated allowing a broader dollar stabilization. The S&P 500 briefly traded above 7K for the first time before retreating away from that round, big figure.

Crude oil prices found a bid as expectations for Iran military action climbed in the wake of fresh rhetoric from President Trump and the arrival of key US military assets into the region. Natural gas prices surged during the first half of the week only to give those gains back as much of the U. S. continued to contend with an ongoing arctic blast.

Wednesday’s FOMC meeting was largely uneventful as the Fed slipped into an expected pause that will likely carry on through the end of Chair Powell’s term. On Friday, President Trump confirmed his pick for Fed Chair would be Kevin Warsh, surprising some, but potentially assuaging those who were increasingly worried about Fed independence under a new regime. The Warsh pick did result in some new uncertainties for investors to think about.

His past comments on QE and deploying the balance sheet can be viewed as quite hawkish in a vacuum, and potentially lay the groundwork for a flash point with Trump down the road. The Warsh announcement was also muddied a bit by Republican Senator Tom Tillis’ pledge to block the nomination process until the DoJ fully resolves its “frivolous prosecution” of the current Fed Chair. Longer dated Treasury yields rose and the U.

S. dollar continued to firm up. The U. S. 10-year yield remained pinned at 4.25%.

Silver had a record one-day decline on Friday, trading down as much as 35% at one point, while gold prices dipped below $5K/oz as momentum from the mid-week reversal picked up after the Walsh announcement. Stocks touched record levels but ended mixed for the week, with the S&P edging up 0.3%, the DJIA falling 0.4%, and the Nasdaq lower by 0.2%. European bourses retreated with the German DAX falling 1.5% and the French CAC 40 slipped 0.2%.

Asia Pacific stock markets were once again bifurcated, with the Hong Kong Hang Seng gaining 2.4% while Japan was lower by 1%. Your BSD Global Tech Hedge Fund fell 1.4% this past week. Corporate earnings reports arrived quickly, showing varying degrees of success.

Mega-cap earnings exposed a widening gap between AI monetization winners and capital-intensive laggards, driving sharp stock-level divergence while keeping index-level damage contained. For example, Meta and Microsoft reported within minutes of each other but had vastly different outcomes. Meta shares gained 10% on blowout earnings and strong guidance that overshadowed ever increasing expenses, while Microsoft saw its shares decline 10% as its solid top and bottom line results were not enough to overcome concerns about disappointing Copilot utilization figures.

Apple put up record results but was held back by worries about supply chain constraints and higher memory costs. Sandisk has been the chief beneficiary of those surging memory prices, as it posted astounding earnings results and guidance that essentially doubled what analysts had estimated. General Motors impressed investors with a strong Q4 result and a robust capital plan including buybacks and a 20% dividend increase.

Meanwhile, Tesla posted decent earnings numbers and touted its future beyond EVs, noting that Cybercab, Tesla Semi and Megapack 3 are on schedule for volume production this year, and that Gen 3 Optimus robots will be revealed in Q1 and will start production at the company’s original plant in Freemont. Questions and concerns around ROI and circular spending relationships underpinning the AI stack only grew more pronounced this week after Nvidia pumped another $2B into Coreweave and, Amazon was speculated to be looking to invest $50B into another $100B funding round for OpenAI. In other deals news, reports suggest that within Elon Musk’s empire, SpaceX is mulling a merger with either xAI or Tesla as an alternative to an IPO later this year.

This coming week brings a heavy mix of megacap earnings and top-tier economic data, headlined by the U. S. January jobs report.

On the macro side, investors will track a packed U. S. calendar: S&P Global Manufacturing PMI, ISM Manufacturing PMI, and ISM Manufacturing Prices hit Monday, followed by JOLTS job openings on Tuesday. Wednesday brings ADP private payrolls plus S&P Global Services and Composite PMI readings.

Initial jobless claims arrive Thursday, and the week closes with the marquee releases: Nonfarm Payrolls and the unemployment rate on Friday. Earnings will be equally front-loaded. The week features results from major S&P 500 names, including Alphabet, Amazon, Advanced Micro Devices, Merck, and Pfizer, alongside other large pharma and tech reports that could shape sentiment across sectors. Both the Bank of England (BoE) and the European Central Bank (ECB) meet on Thursday, February 5.
