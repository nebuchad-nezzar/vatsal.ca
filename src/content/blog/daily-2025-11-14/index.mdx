November 14, 2025

No surprise given the weak handoff from North America yesterday stocks were under pressure from the outset in Asia. We saw a data dump out of China and in general the numbers were softer than expected adding to the negative sentiment. Industrial production at 4.9% YoY sounds great but the bar is always set pretty high for China and the print fell short of expectations.

Fixed asset investment (-1.7%) and property investment (-14.7%) numbers were also weaker than forecasted fueling further losses in stocks. Things didn’t get a whole lot better for European equity markets. Early on in the session the FT reported that the UK government is reversing plans to increase income taxes in this month’s budget.

Gilts were immediately smashed and Sterling came under selling pressure. Losses for both have been pared though after details emerged noting that the reason for the income tax change was an improved OBR economic forecast showing a smaller fiscal gap. The UK ten year yield is still up about 8bps this morning.

On the plus side, Eurozone GDP was a touch better than expected at 1.4% YoY but that hasn’t even come close to offsetting the overall negative sentiment. Following the sell-off seen during Thursday’s session, North American stock markets saw further downside in early trading on Friday but showed a significant recovery attempt as the day progressed. The Nasdaq and the S&P 500 climbed well off their lows and into positive territory before eventually ending the day roughly flat.

After seeing considerable volatility over the past few days, the major averages turned in a mixed performance for the week. Weakness among technology stocks continued to weigh on the session amid lingering valuation concerns. However, tech heavyweights Nvidia, Palantir Technologies and Tesla moved to the upside over the course of the session after initial weakness.

While some traders used the initial slump as an opportunity to pick up stocks at relatively reduced levels, buying interest remained somewhat subdued amid uncertainty about the outlook for interest rates. Recent comments from Federal Reserve officials as well as indications key U. S. economic data may never be released due to the government shutdown have reduced confidence the central bank will continue lowering interest rates next month.

CME Group’s FedWatch Tool currently indicates the chances of another quarter point rate cut at the next Fed meeting have slumped to 45.9 percent from 66.9 percent a week ago. In the bond market, treasuries came under pressure over the course of the session after seeing initial strength. Subsequently, the yield on the benchmark ten-year note, which moves opposite of its price, climbed 3.6 basis points to 4.148 percent after hitting a low of 4.065 percent.
