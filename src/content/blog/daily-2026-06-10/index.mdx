June 10, 2026

Last night, the Asian session saw indices ex-ASX all finish lower. The see saw back and forth in the Kospi continues, down 8% Monday, up 8% Tuesday and back down about 4.5% last night. Tech names were under pressure and that led to losses of 3.3% in Taiwan (where Central Bank Governor Yang thinks an equity market pull back is “healthy”) and 1.9% in Japan (SoftBank down 8% after reports that is has stalled in its attempt to get a $6billion margin loan secured by its OpenAI stake after creditors hesitated).

Of course, escalation in Iran didn’t help sentiment either with U. S. forces attacking Iranian air defence and radar systems after Iran shot down a U. S. helicopter with Iran now vowing further retaliation.

Chinese PPI came in as expected at 3.9%, a significant jump from 2.8% the prior month. Japanese PPI came in far higher than expected at 6.3% YoY (highest in more than three years) which didn’t help last night’s very weak 30-year JGB auction. Despite a surprise rate hike earlier this week geared at shoring up the currency, Indonesia's Rupiah remains under pressure with USD/IDR touching 18,000 again overnight.

Meanwhile, South Korea’s Finance Ministry notes that volatility in the financial sector is expanding. Interesting note, reports say China is considering a $295bio AI data center build out over the next five years. The build will be led by the National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC).

This state-directed infrastructure push will require that at least 80% of the underlying technology, including AI chips, be sourced from domestic suppliers. The AI race is on big time. European bourses all closed in the red today.

No surprise really, there isn’t all that much to cheer about is there? That ceasefire in Iran is looking more tenuous by the minute, oil prices have jumped and fears of second round energy driven inflation effects are forcing the ECB to (very likely) hike rates while the European economy is weakening. The lone bright spot was Italy’s industrial production data, which was better than expected but that sure isn’t enough to lift sentiment this morning.

North American stock markets drifted lower for most of the day. May CPI data was largely in line with market expectations, allaying fears of an aggressive break higher. Core m/m at 0.2% came in 0.1% below expectations amid slowing growth in food prices and shelter costs.

President Trump’s rhetoric toward Iran took an aggressive turn with fewer posts about a deal and more threats about wider military intervention, while Tehran did not appear to blink. Stocks retreated further while oil prices and Treasury yields moved up after the Iranians claimed the U. S. struck vital civilian water infrastructure. Gold prices remained under significant pressure falling back towards $4K, while Bitcoin managed to edge higher.
