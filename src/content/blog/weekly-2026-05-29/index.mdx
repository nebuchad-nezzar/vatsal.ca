May 29, 2026

Trading resumed following the long U. S. holiday weekend with money continuing to rotate into technology shares. Micron surged another ~20% to reach a $1T market cap for the first time, and shares of Snowflake and Dell rocketed higher after AI-infused earnings reports.

As May came to a close, the list of companies that saw their market caps nearly double in just the last month was nearly unprecedented. Stock sentiment also benefited from inflation readings that were significantly cooler than expected in Europe, and a not-as-hot-as-feared U. S.

PCE print. Investors also heard generally constructive comments from a long list of key U. S.

CEO’s at the Bernstein investor conference. Finally, oil prices and bond yields both moved lower on hopes that the U. S. and Iran will sign an MOU extending the tentative ceasefire for 60 days, potentially laying a path to reopening the Strait of Hormuz.

For the week, the S&P gained 1.4%, the DJIA was up 0.9%, and the Nasdaq rose 2.4%. European bourses joined the risk on sentiment with the German DAX rising 0.9% and the CAC 40 gaining 0.8%. Asia Pacific stock markets were once again bifurcated, with Japan advancing 4.7% and the Hang Seng falling 1.7%.

Your BSD Global Tech Hedge Fund gained 1.4% this past week. Tech, particularly AI-related tech, continued to drive the corporate news narrative. Dell, NetApp, and Marvell all reported impressive earnings and trumpeted their growing AI business as they raised their outlooks.

Meta got a lot of likes from its investors as it found a new revenue stream by launching subscription plans for enhanced versions of its Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp platforms. Among the struggling software names under threat from the AI revolution, Salesforce was able to regain some of its former luster with a strong Q1 report and increased guidance as operating margins improved and its own AI product line Agentforce saw ARR growing at over 200%. Outside of tech, embattled retailer Kohl’s saw some signs of a turnaround, notching its best same store sales result in years, still declining but at a slower rate.

Gap Inc. shares gapped lower on a tepid Q1 report with mixed guidance as online sales slipped and the once trendy Athleta brand now proving to be a drag on business. Airlines had a good week, executives made upbeat comments at the Bernstein conference, noting that demand remains strong despite rising airfares due to the oil shock. In the coming week, the main economic event arrives Friday with the May U.

S. nonfarm payrolls report. Economists expect the U. S. economy added 93K jobs during the month, while the unemployment rate is forecast to hold steady at 4.3%.

Investors will also monitor U. S. manufacturing and services activity data, JOLTS job openings, and the Federal Reserve’s Beige Book for fresh clues on economic momentum. Broadcom, CrowdStrike, Hewlett Packard Enterprise, Palo Alto Networks, and Docusign headline the earnings calendar, with Broadcom’s results likely to provide another important read on AI infrastructure spending.

The AI theme extends beyond earnings. Nvidia, Qualcomm, Intel, Arm Holdings, and other industry leaders will take center stage at Computex Taipei, while Microsoft hosts its annual Build developer conference. Both events are expected to feature major announcements around AI, datacenters, software, and robotics.

In the IPO market, Honeywell-backed quantum computing company Quantinuum is expected to debut at a valuation of roughly $12.7B, making it one of the largest technology offerings of the year. Meanwhile, FedEx will complete the spinoff of FedEx Freight, creating a new standalone S&P 500 company and marking one of the year’s most significant corporate restructurings.
